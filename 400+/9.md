# **WORD POWER MADE EASY: BLABBERING**



UPSC Vocabulary Builder

Etymology + Semantic Evolution



## **1. BLABBERING**

Pronunciation: /ˈblæb.ər.ɪŋ/ | Part of speech: Noun / Verb (present participle)

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The word *blabber* is an old expressive word imitating the sound of someone speaking excessively, foolishly or indistinctly.

- **Blab** = to talk excessively, indiscreetly or without restraint.
- **-er** = a verbal ending associated with the action or its performance.
- **-ing** = indicates an ongoing action; it can also form a noun (gerund).

*Note:* The word does not have a clearly identifiable combination of classical Latin or Greek roots. Its origin is primarily Germanic and sound-symbolic.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To keep producing words without exercising restraint, discretion or meaningful control over speech.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from the expressive imitation of continuous or indistinct speech.

Its semantic development can be understood as:

**Continuous speech → Excessive talking → Unrestrained talking → Foolish or indiscreet talking**

The emphasis gradually shifted from merely talking a lot to talking without sufficient thought, relevance or discretion.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Blabbering** refers to talking continuously, often foolishly, pointlessly or indiscreetly, without considering whether what one says is useful, appropriate or necessary.

It generally carries a negative or dismissive connotation.

**Important distinction:**

- Talking: Neutral act of speaking.
- Chattering: Light, informal and continuous conversation.
- Babbling: Incoherent, childish or unintelligible speech.
- Blabbering: Excessive, foolish or indiscreet speech.
- Rambling: Speaking at length without maintaining a logical direction.
- Prating: Talking foolishly or at tedious length.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1. **Blabbering nonsense** – Speaking foolishly or without meaningful content.
2. **Blabbering indiscriminately** – Speaking without exercising discretion.
3. **Stop blabbering** – A dismissive expression used to tell someone to stop talking unnecessarily.

**UPSC Application:**

In an essay on political discourse:

"Democratic deliberation requires reasoned argumentation rather than political blabbering, misinformation and unsubstantiated allegations."

# **2. ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY AND RELATED VOCABULARY**

An important etymological clarification before proceeding:

Not every word that resembles *blabber* belongs to the same historical root. We should distinguish genuine etymological relatives from semantic associates.

The historical origin of *blabber* is uncertain, and it is generally associated with expressive Germanic speech formations. Therefore, the following family is divided into:

1. Words historically related to the *blab/blabber* formation.
2. Closely associated vocabulary useful for UPSC.

This distinction prevents false etymological learning.

## **PART I — WORDS RELATED TO BLAB / BLABBER**

### **1. BLAB**

Part of speech: Verb / Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

\- **Blab** = an expressive speech formation referring to indiscreet or excessive talking.

**Literal Meaning**

To let words escape without controlling one's speech.

**Semantic Evolution**

Talking continuously → Revealing information carelessly → Disclosing confidential information.

**Modern Meaning**

To reveal information that should have remained secret, or to talk excessively and indiscreetly.

**Common Usage**

1. Blab a secret.
2. Blab confidential information.
3. Don't blab about the matter.

**UPSC Application:**

"Public officials must not blab confidential information, as doing so may undermine institutional integrity and national security."

### **2. BLABBER**

Part of speech: Verb / Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

- **Blab** = indiscreet or excessive speech.
- **-er** = verbal extension forming the word *blabber*.

**Literal Meaning**

To engage in uncontrolled or excessive speech.

**Semantic Evolution**

Unrestrained talking → Foolish talking → Indiscreet disclosure.

**Modern Meaning**

To talk excessively, foolishly or without discretion.

As a noun, a *blabber* is someone who talks excessively or reveals secrets.

**Common Usage**

1. Blabber incessantly.
2. Blabber about confidential matters.
3. A habitual blabber.

**UPSC Application:**

"Responsible public communication requires discretion rather than habitual blabbering."

### **3. BLABBERMOUTH**

Part of speech: Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

- **Blabber** = someone who talks excessively or indiscreetly.
- **Mouth** = the organ of speech.

**Literal Meaning**

A mouth that constantly produces uncontrolled speech.

**Semantic Evolution**

Excessive speech → Indiscreet speech → A person unable to keep information confidential.

**Modern Meaning**

An informal, generally derogatory term for someone who talks too much or cannot keep secrets.

**Common Usage**

1. A notorious blabbermouth.
2. Being a blabbermouth.
3. A political blabbermouth.

**UPSC Application:**

Avoid this informal term in formal answers. Prefer *indiscreet speaker*, *loquacious individual* or *breach of confidentiality*, depending on context.

These words are not necessarily derived from the root *blab*. They belong to the same conceptual vocabulary cluster and are particularly useful for UPSC Essay, Ethics, Governance and Political Science.

## **4. BABBLE**

Part of speech: Verb / Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

\- **Babble** = an expressive, repetitive formation imitating indistinct or unintelligible speech.

**Literal Meaning**

To produce sounds or words continuously without clear articulation.

**Semantic Evolution**

Indistinct sounds → Childlike speech → Incoherent or meaningless talking.

**Modern Meaning**

To speak rapidly and incoherently, often without making much sense.

**Common Usage**

1. Babble incoherently.
2. Political babble.
3. Babble of voices.

**UPSC Application:**

"Public debate must be based on evidence and rational deliberation rather than ideological babble."

## **5. GABBLE**

Part of speech: Verb / Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

\- **Gabble** = an expressive word associated with rapid, confused speech.

**Literal Meaning**

To produce words rapidly, often without clear articulation.

**Semantic Evolution**

Rapid speech → Confused speech → Difficult-to-understand talking.

**Modern Meaning**

To speak quickly and indistinctly, making comprehension difficult.

**Common Usage**

1. Gabble rapidly.
2. Gabble incoherently.
3. A gabble of voices.

**UPSC Application:**

"Policy communication should be clear and accessible rather than couched in technical language that makes public discourse resemble an unintelligible gabble."

## **6. PRATTLE**

Part of speech: Verb / Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

\- **Prattle** = a word historically associated with childish or foolish talking.

**Literal Meaning**

To keep talking in a trivial or childish manner.

**Semantic Evolution**

Childlike talking → Trivial conversation → Foolish or inconsequential speech.

**Modern Meaning**

To talk continuously about unimportant matters, often in a childish or silly way.

**Common Usage**

1. Prattle incessantly.
2. Childish prattle.
3. Prattle about trivial matters.

**UPSC Application:**

"Parliamentary proceedings should prioritise substantive deliberation over partisan prattle."

## **7. PRATE**

Part of speech: Verb

**Meaning of the Components**

\- **Prate** = to talk foolishly or excessively; historically related to Middle English *praten*.

**Literal Meaning**

To produce a stream of unnecessary words.

**Semantic Evolution**

Talking → Talking at length → Foolish or tedious talking.

**Modern Meaning**

To talk foolishly or at unnecessary length.

**Common Usage**

1. Prate about morality.
2. Prate endlessly.
3. Prate of idealism.

**UPSC Application:**

"Political leadership must demonstrate ethical conduct rather than merely prate about constitutional morality."

## **8. PRATTLE vs PRATE**

| Word    | Central emphasis                     |
| ------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Prattle | Childish, trivial or silly talking   |

## **9. GAB**

Part of speech: Noun / Verb

**Meaning of the Components**

\- **Gab** = informal, continuous talking.

**Literal Meaning**

To engage in casual conversation.

**Semantic Evolution**

Casual conversation → Excessive talking → Socially trivial conversation.

**Modern Meaning**

Informal, often prolonged conversation.

**Common Usage**

1. Gift of the gab.
2. Gab incessantly.
3. Chewing the fat (a related informal expression).

**UPSC Application:**

"The gift of the gab may help political mobilisation, but effective governance requires institutional competence and evidence-based decision-making."

**Important expression: Gift of the gab**

The natural ability to speak fluently and persuasively.

It can be used positively or negatively depending on context.

High-value vocabulary

## **15. RHETORIC**

Part of speech: Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

- Greek *rhētōr* = public speaker or orator.
- Related to Greek *rhētorikē* = the art of public speaking.

**Literal Meaning**

The art or practice of public speaking.

**Semantic Evolution**

Art of persuasion → Persuasive language → Sometimes language designed to impress rather than communicate substantive meaning.

**Modern Meaning**

The art of effective or persuasive speaking and writing; also, language that may sound impressive but lacks practical substance.

**Common Usage**

1. Political rhetoric.
2. Empty rhetoric.
3. Rhetoric versus reality.

**UPSC Application:**

"Bridging the gap between political rhetoric and administrative reality is essential for democratic legitimacy."

*Important:* Rhetoric is not inherently negative. Persuasive communication is indispensable to democratic politics.

# **PART IV — VOCABULARY OF IRRELEVANT OR UNNECESSARY SPEECH**

## **18. RAMBLE**

Part of speech: Verb / Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

\- From Middle English *rambelen*, meaning to wander or roam.

**Literal Meaning**

To wander without a fixed direction.

**Semantic Evolution**

Physical wandering → Wandering from the main subject → Disorganised speech.

**Modern Meaning**

To speak or write at length without maintaining a clear or logical sequence.

**Common Usage**

1. Ramble aimlessly.
2. Rambling speech.
3. Ramble off the point.

**UPSC Application:**

"An effective Mains answer should maintain logical progression rather than ramble without addressing the directive."

## **19. DIGRESS**

Part of speech: Verb

**Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *dis-* = away or apart.
- Latin *gradi* = to step or walk.

**Literal Meaning**

To step away from the main path.

**Semantic Evolution**

Moving away from a path → Moving away from the main subject → Departing from the central argument.

**Modern Meaning**

To temporarily depart from the main topic of discussion or writing.

**Common Usage**

1. Digress from the topic.
2. Needless digression.
3. Avoid digressing.

**UPSC Application:**

"While answering analytical questions, candidates should avoid unnecessary digression and maintain thematic coherence."

## **20. CIRCUMLOCUTION**

Part of speech: Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *circum* = around.
- Latin *locutio* = speech or expression, from *loqui* = to speak.

**Literal Meaning**

Speaking around something rather than addressing it directly.

**Semantic Evolution**

Indirect expression → Avoiding direct statements → Excessively roundabout language.

**Modern Meaning**

Using more words than necessary to express something, especially by avoiding a direct statement.

**Common Usage**

1. Bureaucratic circumlocution.
2. Resort to circumlocution.
3. Avoid unnecessary circumlocution.

**UPSC Application:**

"Citizen-centric administration requires clarity and transparency rather than bureaucratic circumlocution."

## **21. PERIPHRASIS**

Part of speech: Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

- Greek *peri* = around.
- Greek *phrasis* = expression or speech.

**Literal Meaning**

An expression that goes around the subject.

**Semantic Evolution**

Indirect expression → Using multiple words instead of a direct expression → Roundabout description.

**Modern Meaning**

The use of a longer or indirect expression in place of a simpler, more direct one.

**Common Usage**

1. Verbal periphrasis.
2. Literary periphrasis.
3. Unnecessary periphrasis.

**UPSC Application:**

"Policy documents should avoid unnecessary periphrasis to ensure accessibility to ordinary citizens."

# **OBNOXIOUS**

Adjective

C1 – Advanced

Useful for UPSC

**Pronunciation:** /əbˈnɒk.ʃəs/ (uhb-NOK-shuhs)

**Core idea:** Something or someone so unpleasant, offensive or irritating that it provokes strong dislike.

## **1. Meaning of the Components**

The word *obnoxious* comes from Latin *obnoxiosus*, derived from *obnoxius*.

Break it into two historical components:

| Component      | Origin       | Meaning                              |
| -------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Ob-**        | Latin prefix | Toward, against, in the direction of |
| **Nox / Noci** | Latin *noxa* | Harm, injury, damage                 |

**Important root:** *Noxa* = harm or injury.

The underlying idea is being exposed to, liable to, or subject to harm.

## **2. Literal Meaning**

The original sense of *obnoxius* was:

**Liable to harm, exposed to injury, or subject to another's power.**

Think of a person who is vulnerable to someone else's authority or punishment.

Over time, the word's meaning shifted from being *liable to harm* to being *harmful, offensive or disagreeable*.

## **3. Semantic Evolution**

1. **Latin: Obnoxius**

   Liable to harm; exposed or subject to another's power.
2. **Later usage**

   Harmful, injurious or morally objectionable.
3. **Modern English: Obnoxious**

   Extremely unpleasant, offensive, irritating or objectionable.

**Memory hook:** Something obnoxious is so disagreeable that it becomes almost harmful to your peace of mind!

## **4. Modern Meaning**

**Obnoxious (adjective):**

Extremely unpleasant or offensive, especially in a way that provokes irritation, resentment or disgust.

**Synonyms:** Offensive, repulsive, disagreeable, irritating, odious, insufferable.

**Antonyms:** Pleasant, agreeable, courteous, congenial, amiable.

**Examples:**

1. The minister's obnoxious remarks about marginalised communities attracted widespread condemnation.
2. Obnoxious behaviour by public officials undermines institutional credibility.
3. The excessive use of loudspeakers creates an obnoxious environment in residential areas.

## **5. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation             | Meaning                                   |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Obnoxious behaviour** | Extremely unpleasant or offensive conduct |
| **Obnoxious remarks**   | Offensive or insulting statements         |
| **Obnoxious odour**     | An extremely unpleasant smell             |

**UPSC application:**

- GS-I: Social intolerance, discriminatory behaviour and communal disharmony.
- GS-II: Ethical conduct of public officials, accountability and institutional credibility.
- GS-IV: Public service values, courtesy, empathy and emotional intelligence.

Root-based vocabulary expansion

The Latin root *noxa* (harm, injury) and its related forms *nocere* (to harm) and *noxius* (harmful) have contributed several useful English words.

## **6.1 NOXIOUS**

Adjective

**Meaning of the Components**

- *Noxius*: Latin for harmful or injurious.
- Related to *nocere*: to harm.

**Literal Meaning**

Causing harm or injury.

**Semantic Evolution**

Originally associated with something injurious or damaging; it came to describe substances, conditions or influences that are harmful to health or well-being.

**Modern Meaning**

Harmful, poisonous or injurious, particularly to living organisms or the environment.

**Common Usage**

1. Noxious emissions
2. Noxious chemicals
3. Noxious gases

**UPSC Example:**

Industrial units releasing noxious gases pose a serious threat to environmental sustainability and public health.

**Memory hook:** Noxious = Causes harm.

## **6.2 INNOCUOUS**

Adjective

**Meaning of the Components**

- *In-* = Not (negative prefix).
- *Nocuus* = Harmful, injurious (related to *nocere*).

**Literal Meaning**

Not harmful.

**Semantic Evolution**

The Latin formation conveyed the idea of being harmless or causing no injury. English retained this sense and extended it to things that are unlikely to offend or attract attention.

**Modern Meaning**

Harmless, inoffensive or unlikely to cause damage.

**Common Usage**

1. Innocuous remarks
2. Innocuous substance
3. Apparently innocuous behaviour

**UPSC Example:**

An apparently innocuous administrative decision may have far-reaching consequences for vulnerable communities.

**Memory hook:** In + nocuous = Not harmful.

**Contrast:**

- Obnoxious = Extremely unpleasant or offensive.
- Innocuous = Harmless or inoffensive.

## **6.3 INNOCENT**

Adjective / Noun

**Meaning of the Components**

- *In-* = Not.
- *Nocent* = Harming or guilty of wrongdoing, from Latin *nocere*.

**Literal Meaning**

Not causing harm; not guilty of wrongdoing.

**Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *innocens* referred to someone who did no harm or was free from wrongdoing. English developed its legal, moral and everyday senses.

**Modern Meaning**

1. Not guilty of a crime or offence.
2. Free from moral wrongdoing.
3. Without harmful intentions or knowledge.

**Common Usage**

1. Presumption of innocence
2. Innocent until proven guilty
3. Innocent civilians

**UPSC Example:**

# **1. DUCTILITY**

**Pronunciation:** /dʌkˈtɪləti/ (duk-TIL-uh-tee)

**Part of Speech:** Noun

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

The word *ductility* comes from the Latin root:

| Component | Origin         | Meaning                      |
| --------- | -------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Duct-     | Latin *ducere* | To lead, draw, or pull       |
| -ile      | Latin *-ilis*  | Capable of being             |
| -ity      | Latin *-itas*  | State, quality, or condition |

**Root mnemonic:** *DUC = TO LEAD / DRAW.*

Think of drawing a metal wire by pulling it. The metal changes shape without breaking.

### **2. Literal Meaning**

The quality or condition of being capable of being drawn out.

In its original physical sense, ductility describes the ability of a material to undergo deformation under tensile stress without fracturing.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The semantic development can be understood through three stages:

1. **Physical action:** To lead, draw, or pull something.
2. **Material property:** The ability of a substance, particularly metal, to be drawn into wires without breaking.
3. **Extended conceptual meaning:** The capacity to be shaped, modified, or influenced without losing essential integrity.

The metaphorical extension is particularly useful in social sciences: a system or institution may be described as ductile when it can accommodate changes without collapsing.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

**Ductility** refers to:

- **In Material Science:** The ability of a material to undergo considerable plastic deformation under tensile stress before fracture.
- **In Engineering:** The capacity of a structural material to deform and absorb energy before failure.
- **In Metaphorical Usage:** The ability to adapt, accommodate, or be shaped without losing essential coherence.

**Important distinction:**

- Ductility = Ability to undergo deformation under tensile stress.
- Malleability = Ability to be shaped under compressive stress, such as hammering into sheets.

### **5. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant Collocations)**

1. **Ductility of metals** — The capacity of metals to be drawn into wires.
2. **Ductility of structural materials** — The ability of construction materials to deform before failure, important in earthquake-resistant structures.
3. **Institutional ductility** — The ability of institutions to accommodate social, political, and economic changes without losing their essential character.

**UPSC application:**

"Constitutional flexibility and institutional ductility enable democratic institutions to accommodate evolving social aspirations while maintaining constitutional stability."

## **1. NITPICKING — Detailed Word Analysis**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈnɪtˌpɪkɪŋ/ (NIT-pik-ing)

**Part of Speech:** Noun (gerund); also used as an adjective.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The word consists of two components:

1. **Nit:** The egg of a louse (a tiny parasitic insect), particularly one attached to human hair.
2. **Picking:** The act of removing something painstakingly, usually with one's fingers.

Historically, removing lice eggs from hair required careful attention to extremely small objects.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Nitpicking = The act of picking out tiny lice eggs one by one.**

The image is of someone painstakingly searching for and removing the smallest possible objects.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The semantic development can be understood through three stages:

1. **Literal sense:** Removing tiny lice eggs from hair.
2. **Figurative extension:** Paying excessive attention to minute details.
3. **Modern evaluative sense:** Finding fault with trivial, insignificant or inconsequential details, often while ignoring the larger picture.

The original physical act of painstakingly examining tiny objects acquired a metaphorical meaning of excessive attention to insignificant details.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Nitpicking** refers to the practice of finding fault with minor, insignificant or inconsequential details, often in an excessively critical or pedantic manner.

It generally carries a negative connotation.

**Conceptual distinction:**

- Attention to detail = Careful examination to improve accuracy.
- Scrutiny = Critical and systematic examination.
- Nitpicking = Excessive criticism of trivial details.

Thus, not every detailed examination is nitpicking. The defining feature is disproportionate attention to insignificant faults.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Expression                   | Meaning                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nitpicking criticism         | Criticism focused on trivial flaws.                            |
| Engage in nitpicking         | To waste disproportionate effort finding minor faults.         |
| Avoid unnecessary nitpicking | Focus on substantive issues rather than trivial imperfections. |

**UPSC-style examples:**

1. Parliamentary debates should focus on substantive policy disagreements rather than descend into unnecessary nitpicking.
2. Excessive nitpicking in administrative procedures can undermine the efficiency of public service delivery.
3. Constructive scrutiny strengthens governance, whereas nitpicking can obstruct institutional decision-making.

**Important etymological clarification:** Unlike many Latin- or Greek-derived vocabulary families, *nitpicking* does not have a large, productive family of words derived from the root *nit*. Its closest direct relatives are *nitpicker*, *nitpick*, and *nitpicky*.

I will distinguish direct derivatives from semantic relatives so that you do not accidentally memorise unrelated words as sharing the same historical root.

## **A. Words Related to Excessive Attention to Details**

**C. Semantic Evolution**

It developed into the idea of being excessively difficult to please because of very high standards.
