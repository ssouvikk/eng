# **1. TURMOIL**

*Pronunciation: /ˈtɜːrmɔɪl/ (TUR-moyl)*

**Central idea:** A state of confusion, disorder, agitation or violent disturbance in which normal stability and order have broken down.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

Unlike many English words, *turmoil* cannot be reliably divided into two meaningful modern components. Its historical origin is uncertain.

For memory purposes, associate it with three ideas:

- **Disorder:** Breakdown of an established arrangement.
- **Agitation:** A state of intense mental or social disturbance.
- **Instability:** Absence of peace, predictability or normal functioning.

**Memory association:** Turmoil = Turbulence + Disorder + Instability.

*Note:* This is a mnemonic association, not the actual etymological derivation.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The word does not have a dependable word-for-word literal translation because its origin is obscure.

Its established sense conveys a condition of disturbance, commotion and confusion.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The historical development of the word is not sufficiently certain to construct a precise sequence of component meanings.

Its established English usage developed around the idea of disturbance and commotion, eventually acquiring broader applications to:

1. Physical disorder and commotion.
2. Political and social instability.
3. Mental and emotional disturbance.
4. Economic and institutional disruption.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Turmoil (noun):** A state of great confusion, disorder, agitation or upheaval, especially when accompanied by instability and uncertainty.

It can be used across four dimensions:

| Dimension     | Meaning                                              | Example           |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Political     | Instability in governance or political authority     | Political turmoil |
| Social        | Disturbance in social relationships and institutions | Social turmoil    |
| Economic      | Disruption in markets and economic systems           | Economic turmoil  |
| Psychological | Emotional confusion and inner disturbance            | Inner turmoil     |

**Important distinction:**

- **Turmoil:** A condition of prolonged or intense disorder.
- **Chaos:** A state in which order and organisation are absent.
- **Upheaval:** A major and often sudden disruption of an existing system.
- **Unrest:** Dissatisfaction or agitation, particularly among people.
- **Turbulence:** Violent disturbance or instability, literally or metaphorically.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Political turmoil** — Instability arising from political conflicts, leadership crises or institutional breakdown.

*Example:* Political turmoil in a neighbouring country can generate refugee flows and regional security concerns.

2\. **Economic turmoil** — Severe disruption in economic activity, markets, employment or financial stability.

*Example:* Global economic turmoil can adversely affect developing economies through capital flight and declining exports.

3\. **Social turmoil** — Widespread social disturbance arising from inequality, discrimination, conflict or competing interests.

*Example:* Persistent social turmoil can undermine social cohesion and weaken democratic institutions.

## **2. Etymological Word Family of TURMOIL**

Important etymological distinction

A genuinely important point for advanced vocabulary building:

**Turmoil does not have an extensive, securely established etymological family.**

According to the *Online Etymology Dictionary*, it first appeared as a verb in the early sixteenth century, and its origin remains uncertain. One hypothesis connects it with Old French *tremouille* (a mill hopper), whose constant back-and-forth movement might suggest agitation. Another possibility is the influence of the English verb *moil* (to toil or labour). Neither explanation is established as definitive.&#x20;

# **PART I — THE TURB- WORD FAMILY**

## **3. TURBULENT**

*Part of speech: Adjective*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin **turba** = crowd, commotion, disorder.
- Latin **-ulentus** = full of, characterised by or abounding in.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Full of commotion or disorder.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally used to describe something restless, disturbed or disorderly. It subsequently acquired physical, political and metaphorical meanings.

The word came to describe:

- Disturbed liquids.
- Unruly or disorderly people.
- Violent weather and air movement.
- Unstable political and social conditions.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Turbulent:** Characterised by instability, disorder, agitation or unpredictable change.

It can describe both physical disturbances and metaphorical instability.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Turbulent political transition** — A change in political authority accompanied by instability and conflict.

2\. **Turbulent geopolitical environment** — An international environment characterised by power struggles, conflict and uncertainty.

3\. **Turbulent socio-economic conditions** — Conditions involving significant economic and social instability.

*UPSC application:* India's neighbourhood has experienced turbulent political transitions, with implications for regional stability and national security.

## **4. TURBULENCE**

*Part of speech: Noun*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Turbul-** = disturbed, agitated, disorderly (from Latin *turbulentus*).
- **-ence** = state, condition or quality.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The state or condition of being disturbed or disorderly.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Initially associated with cloudiness and disturbance in liquids, the word gradually expanded to describe instability in physical systems, societies, economies and political institutions.

In modern physics and meteorology, it also has a specific technical meaning relating to irregular fluid motion.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Turbulence:** A condition of instability, irregular movement, agitation or unpredictable disruption.

It has three important applications:

- **Meteorological:** Irregular atmospheric movement.
- **Political:** Instability in governance and political systems.
- **Economic:** Uncertainty and volatility in markets and financial systems.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Political turbulence** — Instability resulting from political competition, institutional crises or leadership conflicts.

2\. **Economic turbulence** — Disruptions caused by inflation, financial crises, market volatility or external shocks.

3\. **Global turbulence** — Worldwide instability resulting from geopolitical conflicts, economic disruptions or systemic crises.

*UPSC application:* Economic turbulence in advanced economies can transmit financial shocks to emerging markets through capital flows and exchange-rate volatility.

## **5. TURBID**

*Part of speech: Adjective*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin **turbidus** = muddy, confused, disturbed.
- Derived from **turba** = commotion, disorder.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Muddy, clouded or disturbed.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally used for liquids containing suspended matter that made them unclear or cloudy.

The meaning subsequently expanded metaphorically to describe thoughts, situations and arguments that lack clarity.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Turbid:** Not clear or transparent; cloudy, muddy or confused.

It has both a scientific and figurative application.

- Scientific: Water containing suspended particles.
- Figurative: A situation, argument or state of mind lacking clarity.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Turbid water** — Water containing suspended particles, reducing its transparency.

2\. **Turbid river water** — Water carrying sediments due to erosion, runoff or other disturbances.

3\. **Turbid political atmosphere** — A politically confusing or unclear environment. (Less common than *murky political atmosphere*.)

*UPSC application:* Increased turbidity in rivers can affect aquatic ecosystems, reduce light penetration and adversely affect freshwater biodiversity.

**Retention tip:** Turbid = unclear water; turbulent = disturbed movement.

## **6. TURBIDITY**

*Part of speech: Noun*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Turbid** = muddy, cloudy or unclear.
- **-ity** = state or quality.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The state or quality of being cloudy or muddy.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The term evolved from the description of unclear liquids into a technical environmental and water-quality parameter.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Turbidity:** The degree to which water loses its transparency because of suspended particles such as clay, silt, organic matter and microorganisms.

It is an important indicator in water-quality assessment, although it does not independently establish whether water is safe to drink.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Water turbidity** — The cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles.

2\. **Turbidity levels** — The measured degree of water cloudiness.

3\. **Turbidity monitoring** — Monitoring water quality through measurement of suspended-particle-related light scattering.

*UPSC application:* Turbidity monitoring is important in drinking-water treatment, river pollution assessment and environmental governance.

# **PART II — THE DISTURBANCE FAMILY**

## **7. DISTURB**

*Part of speech: Verb*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin **dis-** = apart, away or in different directions.
- Latin **turbare** = to throw into confusion or disorder.
- **Turbare** derives from **turba** = crowd, commotion or disorder.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To throw something into disorder or confusion.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally conveyed the idea of throwing something into confusion or disorganising an existing arrangement.

It subsequently developed broader meanings:

- Interrupting an activity.
- Disrupting peace or tranquillity.
- Interfering with normal functioning.
- Causing mental or emotional agitation.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Disturb:** To interrupt, disrupt, disorganise or interfere with the normal condition, functioning or tranquillity of something or someone.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Disturb public order** — Disrupt the maintenance of peace and order in society.

2\. **Disturb ecological balance** — Interfere with the natural equilibrium of an ecosystem.

3\. **Disturb communal harmony** — Disrupt peaceful relations between different religious or social communities.

*UPSC application:* Hate speech and misinformation can disturb communal harmony and undermine constitutional fraternity.

## **8. DISTURBANCE**

*Part of speech: Noun*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Dis-** = apart, away or reversal.
- **Turb** = disorder or confusion.
- **-ance** = state, condition or result.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The act or condition of throwing something into disorder.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed from the verb *disturb*, gradually acquiring meanings relating to interruption, disorder, disruption and abnormal functioning.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Disturbance:** An interruption of normal functioning, tranquillity, order or equilibrium.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Public disturbance** — An incident that disrupts public peace or normal social functioning.

2\. **Ecological disturbance** — A natural or human-induced event that alters an ecosystem.

3\. **Law-and-order disturbance** — An event disrupting public peace and security.

*UPSC application:* Unregulated mining can cause ecological disturbances by altering landscapes, degrading habitats and disrupting hydrological systems.

## **9. PERTURB**

*Part of speech: Verb*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin **per-** = thoroughly, completely or intensely.
- Latin **turbare** = to disturb or throw into confusion.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To disturb thoroughly or throw into considerable confusion.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Initially conveyed the idea of throwing something into disorder. It later developed a more specific psychological meaning: causing anxiety, agitation or mental disturbance.

In scientific and mathematical contexts, it also came to describe a deviation from an expected or normal condition.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Perturb:** To disturb, unsettle, disconcert or cause anxiety.

It is also used in science to describe a disturbance affecting an otherwise stable system.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Perturb the equilibrium** — Disturb the existing balance of a system.

2\. **Perturb the natural ecosystem** — Disrupt the normal functioning of an ecosystem.

3\. **Deeply perturbed by developments** — Seriously disturbed or concerned by unfolding events.

*UPSC application:* Unregulated extraction of groundwater can perturb the hydrological equilibrium of an aquifer.

## **10. PERTURBATION**

*Part of speech: Noun*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Per-** = thoroughly or intensely.
- **Turb** = disorder or disturbance.
- **-ation** = process, action or resulting condition.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The process or condition of being thoroughly disturbed.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed from the Latin-derived verb *perturbare*, acquiring both psychological and technical meanings.

In physics and astronomy, it refers to an influence that causes a system to deviate from its normal or expected state.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Perturbation:** A disturbance, deviation or external influence that alters the normal state or equilibrium of a system.

Applications include:

- Physics and astronomy.
- Ecology and environmental science.
- Economics and mathematical modelling.
- Psychology.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Ecological perturbation** — A disturbance that alters ecosystem structure or functioning.

2\. **External perturbation** — An outside influence that disrupts the normal state of a system.

3\. **Economic perturbation** — A disturbance that causes deviations in economic activity or equilibrium.

*UPSC application:* Climate change acts as a major external perturbation, affecting ecosystems, agricultural productivity and human settlements.

# **PART III — THE DIRECT DERIVATIVES OF TURMOIL**

These are genuine derivatives or historical forms of *turmoil*, rather than words merely associated with its meaning.

## **11. TURMOILOUS**

*Part of speech: Adjective (archaic/obsolete)*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Turmoil** = disturbance, confusion or commotion.
- **-ous** = characterised by, full of or having the quality of.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Characterised by turmoil or disturbance.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The adjective was formed from *turmoil* to describe situations or conditions marked by disorder and agitation.

It appeared in historical English but did not survive as a commonly used modern adjective.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Turmoilous:** Full of turmoil, confusion or disturbance. An archaic and very rare word.

### **E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

This word is not recommended for modern UPSC answer writing. Prefer:

1. Turbulent political environment.
2. Turmoil-ridden society.
3. Socially volatile conditions.

## **12. TURMOIL (VERB)**

*Part of speech: Verb (archaic/obsolete)*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

\- **Turmoil** = disorder, confusion or agitation.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To throw into disorder, agitate or harass.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The verb was actually recorded before the noun became established. Historically, it meant to harass, disquiet, confuse or disturb someone.

Its verbal usage has since become obsolete.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

The historical verb meant to cause disturbance or agitation.

Today, use:

- Disturb.
- Disrupt.
- Agitate.
- Throw into confusion.

### **E. Common Usage — Modern Equivalents**

1. Disrupt institutional functioning.
2. Agitate public opinion.
3. Destabilise the political system.

- **-ly** = in a particular manner.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed from *turbulent*, transferring its meaning of instability and disturbance to the manner in which something occurs.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

In a manner characterised by disorder, instability or violent disturbance.

### **E. Common Usage**

2. Turbulently changing economic environment.

*Part of speech: Noun*

- **Turbulent** = disturbed or disorderly.
- **-cy** = state or condition.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The condition of being turbulent.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

An alternative noun formation associated with the quality of being turbulent.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

The condition of instability or disturbance. However, **turbulence** is overwhelmingly the preferred modern form.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Atmospheric turbulence.
2. Political turbulence.
3. Economic turbulence.

*Part of speech: Noun*

It does not have a reliably separable modern prefix-root structure.

A commotion or uproar.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Initially associated with public commotion, disorder and disturbance, especially those involving crowds.

It gradually acquired broader meanings covering political, social and emotional disturbance.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Tumult:** A loud, confused disturbance, especially involving a large group of people; also a state of emotional agitation.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Political tumult.
2. Public tumult.
3. Tumult of emotions.

*UPSC application:* Democratic societies must accommodate dissent without allowing political tumult to undermine institutional stability.

Full of commotion or uproar.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally described noisy and disorderly gatherings. Its usage expanded to include violent, unstable or emotionally intense situations.

Characterised by confusion, disorder, agitation or violent disturbance.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Tumultuous political transition.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Derived from *tumultuous*, describing the manner in which a situation unfolds.

### **E. Common Usage**

2. Tumultuously contested elections.

**Central idea:** To swing back and forth — physically, intellectually, politically, economically or socially.

The word *oscillation* is particularly useful in UPSC answer writing because it can describe fluctuations in economic indicators, shifts in political preferences, instability in public policy, changing social attitudes and variations in scientific phenomena.

# **PART I — OSCILLATION: Understanding the Original Word**

## **1. Meaning of the Components**

**Oscillation** comes from the Latin noun *oscillum*, meaning a little face or mask, particularly a small mask hung up as an ornament.

It is associated with the Latin verb *oscillare*, meaning **to swing or sway**.

The word's historical development is connected with the Roman practice of hanging small masks, called *oscilla*, from trees. These objects moved back and forth in the wind.

| Component         | Meaning                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Latin *oscillum*  | Little face or mask                        |
| Latin *oscillare* | To swing or sway                           |
| Suffix *-ation*   | The process, action or result of something |

**Important:** The word does not literally divide into two independently meaningful English roots. Its development is Latin-based.

## **2. Literal Meaning**

The act or process of swinging repeatedly from one position to another.

Imagine a pendulum moving from left to right and then right to left.

## **3. Semantic Evolution**

The meaning developed through the following stages:

1. **Physical movement:** The swinging of an object suspended from a fixed point.
2. **Scientific application:** Repeated periodic movement around an equilibrium position.
3. **Generalised movement:** Repeated fluctuations between two positions, conditions or states.
4. **Abstract application:** Instability, variation or shifts between opposing opinions, policies, attitudes or conditions.

The essential idea remained unchanged: **repeated movement between different states or positions.**

## **4. Modern Meaning**

**Oscillation (noun):**

1. The repeated movement of an object between two positions.
2. A regular variation in the value, intensity or condition of something.
3. Repeated fluctuation between different opinions, attitudes, policies or states.

**Conceptual distinction:**

Oscillation does not necessarily mean random instability. It can be regular, periodic and predictable, as in physics, or irregular and unpredictable, as in political and economic behaviour.

## **5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation                      | Meaning / Context                                                            |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Economic oscillations**        | Repeated fluctuations in economic activity or indicators.                    |
| **Oscillation between extremes** | Repeated movement between two opposing positions or viewpoints.              |
| **Periodic oscillations**        | Regular, recurring fluctuations over fixed or approximately fixed intervals. |

**UPSC-ready sentence:**

"Frequent oscillations in government policy can undermine investor confidence and weaken the credibility of long-term economic reforms."

# **PART II — The OSCILL- Word Family**

The principal English derivatives of *oscillare* are built around the stem **oscill-**.

These words retain the central idea of swinging, fluctuating or repeatedly changing.

## **1. Oscillate (Verb)**

High utility

**Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *oscillare* = to swing or sway.
- English verbal ending *-ate* = to perform an action.

**Literal Meaning**

To swing repeatedly from one position to another.

**Semantic Evolution**

Physical swinging → periodic movement → repeated fluctuation → changing between opinions, conditions or positions.

**Modern Meaning**

To move repeatedly between two points, conditions, opinions or states.

**Common Usage**

1. Oscillate between two extremes.
2. Oscillate between growth and stagnation.

## **1. CONVEYOR — Understanding the Word**

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈveɪ.ər/ (kuhn-VAY-er)

**Part of speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The word *conveyor* comes from the verb *convey*, which traces back to Old French *conveier* and ultimately Latin roots.

| Component                                          | Meaning                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Latin *con-*                                       | Together, with                            |
| Latin *via*                                        | Way, road, route                          |
| Latin *conviare* (reconstructed/related formation) | To accompany or travel together           |
| English *convey*                                   | To carry, transport or communicate        |
| English *-or*                                      | A person or thing that performs an action |

**Important:** The historical development of *convey* is through Old French *conveier*, from Latin *conviare*, rather than a simple modern combination of *con-* + *via* + *-or*.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Conveyor = One that conveys or carries something from one place to another.**

Think of a conveyor belt in a factory: it continuously carries materials, products or packages along a predetermined route.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed through three broad stages:

1. **Physical transportation:** Carrying people, goods or materials from one location to another.
2. **Mechanical transportation:** A machine or system designed to move materials continuously.
3. **Figurative transmission:** Something or someone that transmits information, ideas, values or influence.

The underlying idea remains unchanged:

**Movement from one point to another.**

Only the nature of what is being moved changes.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Conveyor (noun):**

1. A mechanical device or system used for transporting materials from one place to another.
2. A person or agency that communicates or transmits something.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation                 | Meaning                                                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Conveyor belt system        | A mechanical arrangement for continuous transportation of goods |
| Conveyor of information     | A medium or channel through which information is transmitted    |
| Conveyor of cultural values | An institution or agency transmitting cultural norms and values |

**UPSC Application:**

- Education is an important conveyor of constitutional values and democratic citizenship.
- Mass media acts as a conveyor of information, shaping public opinion.
- Industrial conveyor systems improve productivity and supply-chain efficiency.

## **6. Final Retention Strategy**

Use this four-step mental association:

2. **VIA** → A road or path; hence deviation means departure from a path.

# **2. THE ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY OF STELL-**

The Latin root *stella* (star) has generated a fascinating family of English words.

However, an important etymological distinction must be maintained:

- **Stell- family:** Words directly derived from Latin *stella*.
- **Astr- family:** Words derived from Greek *astron* (star).
- **Sidereal family:** Words derived from Latin *sidus* (star/constellation).

These roots share the idea of stars but are not interchangeable etymologically.

We will first examine the genuine *stell-* family and then explore closely related astronomical vocabulary useful for UPSC.

**UPSC Application:**

## **15. Cosmic**

**Part of Speech:** Adjective\
**Etymology:** Greek *kosmos* = universe, order or world.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Cosm-** = Universe/order
- **-ic** = Relating to

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Relating to the universe as an ordered whole.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word moved from the Greek philosophical idea of an ordered universe to the modern scientific concept of the cosmos.

It also acquired figurative meanings associated with something immensely large or significant.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

Relating to the universe or the vastness of space.

### **E. Common Usage**

| Collocation        | Meaning                                               |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Cosmic radiation   | High-energy radiation from space                      |
| Cosmic evolution   | Development of the universe                           |
| Cosmic perspective | Understanding humanity in the context of the universe |

**UPSC Application:**

- Cosmic radiation is an important consideration in human spaceflight.
- A cosmic perspective encourages reflection on humanity's shared existence and environmental responsibilities.

## **16. Extraterrestrial**

**Part of Speech:** Adjective/Noun\
**Etymology:** Latin *extra* (outside) + *terrestris* (of Earth).

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Extra-** = Outside
- **Terrestr-** = Earth
- **-ial** = Relating to

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Existing outside Earth.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The development of astronomy and space exploration popularised the term in discussions about life and objects beyond our planet.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

Originating, existing or occurring outside Earth.

### **E. Common Usage**

| Collocation                   | Meaning                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Extraterrestrial life         | Life beyond Earth                          |
| Extraterrestrial intelligence | Hypothetical intelligent life beyond Earth |
| Extraterrestrial environment  | Environment outside Earth                  |

**UPSC Application:**

- The search for extraterrestrial life is an important frontier of scientific research.
- The possibility of extraterrestrial microbial life raises questions about planetary protection and scientific ethics.

## **17. Orbit**

**Part of Speech:** Noun/Verb\
**Etymology:** Latin *orbita* = track or course.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

\- **Orbit** = A circular or curved path

### **B. Literal Meaning**

A track or path followed by a moving object.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally referring to a wheel track or circular path, the word acquired its astronomical meaning to describe the path of a celestial body around another body.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

The curved path followed by an object under the influence of gravity around another object.

### **E. Common Usage**

| Collocation         | Meaning                                                         |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Geostationary orbit | Orbit in which a satellite appears stationary relative to Earth |
| Low Earth orbit     | Orbit relatively close to Earth                                 |
| Orbital velocity    | Velocity required to maintain a particular orbit                |

**UPSC Application:**

- Satellite-based communication depends on appropriate orbital placement.
- India's space programme has strengthened its capacity in satellite deployment and orbital operations.

Star → Disaster → Ill-starred → Catastrophe

Star → Astronaut → Star sailor → Space exploration

Star → Sidereal → Star-referenced → Sidereal day

**A final writing lesson for UPSC:** Use *stellar* selectively to convey excellence, *constellation* to convey interconnectedness, and *astronomical* to convey exceptionally large magnitude. These words are most effective when they add precision rather than merely decorate a sentence.
