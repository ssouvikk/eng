# **WORD POWER MADE EASY: STATURE**



UPSC Vocabulary Builder

Etymology-based learning

Root: STA / STARE



## **1. STATURE**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈstætʃər/\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Root Family:** STA– (to stand)\
**Origin:** Latin *statura* → Latin *status* / *stare* → Old French → English

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **STA / STAT** = To stand, to stand upright, to have a position.
- **-URE** = A suffix used to form nouns indicating a condition, result, state or action.

The underlying Latin verb is *stare*, meaning **to stand**.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Stature literally refers to the condition or measurement of standing upright — particularly, one's physical height.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word underwent an interesting expansion of meaning:

**Stage 1: Physical dimension**

The height or bodily measurement of a person.

**Stage 2: Figurative standing**

A person's position, standing or importance in society.

**Stage 3: Intellectual and moral stature**

The degree of respect, influence, authority or achievement associated with a person.

**Central idea:** Physical height → Social standing → Intellectual and moral eminence.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Stature** has two principal meanings:

1. **Physical height:** The natural height of a human being.
2. **Figurative importance or eminence:** The level of respect, authority, reputation or influence that an individual or institution commands.

**Examples:**

- Physical: The average stature of the population has increased over successive generations.
- Figurative: Dr B.R. Ambedkar's intellectual and constitutional stature remains unparalleled in modern Indian history.
- Institutional: India's growing stature in international diplomacy reflects its expanding geopolitical influence.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation           | Meaning                                       |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| International stature | Global standing or recognition of a country   |
| Intellectual stature  | Intellectual eminence or scholarly reputation |
| Political stature     | Political importance, influence or standing   |

**Additional useful expressions:**

- A person of considerable stature.
- Enhance India's global stature.
- Rise in international stature.

### **F. Important Distinction**

Do not confuse these expressions:

| Word       | Meaning                                       |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Stature    | Importance, eminence or physical height       |
| Status     | Position or rank in society or an institution |
| Standing   | Reputation or accepted position               |
| Status quo | Existing state of affairs                     |

**UPSC usage:**

Instead of writing:

"India has become an important country in global affairs."

Write:

"India's growing economic capabilities, strategic autonomy and diplomatic engagement have enhanced its international stature."

This is more precise and academically sophisticated.

# **2. THE ETYMOLOGICAL ROOT FAMILY: STA / STARE**

Master Root

The Latin verb **STARE = TO STAND** is the central source of this extensive vocabulary family.

Its deeper Indo-European ancestor is reconstructed as the Proto-Indo-European root:

\\[ \*steh_2 = \text{to stand} \\]

This root carries the fundamental idea of standing, remaining, occupying a position, establishing something or maintaining a condition.

Once you understand this root, several apparently unrelated English words become easier to remember.

# **3. DETAILED WORD FAMILY**

## **CATEGORY I: POSITION, RANK AND SOCIAL STANDING**

### **1. STATUS**

**Origin:** Latin *status* ← *stare* (to stand).

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **STAT** = Stand.
- **-US** = Latin noun-forming ending.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The condition or position in which something stands.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally, it referred to a person's or thing's condition, position or state. It gradually acquired the meaning of social rank, legal position and institutional standing.

**D. Modern Meaning**

The recognised position, condition, rank or standing of an individual, group, institution or situation.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Social status – position in the social hierarchy.
2. Legal status – a person's legally recognised position.
3. Status quo – the existing state of affairs.

**UPSC example:** Caste continues to influence social status and access to opportunities in certain sections of Indian society.

### **2. STATION**

**Origin:** Latin *statio* ← *stare* (to stand).

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **STAT** = Stand.
- **-ION** = State, action or result.

**B. Literal Meaning**

A place or position where someone or something stands.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original meaning was a standing place or position. It later referred to a fixed location, a military post, a railway stopping point and eventually a person's position in life.

**D. Modern Meaning**

A fixed place, designated position or point of activity.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Railway station – designated railway stopping point.
2. Police station – local police establishment.
3. Station in life – one's social or professional position.

**UPSC example:** Police stations constitute the first institutional point of contact between citizens and the criminal justice system.

### **3. STANCE**

**Origin:** Old French *estance*, ultimately connected with Latin *stare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **STAN / ST** = Stand or position.
- **-ANCE** = State or condition.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The position in which one stands.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

A physical standing position gradually acquired a figurative meaning: the position one adopts on an issue.

**D. Modern Meaning**

An individual's or institution's definite attitude, position or viewpoint regarding a particular issue.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Firm stance – strongly maintained position.
2. Government's stance – official position of the government.
3. Diplomatic stance – a country's position on an international issue.

**UPSC example:** India's diplomatic stance on global conflicts reflects its commitment to strategic autonomy and peaceful coexistence.

## **CATEGORY II: ESTABLISHMENT, INSTITUTIONS AND CONSTITUTIONAL VOCABULARY**

This is particularly important for GS-II, Polity, Governance and Essay writing.

### **4. ESTABLISH**

**Origin:** Old French *establir* ← Latin *stabilire* ← *stabilis* (firm, stable) ← *stare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **STA / STAB** = Stand firmly.
- **-ISH** = Verb-forming ending in this historical formation.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To make something stand firmly or become firmly established.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original idea of making something stable developed into creating something permanent, organised or institutionally recognised.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To create, found, institute or firmly establish an organisation, system, principle or fact.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Establish an institution – create an organisation.
2. Establish constitutional supremacy – secure the authority of the Constitution.
3. Establish the rule of law – create a system in which law governs everyone.

**UPSC example:** Institutional reforms are necessary to establish transparency, accountability and public trust in governance.

### **5. STABLE**

**Origin:** Latin *stabilis* ← *stare* (to stand).

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **STAB** = Stand firmly.
- **-LE** = Adjectival ending.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Something that stands firmly and does not easily collapse or change.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The idea of physical firmness expanded to economic, political, psychological and institutional steadiness.

**D. Modern Meaning**

Firm, steady, reliable or resistant to sudden changes or disturbances.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Political stability – continuity and predictability in political institutions.
2. Macroeconomic stability – relative stability in prices, output and financial conditions.
3. Stable governance – consistent and effective functioning of government.

**UPSC example:** Macroeconomic stability is essential for sustainable economic growth and long-term investment.

**Word-building extension:**

- Stability – condition of being stable.
- Stabilise – to make stable.
- Stabilisation – the process of making something stable.
- Unstable – lacking stability.

### **6. INSTITUTE**

**Origin:** Latin *instituere* = to establish, arrange or set up; from *in-* + *statuere*, related to *stare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **IN-** = In, upon or into.
- **STATUERE** = To set up, establish or cause to stand.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To set something up or establish it.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The verb originally referred to establishing, arranging or setting something in place. The noun developed to describe an established organisation devoted to a particular purpose.

**D. Modern Meaning**

As a verb: To introduce, establish or initiate a system, policy or arrangement.

As a noun: An organisation created for education, research, training or a specialised purpose.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Institute reforms – introduce reforms.
2. Institute an inquiry – formally initiate an investigation.
3. Educational institution – an organisation devoted to education.

**UPSC example:** The government should institute independent mechanisms to investigate allegations of corruption.

### **7. INSTITUTION**

**Origin:** Latin *institutio* ← *instituere* ← *statuere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **IN-** = In or into.
- **STATUERE** = To establish or set up.
- **-TION** = Process, action or result.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The act or result of establishing something.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The meaning expanded from the act of setting something up to an established arrangement, system, custom or organisation.

**D. Modern Meaning**

An enduring organisation, system, rule or established social arrangement that structures human behaviour and social relations.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Constitutional institutions – bodies established under the Constitution.
2. Democratic institutions – structures that sustain democratic governance.
3. Institutional accountability – mechanisms that make organisations answerable for their actions.

**UPSC example:** Strong democratic institutions act as safeguards against the concentration and arbitrary exercise of power.

**Important distinction:**

An *institute* can mean an organisation or the act of introducing something. An *institution* generally refers to an established organisation, system or social structure.

### **8. CONSTITUTE**

**Origin:** Latin *constituere* = to set up, establish or compose; *con-* + *statuere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CON-** = Together.
- **STATUERE** = To establish or set up.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To set up or establish something together.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The meaning developed from putting things together to forming, composing or legally establishing an entity.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To form, compose, establish or amount to something.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Constitute a committee – formally establish a committee.
2. Constitute a violation – amount to a violation.
3. Constitute an integral part – form an essential component.

**UPSC example:** Fundamental Rights constitute an essential foundation of India's constitutional democracy.

### **9. CONSTITUTION**

**Origin:** Latin *constitutio* ← *constituere* ← *statuere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CON-** = Together.
- **STATUERE** = To establish or set up.
- **-TION** = Act or result.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The act of putting something together or establishing its structure.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

It originally referred to the arrangement, establishment or composition of something. Over time, it acquired the meaning of a formal body of fundamental rules governing a state or organisation.

**D. Modern Meaning**

The fundamental legal framework that establishes the structure, powers, functions and limitations of government and defines the rights and obligations of citizens.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Constitutional morality – adherence to constitutional values and principles.
2. Constitutional supremacy – the Constitution's position as the supreme law.
3. Constitutional governance – governance conducted within constitutional limits.

**UPSC example:** Constitutional morality requires public institutions to uphold liberty, equality, fraternity and dignity beyond mere formal compliance with the law.

### **10. CONSTITUENT**

**Origin:** Latin *constituens* ← *constituere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CON-** = Together.
- **STITU / STATUERE** = To establish or form.
- **-ENT** = One that performs an action or has a particular role.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Something or someone that forms part of an entity.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

It developed from the idea of forming or establishing something to describing the individual components or members that make up a whole.

**D. Modern Meaning**

A component, element or member of a larger entity. In politics, it can refer to a person represented by an elected representative.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Constituent Assembly – body responsible for framing a constitution.
2. Constituent elements – components that make up a whole.
3. Constituency and constituents – an electoral area and its voters.

**UPSC example:** The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution of India on 26 November 1949.

### **11. STATUTE**

**Origin:** Latin *statutum* ← *statuere* = to establish, decree or prescribe.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **STATU** = To establish or set down.
- **-TUM** = Latin past participle / result-forming element.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Something formally established or laid down.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The idea of formally laying down a rule evolved into a written law enacted by a legislative authority.

**D. Modern Meaning**

A written law formally enacted by Parliament or a State Legislature.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Statutory body – an organisation established under a statute.
2. Statutory provisions – provisions contained in legislation.
3. Statutory obligations – duties imposed by law.

**UPSC example:** The Central Information Commission is a statutory body established under the Right to Information Act, 2005.

**Related words:**

- Statutory – established or required by statute.
- Statutorily – in accordance with a statute.
- Statute-book – the body of enacted laws.

## **CATEGORY III: CONTINUITY, CONSISTENCY AND PERSISTENCE**

These words are particularly useful in Ethics, Essay, Governance, International Relations and GS-II/GS-IV.

### **12. CONSTANT**

**Origin:** Latin *constans* ← *constare* = to stand firm; *con-* + *stare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CON-** = Together, firmly.
- **STANT** = Standing.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Standing firm or remaining unchanged.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original sense of standing firmly developed into the idea of remaining steady, dependable or unchanged over time.

**D. Modern Meaning**

Something that remains continuous, consistent or relatively unchanged.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Constant vigilance – continuous alertness.
2. Constant endeavour – sustained effort.
3. Constant change – continuous transformation.

**UPSC example:** Democratic governance requires constant vigilance against corruption and the arbitrary exercise of executive power.

### **13. CONSISTENT**

**Origin:** Latin *consistere* = to stand together, remain firm; *con-* + *sistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CON-** = Together.
- **SISTERE** = To stand, remain or take a position.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Standing together firmly or remaining in the same position.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The idea of standing together developed into logical agreement, uniformity and reliability over time.

**D. Modern Meaning**

Acting or behaving in a manner that is compatible, coherent and free from significant contradictions.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Consistent with constitutional principles.
2. Consistent policy framework.
3. Consistent performance.

**UPSC example:** Government policies must remain consistent with constitutional principles and the broader objectives of inclusive development.

**Related vocabulary:**

- Consistency – the quality of being consistent.
- Inconsistent – contradictory or incompatible.
- Inconsistency – lack of agreement or uniformity.

### **14. CONSISTENCY**

**Origin:** Latin *consistentia* ← *consistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CON-** = Together.
- **SISTERE** = To stand or remain firm.
- **-ENCY** = State or quality.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The quality of standing together or remaining firm.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

It developed from physical firmness to logical coherence, regularity and reliability.

**D. Modern Meaning**

The quality of maintaining uniformity, coherence and dependability in thought, action, performance or policy.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Policy consistency – continuity and coherence in public policy.
2. Internal consistency – absence of contradictions within an argument or system.
3. Consistency in implementation – uniform execution of policies.

**UPSC example:** Policy consistency is crucial for attracting long-term investment and ensuring administrative predictability.

### **15. PERSIST**

**Origin:** Latin *persistere* = to continue steadfastly; *per-* + *sistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **PER-** = Through, thoroughly or continuously.
- **SISTERE** = To stand or remain.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To continue standing firmly through difficulties or over time.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The meaning developed from remaining steadfast to continuing an action despite obstacles, opposition or difficulties.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To continue firmly or exist over an extended period despite challenges or attempts to eliminate something.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Persistent poverty – poverty that continues over time.
2. Persistent inequalities – inequalities that remain despite interventions.
3. Persist in efforts – continue making efforts despite difficulties.

**UPSC example:** Despite decades of developmental interventions, regional disparities and multidimensional poverty persist in several parts of India.

**Related words:**

- Persistence – continued effort or existence.
- Persistent – continuing despite difficulty.
- Persistently – in a continuous or determined manner.

### **16. INSIST**

**Origin:** Latin *insistere* = to stand upon or press firmly; *in-* + *sistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **IN-** = Upon or against.
- **SISTERE** = To stand.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To stand firmly upon a particular position.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original idea of standing firmly on something evolved into maintaining a position forcefully or demanding something firmly.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To state or demand something firmly, refusing to abandon one's position.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Insist on transparency.
2. Insist upon accountability.
3. Insist on procedural safeguards.

**UPSC example:** Citizens and civil society organisations increasingly insist on transparency and accountability in public administration.

**Related words:**

- Insistence – firm demand or assertion.
- Insistent – demanding attention or action persistently.
- Insistently – in a forceful or persistent manner.

### **17. EXIST**

**Origin:** Latin *existere* / *exsistere* = to emerge, appear or come into being; *ex-* + *sistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **EX-** = Out, forth.
- **SISTERE** = To stand or emerge.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To stand forth, emerge or come into being.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original idea of emerging or appearing developed into the broader philosophical and everyday concept of being or having actual existence.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To be real, to be present or to continue to live or operate.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Coexistence of diverse communities.
2. Existing institutional framework.
3. Existential crisis – a crisis concerning the meaning, purpose or nature of existence.

**UPSC example:** India's constitutional framework seeks to ensure the peaceful coexistence of diverse linguistic, religious and cultural communities.

**Related words:**

- Existence – the state of being.
- Existing – currently present or operating.
- Coexist – to exist together.
- Coexistence – peaceful or simultaneous existence.

### **18. RESIST**

**Origin:** Latin *resistere* = to stand against; *re-* + *sistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **RE-** = Against or back.
- **SISTERE** = To stand.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To stand against something.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original meaning of physically standing against an opponent developed into opposing, withstanding or refusing to accept something.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To oppose, withstand or refuse to submit to a force, pressure, influence or authority.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Resist authoritarianism.
2. Resistance to social change.
3. Institutional resistance to reform.

**UPSC example:** Constitutional safeguards help resist the arbitrary concentration of executive power.

**Related words:**

- Resistance – opposition or ability to withstand.
- Resistant – capable of withstanding something.
- Resilience – the capacity to recover from adversity; related conceptually, but not a derivative of *resist*.

### **19. RESISTANCE**

**Origin:** Latin *resistentia* ← *resistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **RE-** = Against.
- **SISTERE** = To stand.
- **-ANCE** = State or condition.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The condition of standing against something.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

It developed from physical opposition to broader forms of political, social, cultural and psychological opposition.

**D. Modern Meaning**

The act or capacity of opposing, withstanding or preventing a particular force, policy, authority or influence.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Resistance movements – organised opposition to an oppressive authority or occupation.
2. Antimicrobial resistance – the ability of microorganisms to withstand antimicrobial medicines.
3. Institutional resistance – opposition to organisational or administrative change.

**UPSC example:** Antimicrobial resistance is an emerging public health challenge that requires coordinated action under the One Health approach.

### **20. SUBSIST**

**Origin:** Latin *subsistere* = to stand firm, remain or withstand; *sub-* + *sistere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **SUB-** = Under, beneath or up to.
- **SISTERE** = To stand or remain.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To remain standing or continue to exist.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original idea of continuing to stand developed into the meaning of maintaining existence, especially with limited resources.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To remain alive or continue to exist, often with the minimum resources necessary for survival.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Subsistence agriculture – agriculture primarily intended to meet the needs of the farmer's household.
2. Subsistence livelihood – a livelihood that provides basic necessities.
3. Subsistence economy – an economy in which production is largely directed towards basic consumption.

**UPSC example:** A significant section of India's agricultural population continues to depend on small-scale and subsistence-oriented farming.

**Related words:**

- Subsistence – means of maintaining life.
- Subsistence-level income – income sufficient only for basic necessities.
- Subsistence farming – farming primarily for household consumption.

## **CATEGORY IV: POSITION, DISTANCE AND CIRCUMSTANCES**

### **21. DISTANCE**

**Origin:** Latin *distantia* ← *distare* = to stand apart; *dis-* + *stare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **DIS-** = Apart or separately.
- **STARE** = To stand.
- **-ANCE** = State or condition.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The condition of standing apart.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original spatial separation developed into physical, temporal, emotional and conceptual separation.

**D. Modern Meaning**

The measurable or perceived separation between two points, objects, individuals or ideas.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Geographical distance – physical separation between places.
2. Social distance – perceived separation between social groups.
3. Maintain distance from political interference – remain institutionally independent.

**UPSC example:** Social distance arising from caste-based discrimination continues to undermine substantive equality.

### **22. INSTANCE**

**Origin:** Latin *instantia* ← *instare* = to stand near, press upon or be present.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **IN-** = Upon or near.
- **STARE** = To stand.
- **-ANCE** = State or condition.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Something that stands before us or is immediately present.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The idea of something being present or pressing upon attention developed into a particular example or occurrence illustrating a general principle.

**D. Modern Meaning**

A particular example, occurrence or case of something.

**E. Common Usage**

1. For instance – for example.
2. An instance of corruption – a particular case of corruption.
3. In the first instance – at the initial stage or in the first place.

**UPSC example:** The Right to Information Act is an important instance of institutionalising transparency in governance.

### **23. CIRCUMSTANCE**

**Origin:** Latin *circumstantia* ← *circumstare* = to stand around; *circum-* + *stare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CIRCUM-** = Around.
- **STARE** = To stand.
- **-ANCE** = State or condition.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The conditions or things standing around a person or event.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original image of surrounding conditions developed into the collective conditions influencing an event, decision or individual.

**D. Modern Meaning**

A fact, condition or situation that influences or affects an event, decision or person's behaviour.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Socioeconomic circumstances.
2. Under exceptional circumstances.

**UPSC example:** Public policy must account for the socioeconomic circumstances of vulnerable and marginalised communities.

**Related words:**

- Circumstantial – based on surrounding facts or indirect evidence.
- Circumstantially – in relation to the surrounding circumstances.
- Circumstantial evidence – evidence that indirectly establishes a fact.

### **24. CONSTANTLY**

**Origin:** From *constant* ← Latin *constare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **CON-** = Together or firmly.
- **STANT** = Standing.
- **-LY** = In a particular manner.

**B. Literal Meaning**

In a manner that continues to stand or remain.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The idea of remaining firm developed into the idea of happening continuously or repeatedly.

**D. Modern Meaning**

Continuously, repeatedly or without significant interruption.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Constantly evolving society.
2. Constantly changing economic environment.
3. Constantly monitor implementation.

**UPSC example:** Public institutions must constantly adapt to emerging technological and socioeconomic challenges.

## **CATEGORY V: RESTORATION, SUBSTITUTION AND REINSTATEMENT**

### **25. RESTORE**

**Origin:** Old French *restorer* ← Latin *restaurare*.

This word is traditionally connected to the Indo-European *sta* family through its historical development, although its Latin formation is not simply *re-* + *stare*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **RE-** = Again or back.
- **STAURARE** = To rebuild or restore.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To rebuild something or bring it back to its former condition.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The meaning expanded from physically rebuilding something to recovering a previous condition, right, institution or relationship.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To bring something back to its original, previous or desired condition.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Restore constitutional order.
2. Restore ecological balance.
3. Restore public confidence.

**UPSC example:** Institutional transparency and effective grievance redressal are essential to restore public confidence in governance.

**Related words:**

- Restoration – the process of restoring.
- Restorative justice – an approach to justice focused on repairing harm.
- Restorative measures – measures aimed at recovery or repair.

### **26. REINSTATE**

**Origin:** *Re-* + *instate*, ultimately associated with Latin *status* and *statuere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **RE-** = Again or back.
- **INSTATE** = To place in a position or office.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To put someone back into an earlier position.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The original idea of placing someone in a position developed into restoring a person to a previously held office, rank, right or condition.

**D. Modern Meaning**

To restore someone to a previous position, employment, status or legal condition.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Reinstate an employee.
2. Reinstate constitutional safeguards.
3. Reinstate a suspended official.

**UPSC example:** Administrative decisions involving the reinstatement of public officials must comply with principles of natural justice and applicable service rules.

### **27. SUBSTITUTE**

**Origin:** Latin *substituere* = to put in place of another; *sub-* + *statuere*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **SUB-** = Under, in place of.
- **STITUERE** = To place or establish.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To place one thing in the position of another.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The idea of placing something in another's position developed into replacing one person, thing or arrangement with another.

**D. Modern Meaning**

A person or thing that replaces another; also, to use one thing instead of another.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Substitute teacher – a teacher replacing the regular teacher.
2. Substitute goods – goods that can replace one another in consumption.
3. Substitute one policy for another – replace an existing policy.

**UPSC example:** Renewable energy can substitute for conventional fossil-fuel-based energy sources in several sectors.

**Related words:**

- Substitution – replacement of one thing by another.
- Substitutability – the ability of one thing to replace another.
- Substitutable – capable of being replaced.

## **CATEGORY VI: EVERYDAY ROOT DERIVATIVES WITH HIGH UPSC UTILITY**

The following words belong to the Germanic branch of the same ancient Indo-European root family. They are especially useful because they connect the Latin vocabulary studied above with ordinary English.

### **28. UNDERSTAND**

**Origin:** Old English *understandan*, formed from *under* + *standan*.

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **UNDER** = Among, between or in an underlying position (historically, its precise sense in this compound is debated).
- **STAND** = To stand.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Historically associated with standing among or being positioned in relation to something.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

# **5. IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS: AVOID UPSC VOCABULARY TRAPS**

| Stationary vs Stationery   | Stationary = not moving; Stationery = writing materials.                                    |

A group of people assembled together.

A group of individuals sharing a common characteristic, experience or period of exposure, often studied collectively.

**Common Usage**

- Demographic cohort

- *Study*: systematic investigation.

**Literal Meaning**

A systematic investigation of a defined group of people.

**Semantic Evolution**

The demographic and medical sciences adopted the term to describe research that follows a defined population over time to investigate outcomes associated with particular exposures or characteristics.

**Modern Meaning**

An observational research design in which a group sharing a characteristic or exposure is followed over time to examine the occurrence of particular outcomes.

**Common Usage**

- Longitudinal cohort study

Examination of a defined group.

**Semantic Evolution**

The expression distinguishes judicial institutions from royal courts and other assemblies.

**Modern Meaning**

A judicial institution authorised to hear disputes, interpret law and deliver judgments.

**Common Usage**

- Access to a court of law
- Independence of courts of law
- Appeal before a court of law

**UPSC connection:** GS-II – Rule of law, judicial review, constitutional remedies and access to justice.

## **21. COURT MARTIAL**

**Meaning of the Components**

- *Court*: judicial institution.
- *Martial*: relating to war or the military; from Latin *Martialis*, associated with Mars, the Roman god of war.

**Literal Meaning**

A military judicial institution.

**Semantic Evolution**

The term developed to describe a specialised judicial process for trying military personnel under military law.

**Modern Meaning**

A military court or judicial proceeding dealing with offences committed by members of the armed forces under applicable military law.

**Common Usage**

- General court martial
- Court martial proceedings
- Military discipline and court martial

**UPSC connection:** GS-II – Armed forces, military justice, constitutional governance and civil-military relations.

## **22. COURT OF PUBLIC OPINION**

**Meaning of the Components**

- *Court*: an institution of judgment.
- *Public opinion*: the collective views of society.

**Literal Meaning**

A metaphorical institution where society judges an individual or issue.

**Semantic Evolution**

The judicial metaphor was extended to describe social judgment outside formal legal institutions.

**Modern Meaning**

The collective judgment of society, particularly through public discourse and media.

**Common Usage**

- Trial in the court of public opinion
- Judged by the court of public opinion
- Influence of the court of public opinion

**UPSC connection:** GS-II – Media, democracy, accountability, public discourse and freedom of expression.

## **23. COURT OF LAST RESORT**

**Meaning of the Components**

- *Court*: judicial institution.
- *Last*: final.
- *Resort*: a source of help or recourse.

**Literal Meaning**

The final institution to which one can turn for judicial relief.

**Semantic Evolution**

The expression came to denote the highest available judicial authority within a particular legal system.

**Modern Meaning**

The final appellate judicial authority in a jurisdiction.

**Common Usage**

- Supreme Court as the court of last resort
- Final judicial recourse
- Court of last resort in constitutional disputes

**UPSC connection:** GS-II – Supreme Court, appellate jurisdiction, constitutional remedies and finality of judicial decisions.

# **9. Related Cognates: The Wider ROOT FAMILY**

Some words are not direct derivatives of *cohors* or *hortus*, but are historically connected through the deeper Proto-Indo-European root *gher-* (to enclose, surround or grasp).

These are useful extensions for advanced vocabulary building.

## **24. GARDEN**

**Meaning of the Components**

- From Old French *gardin*, related to Frankish/Germanic forms meaning an enclosed garden.
- Ultimately associated with the broader Germanic and Indo-European vocabulary of enclosure.

**Literal Meaning**

An enclosed or protected area for cultivation.

**Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from the idea of an enclosed cultivated space into the general term for a cultivated area containing plants.

**Modern Meaning**

An area of land used for growing flowers, vegetables, fruits or ornamental plants.

**Common Usage**

- Kitchen garden
- Botanical garden
- Urban gardening

**UPSC connection:** GS-III – Urban agriculture, nutrition security and sustainable urban development.

## **25. YARD**

**Meaning of the Components**

- Old English *geard*: enclosure, courtyard or enclosed space.
- Related to the Germanic family of words meaning an enclosed area.

**Literal Meaning**

An enclosed area surrounding or adjoining a building.

**Semantic Evolution**

The original meaning of an enclosure gradually developed into the modern meanings of a domestic yard, workplace area and unit of measurement.

**Modern Meaning**

An open area adjoining a building or a specified enclosed area.

**Common Usage**

- Shipyard
- Construction yard
- Courtyard and yard spaces

**UPSC connection:** GS-I – Settlement geography and traditional architecture; GS-III – Industrial infrastructure.

**Etymological note:** *Garden* and *yard* are related cognates in the broader enclosure family, not direct derivatives of *curtain*.
