# **WORD POWER MADE EASY: OBLIVIOUS**

ETYMOLOGY • ROOT ANALYSIS • WORD FAMILY • UPSC VOCABULARY

## **1. OBLIVIOUS**

**Pronunciation:** /əˈblɪviəs/\
**Part of Speech:** Adjective\
**Core idea:** Being unaware of something because it has escaped one's attention or consciousness.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The word *oblivious* traces its origin to Latin:

- **Oblivisci** = to forget, to become forgetful.
- **Oblivio** = forgetfulness, oblivion.
- **Ob-** = a prefix historically associated with a range of meanings; it should not be interpreted mechanically as simply "against" in this word.

The important root is Latin **liv-/livi-**, associated with forgetting in the historical development of *oblivisci* and *oblivio*.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Oblivious = Having forgotten or having no awareness of something.**

Think of a person whose attention is so detached from reality that an important development takes place around them, yet they fail to notice it.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The semantic journey can be understood as:

*Forgetfulness → Absence of conscious awareness → Failure to notice → Unawareness of one's surroundings or circumstances.*

Originally associated with the idea of forgetting, the word gradually acquired the broader sense of being unaware or inattentive.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Oblivious (adjective):** Not aware of, or not noticing, something happening around oneself or something that should reasonably attract one's attention.

It often conveys more than simple ignorance: it suggests a failure to perceive something that is present, evident or significant.

**Important distinction:**

| Word        | Meaning                                                      |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Ignorant    | Lacking knowledge or information                             |
| Unaware     | Not conscious of something                                   |
| Oblivious   | Completely unaware, often despite something being noticeable |
| Inattentive | Not paying sufficient attention                              |
| Indifferent | Not caring about something                                   |

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1. **Oblivious to ground realities** – Unaware of actual conditions prevailing on the ground.
2. **Oblivious to social inequalities** – Failing to recognise the existence or seriousness of social inequalities.
3. **Remain oblivious to environmental degradation** – Continue to be unaware of ecological deterioration.

**Examples:**

- Policymakers who remain oblivious to ground realities often formulate ineffective welfare interventions.
- Economic growth cannot be considered inclusive if development strategies remain oblivious to regional disparities.
- Citizens cannot remain oblivious to their constitutional duties in a participatory democracy.

**Memory hook:** An *oblivious* person has effectively forgotten to notice what is happening around them.

## **2. THE ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY OF OBLIVIOUS**

The central Latin word is **OBLIVIO (forgetfulness)**, from which the English word family developed.

The family is relatively small. Unlike roots such as *dict* or *spect*, it does not generate a large number of productive English words. However, its derivatives are useful for expressing ideas concerning memory, historical neglect, public awareness and collective consciousness.

**Word-family tree**

Latin origin

# **OBLIVIO**

Forgetfulness / loss of memory

**OBLIVIOUS**

**Obliviously**

**OBLIVION**

The state of being forgotten or completely disregarded

Related historical formation: OBLIVIATE (to cause to forget)

*Etymological note:* These are derivatives and formations associated with the Latin noun *oblivio* and verb *oblivisci*. The sequence above illustrates the word family, not a claim that every word developed directly from the immediately preceding English word.

## **3. OBLIVION**

**Pronunciation:** /əˈblɪviən/\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Core idea:** The state of being forgotten, erased from memory or removed from public attention.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Latin:** *Oblivio* = forgetfulness, loss of memory.
- **Latin:** *Oblivionem* = accusative form of *oblivio*, meaning forgetfulness or oblivion.
- **English suffix:** *-ion* is part of the historical formation of the noun, not a separately meaningful suffix added to the modern English word *oblivious*.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Oblivion = A state of complete forgetfulness.**

Imagine an individual, institution, tradition or historical event gradually disappearing from collective memory.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

*Forgetfulness → Loss of remembrance → Disappearance from public memory → A state of being forgotten or ignored.*

The word expanded from individual forgetfulness to collective and historical forgetting.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Oblivion (noun):**

1. The state of being completely forgotten.
2. The condition of being ignored, neglected or no longer remembered.
3. A state of unconsciousness or complete unawareness in certain contexts.

In political and historical writing, it often describes the disappearance of events, people, institutions or cultural practices from public memory.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1. **Fall into oblivion** – Gradually disappear from public memory.
2. **Consign to oblivion** – Deliberately or effectively cause something to be forgotten.
3. **Sink into oblivion** – Become forgotten or irrelevant over time.

**Examples:**

- Several indigenous languages are at risk of falling into oblivion due to declining intergenerational transmission.
- Historical contributions of marginalised communities should not be consigned to oblivion.
- Traditional ecological knowledge may sink into oblivion if it is not documented and transmitted to younger generations.

**UPSC connection:** GS-I (Indian heritage and culture), GS-II (social justice), Anthropology (cultural transmission, acculturation and cultural change).

## **4. OBLIVIOUSLY**

**Pronunciation:** /əˈblɪviəsli/\
**Part of Speech:** Adverb\
**Core idea:** In an unaware or inattentive manner.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Oblivious** = unaware, not noticing.
- **-ly** = an adverb-forming suffix meaning in a particular manner.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Obliviously = In an unaware manner.**

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

*Forgetfulness → Lack of awareness → Unawareness expressed through one's behaviour → Acting without noticing something.*

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Obliviously (adverb):** In a manner that shows a lack of awareness of one's surroundings, circumstances or the implications of one's actions.

It is less frequently used in formal UPSC writing than *oblivious* itself.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1. **Act obliviously** – Behave without recognising the surrounding circumstances.
2. **Move obliviously through society** – Remain unaware of prevailing social realities.
3. **Obliviously ignore warning signs** – Fail to notice important indications of danger.

**Examples:**

- Administrative authorities cannot act obliviously to the concerns of vulnerable communities.
- Development interventions should not proceed obliviously to local cultural sensitivities.

**UPSC connection:** GS-II (responsive governance), GS-IV (empathy and sensitivity).

## **5. OBLIVIOUSNESS**

**Pronunciation:** /əˈblɪviəsnəs/\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Core idea:** The condition or quality of being unaware.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Oblivious** = unaware.
- **-ness** = a suffix that converts an adjective into a noun, expressing a state or quality.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Obliviousness = The state of being unaware.**

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

*Forgetfulness → Lack of awareness → A persistent condition of not noticing → Unawareness as a personal or institutional characteristic.*

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Obliviousness (noun):** The state of being unaware of one's surroundings, social realities, responsibilities or the consequences of one's actions.

Unlike *ignorance*, which can simply indicate a lack of knowledge, *obliviousness* often suggests a failure to recognise something that deserves attention.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1. **Institutional obliviousness** – Institutional failure to recognise important social or administrative realities.
2. **Obliviousness to social realities** – Failure to acknowledge prevailing social conditions.
3. **Wilful obliviousness** – Deliberately maintaining a state of unawareness or refusing to acknowledge an evident reality.

**Examples:**

- Institutional obliviousness to the needs of tribal communities can undermine the effectiveness of development programmes.
- Wilful obliviousness to gender discrimination perpetuates structural inequalities.
- Policy failures sometimes arise from bureaucratic obliviousness to local conditions.

**UPSC connection:** GS-II (accountability and governance), GS-IV (attitude, empathy and emotional intelligence).

## **6. OBLIVIATE**

**Pronunciation:** /əˈblɪvieɪt/\
**Part of Speech:** Verb (rare)\
**Core idea:** To cause someone to forget something.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Latin:** *Oblivisci* = to forget.
- **Obliviate** is a historical English formation associated with the same Latin word family.
- **-ate** = a verb-forming element in English.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Obliviate = To cause forgetfulness.**

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

*Forgetfulness → Causing something to be forgotten → Erasing or suppressing memory.*

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Obliviate (verb):** To cause someone to forget something; to erase a memory.

This is an uncommon and largely archaic or literary word. It is also familiar in popular culture through the *Harry Potter* series, where it refers to a memory-erasing spell.

It is not a recommended substitute for *forget*, *erase* or *suppress* in formal UPSC answers.

### **E. Common Usage: Illustrative Expressions**

1. **Obliviate a memory** – Cause a memory to be forgotten.
2. **Obliviate past experiences** – Erase memories of previous experiences.
3. **Obliviate an incident** – Make an incident disappear from someone's memory.

**UPSC connection:** Useful mainly for understanding etymology and literary vocabulary, not for routine answer writing.

## **7. IMPORTANT ETYMOLOGICAL DISTINCTION**

A few distinctions are important for accurate vocabulary building.

| Word          | Grammatical form | Central meaning            |
| ------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------- |
| Oblivious     | Adjective        | Unaware                    |
| Obliviously   | Adverb           | In an unaware manner       |
| Obliviousness | Noun             | State of being unaware     |
| Oblivion      | Noun             | State of being forgotten   |
| Obliviate     | Verb (rare)      | To cause someone to forget |

**Do not confuse:**

- **Obliviousness** = The state of not being aware.
- **Oblivion** = The state of being forgotten or disappearing from memory.

For example:

- The administration's **obliviousness** to tribal concerns resulted in policy failure.
- Several tribal languages are gradually falling into **oblivion**.

This distinction is particularly useful in GS-II and Anthropology.

# **WORD POWER MADE EASY: INTRUDING**

WORD ROOT STUDY • LATIN ROOT: TRUD / TRUS • UPSC VOCABULARY BUILDER

# **1. INTRUDING**

**Pronunciation:** in-TROO-ding\
**Part of Speech:** Verb (present participle of *intrude*)\
**Root:** Latin *trudere* = to thrust, push, or force

## **A. Meaning of the Components**

Break the word into its historical components:

| Component | Origin             | Meaning                     |
| --------- | ------------------ | --------------------------- |
| IN-       | Latin prefix *in-* | Into, in, or upon           |
| TRUD      | Latin *trudere*    | To thrust, push, or force   |
| -ING      | English suffix     | Indicates an ongoing action |

**Memory clue:** Imagine someone forcefully pushing themselves INTO a place where they are not welcome.

## **B. Literal Meaning**

**Intruding = thrusting oneself into something or somewhere without invitation, permission, or justification.**

The central idea is not merely entering; it is entering by crossing a boundary.

## **C. Semantic Evolution**

The evolution can be understood in three stages:

1. **Physical thrusting:** The Latin verb *trudere* meant to push or thrust.
2. **Unwelcome entry:** The prefix *in-* added the idea of pushing into something, giving rise to Latin *intrudere*.
3. **Abstract interference:** The meaning expanded from physically entering an unwanted space to interfering in someone's affairs, privacy, rights, or domain.

Thus, the underlying idea of intrusion is **the violation of an established boundary**.

## **D. Modern Meaning**

**Intruding** means entering, interfering with, or imposing oneself upon a space, situation, activity, or domain without being invited, wanted, or authorised.

It has three important dimensions:

- **Physical:** Entering someone's private property without permission.
- **Social:** Interfering in another person's personal affairs.
- **Institutional/Political:** Interfering in the sovereignty, jurisdiction, or legitimate authority of another entity.

**UPSC conceptual connection:** Intrusion is closely associated with privacy, individual liberty, territorial sovereignty, institutional autonomy, and jurisdictional boundaries.

## **E. Common Usage (High-Frequency Collocations)**

1. **Intruding into someone's privacy** – Unnecessarily interfering with another person's private life.
2. **Intruding upon personal liberty** – Interfering with an individual's freedom or autonomy.
3. **Intruding into sovereign affairs** – Interfering in matters falling within another state's sovereign domain.

# **3. INTRUDE**

**Part of Speech:** Verb\
**Root:** IN- + TRUDERE

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **IN-** = Into
- **TRUDERE** = To thrust or push

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To push or thrust oneself into something.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally referred to physically forcing one's way into a place. Later, the meaning expanded to include unwelcome interference in personal, social, and institutional matters.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

To enter a place, situation, or domain without invitation, permission, or proper authority; to interfere in matters that do not concern oneself.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. **Intrude into personal matters** – Interfere in private affairs.
2. **Intrude upon privacy** – Violate someone's private space or personal autonomy.
3. **Intrude into another country's affairs** – Interfere in another country's internal matters.

**UPSC application:** State sovereignty, right to privacy, civil liberties, federal relations.

# **4. INTRUSION**

**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Root:** IN- + TRUDERE

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **IN-** = Into
- **TRUS** = Thrust or pushed
- **-ION** = Act, process, or result

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The act or result of thrusting into something.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The physical act of forcing entry gradually acquired a broader meaning: an unwanted entry or interference that violates an established boundary.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

An unwelcome or unauthorised entry into someone's space, privacy, affairs, or jurisdiction.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. **Government intrusion into private life** – Excessive state interference in citizens' personal affairs.
2. **Digital intrusion** – Unauthorised access to computer systems or networks.
3. **Foreign intrusion into sovereign territory** – Unauthorised entry into another state's territory.

**UPSC application:** Puttaswamy judgment, data protection, cybersecurity, national security, sovereignty.

# **5. INTRUSIVE**

**Part of Speech:** Adjective\
**Root:** IN- + TRUS + -IVE

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **IN-** = Into
- **TRUS** = Thrust
- **-IVE** = Having the quality or tendency of

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Having the tendency to thrust oneself into something.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word evolved from describing something that physically forces its way into a space to describing an action, technology, policy, or behaviour that interferes excessively with privacy or autonomy.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

Causing unwanted interference, particularly with someone's privacy, personal space, or freedom.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. **Intrusive surveillance** – Surveillance that excessively interferes with individual privacy.
2. **Intrusive state intervention** – Excessive government interference in private or social affairs.
3. **Intrusive questioning** – Asking excessively personal or inappropriate questions.

**UPSC application:** Proportionality test, privacy, surveillance reforms, welfare-state intervention.

# **6. EXTRUDE**

**Part of Speech:** Verb\
**Root:** EX- + TRUDERE

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **EX-** = Out, outside
- **TRUDERE** = To thrust or push

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To push something out.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally referred to forcing or pushing something outward. It developed technical meanings in geology, manufacturing, and material science.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

To force or push a substance through an opening or mould, usually to produce a particular shape.

It can also refer to molten material emerging onto the Earth's surface.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. **Extrusion of molten material** – The emergence of molten rock or material.
2. **Extrusion process in manufacturing** – Forcing material through a shaped opening to produce a continuous product.
3. **Extrusive igneous rocks** – Rocks formed when lava cools on the Earth's surface.

**UPSC application:** Indian geography, volcanism, igneous rocks, industrial manufacturing.

- **EX-** = Out
- **TRUS** = Thrust
- **-ION** = Act or process

The act or process of pushing something out.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from the general act of forcing something outward to specialised technical meanings in geology and industrial engineering.

The process of forcing a material through a shaped opening or the emergence of molten material onto the Earth's surface.

## **1. INTERMINGLE**

**Pronunciation:** /ˌɪntərˈmɪŋɡəl/\
**Part of Speech:** Verb (transitive and intransitive)\
**Word Structure:** INTER + MINGLE

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

| Component | Origin               | Meaning                   |
| --------- | -------------------- | ------------------------- |
| INTER-    | Latin *inter*        | Between, among, mutually  |
| MINGLE    | Old English *mengan* | To mix, blend, or combine |

**INTER-** indicates interaction or a relationship between two or more entities.

**MINGLE** indicates bringing different elements together so that they become mixed.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To mix different things with or among one another.

Think of two different streams of water joining together. Their individual waters intermingle, forming a common flow.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from the idea of physically mixing separate substances to the broader idea of combining abstract, social, cultural, or emotional elements.

The semantic progression is:

**Physical mixing**

Different substances combine.

**Mutual interaction**

Different people or groups interact.

**Abstract integration**

Different ideas, emotions and influences combine.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Intermingle (verb):** To become mixed or combined with one another, often in such a way that the individual elements remain distinguishable.

It can refer to:

1. **Physical combination:** Different substances or elements mixing together.
2. **Social interaction:** People belonging to different communities or groups interacting.
3. **Cultural integration:** Different cultural traditions, practices and influences blending.
4. **Abstract combination:** Different emotions, ideas, interests or influences becoming intertwined.

**Important semantic distinction:**

Intermingling does not necessarily imply complete assimilation.

Two cultures may intermingle while retaining their distinctive identities.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation                                       | Meaning                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Intermingling of cultures                         | Interaction and blending of different cultural traditions    |
| Intermingling of communities                      | Social interaction between different communities             |
| Intermingling of economic and political interests | Overlapping or combining of economic and political interests |

**UPSC-ready examples:**

- India's composite culture emerged through centuries of intermingling of diverse cultural traditions.
- Social cohesion requires greater intermingling among different caste, religious and linguistic communities.
- The intermingling of economic and political interests can complicate public policymaking.

**Synonyms:** Blend, intermingle, intermix, commingle, merge, mingle.

**Antonyms (contextual):** Separate, segregate, isolate, differentiate.

## **1. DICEY**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdaɪ.si/ (DYE-see)\
**Part of Speech:** Adjective\
**Register:** Informal; frequently used in journalism, political commentary and analytical writing.

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Dice:** A small cube marked with numbers, traditionally used in games of chance.
- **-y:** A suffix meaning *characterised by, full of, or inclined towards something*.

Thus:

**Dice + -y = Dicey**

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Something that resembles a game of dice: uncertain, dependent on chance, and potentially risky.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from the association between dice games and uncertainty.

1. Dice games involve chance rather than complete control.
2. Outcomes become unpredictable and uncertain.
3. Such uncertainty can involve danger, risk or an unfavourable outcome.
4. The word consequently acquired the figurative meaning of a situation that is uncertain, risky or potentially problematic.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Dicey (adjective):** Risky, uncertain, precarious or potentially dangerous; involving considerable possibility of an undesirable outcome.

It is particularly useful when a situation is not necessarily dangerous but has become difficult to predict or safely manage.

**Synonyms:** Risky, precarious, hazardous, uncertain, touch-and-go.

**Antonyms:** Safe, secure, certain, favourable, risk-free.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation               | Meaning / Context                      |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| A dicey situation         | A risky or uncertain situation         |
| A dicey proposition       | A proposal involving considerable risk |
| A politically dicey issue | An issue carrying political risks      |

**UPSC application:**

- The government's decision to withdraw subsidies presents a politically dicey situation.
- Negotiating a ceasefire amid mutual distrust is a dicey proposition.
- Unregulated expansion of artificial intelligence presents a dicey policy challenge.

**Answer-writing caution:** Dicey is informal. In formal UPSC Mains answers, prefer *precarious, uncertain, contentious, high-risk* or *fraught with challenges*, depending on the context.

# **PREMISES**



UPSC Vocabulary Builder

Latin Root: MITT / MISS



**Pronunciation:** /ˈprɛmɪsɪz/\
**Part of Speech:** Noun (plural); singular: *premise*\
**Core Meaning:** A proposition or assumption on which an argument, theory, or conclusion is based.

## **1. Meaning of the Components**

The word **premises** comes from Latin *praemissa*, the feminine past participle of *praemittere*.

| Component       | Meaning            |
| --------------- | ------------------ |
| **Prae-**       | Before, in advance |
| **Mittere**     | To send            |
| **Praemittere** | To send before     |

**Memory hook:** A premise is something *sent before* the argument begins. It is the foundational proposition that precedes logical reasoning and supports a conclusion.

**Important distinction:** The root of *premises* is **MITT / MISS**, not *MIS* as a standalone prefix.

## **2. Literal Meaning**

**Premise = Something sent or stated beforehand.**

In classical logic, premises are propositions established or assumed before deriving a conclusion.

Example:

- Premise 1: All human beings are mortal.
- Premise 2: Socrates is a human being.
- Conclusion: Socrates is mortal.

The first two statements are the premises from which the conclusion logically follows.

## **3. Semantic Evolution**

The evolution can be understood through four stages:

1. **Latin origin:** *Praemittere* — to send beforehand.
2. **Medieval logic:** A proposition stated before another proposition in a logical argument.
3. **Philosophical usage:** A foundational proposition or assumption supporting an inference.
4. **Modern usage:** An underlying assumption, foundational idea, or proposition on which an argument, policy, theory, or decision rests.

## **4. Modern Meaning**

**Premise (singular):** A foundational proposition, assumption, or statement on which reasoning or an argument is based.

**Premises (plural):** The foundational propositions or assumptions supporting a conclusion.

There is also another meaning:

**Premises (physical location):** A building or property, particularly the land and buildings belonging to an establishment.

Example: Smoking is prohibited on these premises.

### **Important conceptual distinction**

| Term       | Meaning                                                          |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Premise    | Starting proposition or assumption                               |
| Argument   | Reasoning presented to establish a conclusion                    |
| Inference  | Derivation of a conclusion from available information            |
| Conclusion | The proposition derived from premises                            |
| Assumption | Something accepted as true without necessarily being established |
| Hypothesis | A tentative proposition that can be tested                       |

**UPSC analytical insight:** A logically valid conclusion can still be factually incorrect if its premises are false.

For example, a policy recommendation may be logically consistent but ineffective because it rests on an incorrect assumption about ground realities.

## **5. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Underlying premise** – A fundamental assumption supporting an argument or theory.

*Example:* The underlying premise of inclusive development is that economic growth must benefit all sections of society.

2\. **Question the premise** – Challenge the foundational assumption of an argument.

*Example:* The effectiveness of trickle-down economics requires us to question the premise that economic growth automatically reduces inequality.

3\. **On the premise that** – Based on the assumption that something is true.

*Example:* The policy was formulated on the premise that decentralisation improves public service delivery.

- Intermittent power supply
- Intermittent employment

**UPSC application:** Monsoon variability, water availability, rural infrastructure and informal employment.

## **7. Intermission**

**Components:** Inter (between) + mission (sending)

**Literal Meaning:** An interval between activities.

**Semantic Evolution:** A break between successive actions → a temporary pause → an interval in an ongoing activity.

**Modern Meaning:** A temporary interruption or break.

**Common Usage:**

- Temporary intermission
- Intermission in proceedings
- Intermission in economic activity

# **C. WORDS RELATED TO SENDING ACROSS OR THROUGH**

## **8. Transmit**

**Components:** Trans (across) + mittere (to send)

**Literal Meaning:** To send across.

**Semantic Evolution:** Sending something from one place to another → conveying information or signals → transferring diseases, information, energy or data.

**Modern Meaning:** To transfer or communicate something from one person, place, or system to another.

**Common Usage:**

- Transmission of infectious diseases
- Transmit information
- Transmission of electricity

**UPSC application:** Public health, digital governance, communication infrastructure and epidemiology.

## **9. Transmission**

**Components:** Trans (across) + miss (send) + ion (process)

**Literal Meaning:** The process of sending something across.

**Semantic Evolution:** Transfer from one place to another → communication of signals → movement or spread of something between individuals or systems.

**Modern Meaning:** The process of transferring or spreading something.

**Common Usage:**

- Disease transmission
- Transmission of power
- Knowledge transmission

**UPSC application:** Disease transmission, intergenerational transmission of poverty and cultural transmission in anthropology.

## **10. Transmissible**

**Components:** Trans (across) + miss (send) + ible (capable of being)

**Literal Meaning:** Capable of being sent across.

**Semantic Evolution:** Capable of being transferred → capable of spreading from one individual or system to another.

**Modern Meaning:** Capable of being transmitted or spread.

**Common Usage:**

- Transmissible diseases
- Transmissible infections
- Human-to-human transmissibility

**UPSC application:** Public health, epidemic management and infectious disease control.

## **11. Transmission mechanism**

**Components:** Trans (across) + mission (sending)

**Literal Meaning:** The mechanism through which something is transferred.

**Semantic Evolution:** A means of transferring something → a system through which economic, social, biological or institutional effects spread.

**Modern Meaning:** The process or channel through which an effect moves from one variable, individual or institution to another.

**Common Usage:**

- Monetary policy transmission mechanism
- Disease transmission mechanism
- Transmission mechanism of economic shocks

**UPSC application:** Monetary policy, inflation, financial inclusion and macroeconomic governance.

# **D. WORDS RELATED TO SENDING OUT OR RELEASING**

## **12. Emit**

**Components:** E / Ex (out) + mittere (to send)

**Literal Meaning:** To send out.

**Semantic Evolution:** To release something outward → to discharge energy, particles, gases or signals → to produce or release something.

**Modern Meaning:** To release or give out substances, energy, sound, light or signals.

**Common Usage:**

- Emit greenhouse gases
- Emit carbon dioxide
- Emit harmful radiation

**UPSC application:** Climate change, environmental pollution and industrial regulation.

## **13. Emission**

**Components:** E / Ex (out) + miss (send) + ion (process)

**Literal Meaning:** The process of sending something out.

**Semantic Evolution:** Release of something → discharge of gases or energy → environmental release of pollutants.

**Modern Meaning:** The release of gases, particles, energy or other substances into the environment.

**Common Usage:**

- Carbon emissions
- Greenhouse gas emissions
- Vehicular emissions

**UPSC application:** GS III – Climate change, air pollution, carbon neutrality and India's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).

## **14. Emitter**

**Components:** E (out) + mitt (send) + er (one that performs an action)

**Literal Meaning:** Something or someone that sends out.

**Semantic Evolution:** A source of released material or energy → an entity producing emissions or signals.

**Modern Meaning:** A person, organisation, machine or natural source that releases something.

**Common Usage:**

- Major carbon emitter
- Industrial emitter
- Radioactive emitter

**UPSC application:** Climate negotiations, industrial decarbonisation and polluter-pays principle.

## **15. Emissary**

**Components:** Latin *emissarius*, from *emittere* (to send out).

**Literal Meaning:** A person sent out on a mission.

**Semantic Evolution:** A person dispatched by an authority → an official representative → a messenger entrusted with a particular assignment.

**Modern Meaning:** A representative or messenger sent on a special mission.

**Common Usage:**

- Diplomatic emissary
- Special emissary
- Peace emissary

**UPSC application:** Diplomacy, international relations and conflict resolution.

# **E. WORDS RELATED TO SENDING BACK OR RETURNING**

## **16. Remit**

**Components:** Re (back) + mittere (to send)

**Literal Meaning:** To send back.

**Semantic Evolution:** Sending something back → transferring money or payment → referring a matter back to an authority for reconsideration.

**Modern Meaning:**

1. To send money as payment.
2. To refer a matter back to another authority.
3. To cancel or reduce a debt, penalty or obligation (in a different established sense).

**Common Usage:**

- Remit money
- Remit the case to a lower court
- Remit tax payments

**UPSC application:** Remittances, migration, judicial administration and public finance.

## **17. Remittance**

**Components:** Re (back) + miss (send) + ance (act or process)

**Literal Meaning:** The act of sending something back.

**Semantic Evolution:** Sending money back → transferring funds across geographical boundaries → money sent by migrants to their families or home countries.

**Modern Meaning:** A transfer of money, particularly by migrants or overseas workers to their home country.

**Common Usage:**

- Overseas remittances
- Inward remittances
- Remittance inflows

**UPSC application:** Indian diaspora, balance of payments, foreign exchange reserves, migration and development.

**Example:** Remittances from Indians working abroad contribute significantly to India's external-sector resilience.

## **18. Remiss**

**Components:** Latin *remissus*, past participle of *remittere* (to send back, slacken, relax).

**Literal Meaning:** Relaxed or slackened.

**Semantic Evolution:** Loosened or relaxed → lacking strictness or attention → negligent in fulfilling duties.

**Modern Meaning:** Careless, negligent or insufficiently attentive to one's responsibilities.

**Common Usage:**

- Remiss in duty
- Remiss in enforcing regulations
- Remiss in administrative oversight

**UPSC application:** Administrative accountability, public service ethics and regulatory governance.

**Example:** Public authorities would be remiss in their duty if they ignored persistent violations of environmental standards.

# **F. WORDS RELATED TO SENDING AWAY OR DISMISSING**

## **19. Dismiss**

**Components:** Dis (away / apart) + mittere (to send)

**Literal Meaning:** To send away.

**Semantic Evolution:** Sending someone away → releasing someone from an office or responsibility → rejecting a claim, proposal or case.

**Modern Meaning:**

1. To remove someone from employment or office.
2. To reject an argument, claim or application.
3. To formally terminate proceedings in certain contexts.

**Common Usage:**

- Dismiss an employee
- Dismiss a petition
- Dismiss allegations

**UPSC application:** Administrative law, labour relations and judicial processes.

## **20. Dismissal**

**Components:** Dis (away) + miss (send) + al (act or process)

**Literal Meaning:** The act of sending someone away.

**Semantic Evolution:** Sending away → removal from a position → formal rejection or termination.

**Modern Meaning:** The act of removing someone from employment or rejecting a claim, application or case.

**Common Usage:**

- Unlawful dismissal
- Dismissal of a petition
- Summary dismissal

**UPSC application:** Labour rights, natural justice, judicial review and administrative accountability.

## **21. Dismissive**

**Components:** Dismiss (send away) + ive (characterised by)

**Literal Meaning:** Characterised by sending something away.

**Semantic Evolution:** Rejecting something → treating something as unworthy of consideration → showing disregard or contempt.

**Modern Meaning:** Showing that one considers someone or something unimportant or unworthy of attention.

**Common Usage:**

- Dismissive attitude
- Dismissive response
- Dismissive approach towards criticism

**UPSC application:** Civil service ethics, empathy, participatory governance and deliberative democracy.

# **G. WORDS RELATED TO SENDING OR ENTRUSTING ON A MISSION**

## **22. Mission**

**Components:** Latin *missio*, from *mittere* (to send).

**Literal Meaning:** The act of sending or being sent.

**Semantic Evolution:** A task assigned to a person sent somewhere → an official assignment → an important purpose or objective.

**Modern Meaning:** A specific task, objective, assignment or purpose.

**Common Usage:**

- National mission
- Mission-mode implementation
- Mission-oriented governance

**UPSC application:** Swachh Bharat Mission, National Health Mission, Digital India and Aspirational Districts Programme.

## **23. Missionary**

**Components:** Mission + ary (a person associated with an activity)

**Literal Meaning:** A person associated with a mission.

**Semantic Evolution:** A person sent on a religious mission → an individual engaged in spreading religious beliefs or ideas.

**Modern Meaning:** A person sent to promote a religious faith, particularly through evangelisation and related activities.

**Common Usage:**

- Missionary activities
- Missionary education
- Missionary institutions

**UPSC application:** Colonial history, tribal societies, religious change, education and cultural transformation.

**Anthropology connection:** Missionary activities have played an important role in cultural contact, acculturation, religious conversion and social change among indigenous communities.

## **24. Mission-critical**

**Components:** Mission (objective) + critical (essential)

**Literal Meaning:** Essential for accomplishing a mission.

**Semantic Evolution:** Essential to completing an assigned task → indispensable to the functioning of an organisation or system.

**Modern Meaning:** Extremely important for the successful functioning of a system, organisation or operation.

**Common Usage:**

- Mission-critical infrastructure
- Mission-critical services
- Mission-critical technology

**UPSC application:** Critical infrastructure, cybersecurity, defence preparedness and essential public services.

# **H. ADDITIONAL HIGH-VALUE WORDS FROM THE SAME ROOT**

These words extend the family into political science, sociology, governance and analytical writing.

## **25. Omit**

**Components:** Ob (away) + mittere (to send).

**Literal Meaning:** To send away or leave out.

**Semantic Evolution:** To leave something out → to fail to include something → to intentionally or unintentionally exclude.

**Modern Meaning:** To leave out or exclude something.

**Common Usage:**

- Omit relevant information
- Deliberately omit
- Omission of facts

**UPSC application:** Transparency, accountability, parliamentary reporting and research methodology.

## **26. Omission**

**Components:** Ob (away) + miss (send) + ion (process).

**Literal Meaning:** The act of leaving something out.

**Semantic Evolution:** Leaving out → failure to perform an expected action → neglect through non-performance.

**Modern Meaning:** The act of excluding something or failing to do something required.

**Common Usage:**

- Omission of duty
- Material omission
- Error of omission

**UPSC application:** Ethics, negligence, administrative responsibility and constitutional duties.

## **27. Commit**

**Components:** Com (together / completely) + mittere (to send).

**Literal Meaning:** To entrust or send something into someone's care.

**Semantic Evolution:** Entrusting something to another → binding oneself to an obligation → performing an act, including a crime.

**Modern Meaning:**

1. To pledge oneself to something.
2. To perform an act.
3. To entrust someone or something to a particular institution or authority.

**Common Usage:**

- Commit to reforms
- Commit a crime
- Commit public resources

**UPSC application:** Ethical commitment, institutional accountability, criminal justice and policy implementation.

## **28. Commitment**

**Components:** Commit + ment (state or result).

**Literal Meaning:** The state of being entrusted or bound to something.

**Semantic Evolution:** An obligation undertaken → dedication to a cause → willingness to fulfil a responsibility.

**Modern Meaning:** A firm promise, obligation or dedication towards a goal or responsibility.

**Common Usage:**

- Constitutional commitment
- Political commitment
- Commitment to social justice

**UPSC application:** Fundamental values, constitutional morality, public service ethics and sustainable development.

## **29. Committee**

**Components:** From *commit*, via the idea of being entrusted with a task.

**Literal Meaning:** A group entrusted with a particular responsibility.

**Semantic Evolution:** Persons appointed to undertake a specific task → an organised group responsible for deliberation, investigation or recommendations.

**Modern Meaning:** A group of people appointed or elected to examine a particular matter or perform a specific function.

**Common Usage:**

- Parliamentary standing committee
- Expert committee
- Committee recommendations

**UPSC application:** Parliamentary oversight, legislative scrutiny and institutional governance.

## **30. Committal**

**Components:** Commit + al (act or process).

**Literal Meaning:** The act of entrusting or placing something in someone's custody.

**Semantic Evolution:** Entrusting something to another authority → formally sending an accused person or matter to a competent court or institution.

**Modern Meaning:** The formal act of committing a person or matter to legal custody or further judicial proceedings.

**Common Usage:**

- Committal proceedings
- Committal to custody
- Committal of a case

**UPSC application:** Criminal justice administration and procedural law.

# **PART IV — UPSC APPLICATION: USING THE WORD FAMILY IN ANSWER WRITING**

The real value of etymology is not memorising isolated words. It is learning to use precise vocabulary in analytical arguments.

### **1. GS II – Governance**

"Effective governance requires institutional commitment, transparency and accountability. The submission of periodic reports and transmission of information across administrative levels strengthen oversight mechanisms."

Keywords: Commitment, submission, transmission, omission.

### **2. GS III – Environment**

"India's climate commitments require a substantial reduction in greenhouse gas emissions through cleaner technologies, renewable energy and effective monitoring of major industrial emitters."

Keywords: Emissions, emitter, commitment.

### **3. GS II – International Relations**

"Overseas remittances strengthen India's external-sector resilience, while diplomatic emissaries play an important role in facilitating negotiations and conflict resolution."

Keywords: Remittance, emissary, mission.

### **4. GS IV – Ethics**

"A civil servant must not be remiss in the discharge of public duties or dismissive of citizens' grievances. Constitutional commitment must translate into ethical administrative conduct."

Keywords: Remiss, dismissive, commitment.

### **5. Anthropology Optional**

"Missionary activities, migration and cultural transmission have contributed to processes of acculturation, religious change and social transformation among tribal communities."

Keywords: Missionary, transmission, transmissible.

- TRANS → Send across → Transmit
