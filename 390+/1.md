# **WORD POWER & ETYMOLOGY**

## **PREROGATIVE — The Power of Having a Special Right**



UPSC Vocabulary

Etymology-based learning



**Central idea:** A prerogative is a special right, privilege or power belonging to a particular person, office or authority, often by virtue of their position.

Think of the following examples:

- The President has certain constitutional prerogatives.
- Parliament has the prerogative to legislate within its constitutional domain.
- It is the prerogative of the judiciary to interpret the Constitution.
- Choosing one's career is an individual's personal prerogative.

The key distinction: **A prerogative is not merely an ordinary right; it is a special entitlement associated with a particular status, office or authority.**

# **1. PREROGATIVE**

## **A. Meaning of the Components**

**Word:** Prerogative\
**Pronunciation:** pri-ROG-uh-tiv\
**Part of speech:** Noun\
**Root origin:** Latin *praerogativa*

Break the word historically:

| Component    | Meaning                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Prae         | Before                         |
| Rogare       | To ask, question or propose    |
| Praerogativa | The right of being asked first |

The Latin expression is connected with the Roman practice of asking a particular voting group to cast its vote first.

## **B. Literal Meaning**

**The privilege of being asked or consulted first.**

In ancient Rome, a particular voting century (centuria praerogativa) was selected to vote before the others. Its vote could influence subsequent voters.

Thus, the idea of being first gradually acquired an association with special status and privilege.

## **C. Semantic Evolution**

**Latin: Praerogativa**

The privilege of being asked or voting first.

**Special precedence**

A position of distinction or priority.

**Special privilege**

A benefit or entitlement associated with status.

**Modern meaning**

A special right, power or authority belonging to a person, office or institution.

## **D. Modern Meaning**

A prerogative refers to:

1. A special right or privilege enjoyed by virtue of one's position.
2. A power vested in a constitutional or public authority.
3. A matter that falls within someone's exclusive discretion or choice.

**Important distinction for UPSC:**

- Right: An entitlement that may be legally or constitutionally guaranteed.
- Privilege: A special advantage or benefit.
- Prerogative: A special power, right or discretion attached to a particular office or status.

In constitutional law, the term may refer to powers exercised by the executive under constitutional or legal arrangements. It should not automatically be understood as an unlimited or arbitrary power.

## **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation           | Meaning                                                     |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Executive prerogative | Special authority or discretion exercised by the executive. |
| Royal prerogative     | Special powers historically vested in the British Crown.    |
| Personal prerogative  | A matter of individual choice or discretion.                |

**UPSC-ready sentences:**

- The exercise of executive prerogative must remain consistent with constitutional limitations.
- The appointment of constitutional functionaries involves powers distributed among different authorities.
- Personal autonomy includes the prerogative to make choices concerning one's life.

**Memory hook:** *Prae = Before; Rogare = Ask → Asked first → Special privilege.*

# **2. THE ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY OF PREROGATIVE**

## **Understanding the common root: ROG / ROGARE**

Before memorising individual words, understand the common Latin root:

# **ROGARE**

## **TO ASK · TO QUESTION · TO PROPOSE**

**The central semantic idea**

Asking someone → Requesting something → Proposing a law → Claiming authority → Repealing or modifying a law.

The prefix determines the direction or nature of the action.

This root is particularly valuable for UPSC because its derivatives frequently appear in constitutional law, parliamentary procedures, governance, judicial processes and administrative vocabulary.

## **A. ABROGATE — To formally abolish or repeal**

**Part of speech:** Verb\
**Pronunciation:** AB-ruh-gayt

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

| Component | Meaning                  |
| --------- | ------------------------ |
| Ab-       | Away, off, away from     |
| Rogare    | To propose, ask or enact |

### **2. Literal Meaning**

To propose away or remove something that has been established through a proposal or law.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *abrogare* referred to repealing or annulling a law. The idea evolved into the formal abolition of an existing law, rule, institution or practice.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

To repeal, annul or formally abolish a law, regulation, agreement, right or established arrangement.

**Synonyms:** Repeal, rescind, annul, revoke, abolish.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation                        | Meaning                                               |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Abrogate a law                     | Formally repeal a law.                                |
| Abrogate an agreement              | Annul an existing agreement.                          |
| Abrogate constitutional provisions | Formally repeal or abolish constitutional provisions. |

**UPSC application:**

- Parliament may amend the Constitution, but constitutional amendments cannot abrogate its basic structure.
- The abrogation of an established legal arrangement may generate significant political and constitutional consequences.

**Memory hook:** AB = Away + ROG = Propose → Propose away → Repeal.

**Related forms:**

- Abrogation (noun): The act of formally abolishing something.
- Abrogative (adjective): Having the character of repealing or annulling; rare.

## **B. ARROGATE — To claim something without justification**

**Part of speech:** Verb\
**Pronunciation:** AIR-uh-gayt

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

| Component                | Meaning                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Ad- (assimilated to ar-) | To, towards, upon oneself |
| Rogare                   | To ask, claim or propose  |

### **2. Literal Meaning**

To claim or appropriate something for oneself.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *arrogare* meant to claim or assume something for oneself. Over time, the word acquired a negative implication: claiming authority, rights or importance that one does not legitimately possess.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

To claim, assume or take upon oneself a right, power or authority without adequate justification or entitlement.

**Synonyms:** Usurp, presume, appropriate, assume.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation         | Meaning                                     |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Arrogate powers     | Illegitimately assume powers.               |
| Arrogate authority  | Claim authority without proper entitlement. |
| Arrogate to oneself | Claim something for oneself.                |

**UPSC application:**

- No constitutional authority should arrogate to itself powers that the Constitution has entrusted to another institution.
- Excessive centralisation may encourage the executive to arrogate discretionary powers at the expense of institutional accountability.

**Memory hook:** AR = Towards oneself + ROG = Claim → Claim for oneself.

**Related forms:**

- Arrogation (noun): The act of claiming something for oneself.
- Arrogant (adjective): Having an exaggerated sense of one's own importance.
- Arrogance (noun): An attitude of excessive self-importance.

**Important distinction:** Arrogating power is not the same as legitimately exercising constitutional authority.

## **C. DEROGATE — To diminish, detract from or impair**

**Part of speech:** Verb\
**Pronunciation:** DERR-uh-gayt

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

| Component | Meaning                      |
| --------- | ---------------------------- |
| De-       | Down, away, from             |
| Rogare    | To propose, enact or request |

### **2. Literal Meaning**

To take away from or diminish something previously established.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *derogare* originally referred to taking away part of a law or modifying its force. Its meaning subsequently expanded to include detracting from the value, dignity or authority of something.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

To diminish the value, authority, importance or dignity of something.

It is particularly useful in legal and constitutional discussions.

**Synonyms:** Detract, diminish, undermine, impair.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation                             | Meaning                                          |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Derogate from constitutional principles | Undermine or diminish constitutional principles. |
| Derogate from one's dignity             | Diminish someone's dignity.                      |
| Derogate from a right                   | Impair or diminish a right.                      |

**UPSC application:**

- No ordinary legislation should derogate from the fundamental constitutional principles governing the rule of law.
- Administrative discretion must not derogate from the dignity and autonomy of citizens.

**Memory hook:** DE = Down + ROG = Established authority → Bring down its value or force.

**Related forms:**

- Derogation (noun): The act of diminishing or departing from something.
- Derogatory (adjective): Showing disrespect or expressing a disparaging attitude.

### **Important distinction: Derogation vs Abrogation**

| Derogation                                    | Abrogation                                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Diminishes or impairs                         | Formally repeals or abolishes                 |
| May be partial                                | Usually involves complete repeal or annulment |
| Can mean detracting from dignity or authority | Primarily concerns formal legal abolition     |

Example:

- Derogation from rights: A restriction or impairment of rights.
- Abrogation of a law: Formal repeal of that law.

## **D. PROROGUE — To formally end a parliamentary session**

**Part of speech:** Verb\
**Pronunciation:** proh-ROHG

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

| Component | Meaning                         |
| --------- | ------------------------------- |
| Pro-      | Forward, onward or for a period |
| Rogare    | To propose or enact             |

### **2. Literal Meaning**

To extend or carry something forward.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *prorogare* meant to prolong or extend something. Its parliamentary meaning developed around the formal continuation or postponement of legislative proceedings, eventually becoming associated with the formal termination of a parliamentary session.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

To formally discontinue a session of Parliament or a legislative body.

**Synonyms:** Formally end a session, adjourn (not an exact synonym), terminate a session.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation               | Meaning                                            |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Prorogue Parliament       | Formally end a parliamentary session.              |
| Prorogue the House        | Formally terminate a legislative session.          |
| Prorogation of Parliament | The formal termination of a parliamentary session. |

**UPSC application:**

- In India, the President has the constitutional power to prorogue either House of Parliament under Article 85.
- Prorogation is distinct from dissolution, as prorogation does not dissolve the Lok Sabha.

**Memory hook:** PRO = Forward + ROG = Proceed → Carry proceedings forward; in parliamentary usage, formally end the session.

**Related forms:**

- Prorogation (noun): The formal termination of a parliamentary session.
- Prorogative is not a derivative of prorogue in modern usage, despite their shared historical root.

### **UPSC Constitutional Distinction**

| Term                 | Meaning                                                                     |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adjournment          | Suspension of a sitting for a specified period.                             |
| Adjournment sine die | Termination of a sitting without specifying the date of the next meeting.   |
| Prorogation          | Formal termination of a session by the President.                           |
| Dissolution          | Formal ending of the Lok Sabha's existence, necessitating a fresh election. |

## **E. INTERROGATE — To question systematically**

**Part of speech:** Verb\
**Pronunciation:** in-TERR-uh-gayt

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

| Component | Meaning            |
| --------- | ------------------ |
| Inter-    | Between, among     |
| Rogare    | To ask or question |

### **2. Literal Meaning**

To ask questions or question someone systematically.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *interrogare* meant to question or inquire. It developed into the modern idea of systematic questioning, particularly in legal, investigative and research contexts.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

To question someone formally, systematically or intensively to obtain information, establish facts or examine a claim.

**Synonyms:** Question, examine, cross-examine, inquire.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation             | Meaning                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Interrogate a suspect   | Question a suspect during an investigation. |
| Interrogate evidence    | Examine evidence critically.                |
| Interrogate assumptions | Challenge underlying assumptions.           |

**UPSC application:**

- Democratic institutions must interrogate the exercise of executive power through mechanisms of accountability.
- A researcher must interrogate prevailing assumptions before accepting a theoretical explanation.

**Memory hook:** INTER = Between + ROG = Ask → Ask questions to extract information.

**Related forms:**

- Interrogation (noun): Systematic questioning.
- Interrogative (adjective/noun): Relating to questions; a question form.
- Interrogatory (noun/adjective): A formal question, particularly in legal proceedings; relating to such questions.

### **UPSC application of related forms**

| Word          | Example                                                          |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Interrogation | Police interrogation must comply with constitutional safeguards. |
| Interrogative | Interrogative sentences are used to seek information.            |
| Interrogatory | Written interrogatories may be used in civil proceedings.        |

## **F. SURROGATE — A substitute or replacement**

**Part of speech:** Noun / Adjective\
**Pronunciation:** SUR-uh-git

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

| Component                                            | Meaning               |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Sub- (historically assimilated in Latin *surrogare*) | Under, in place of    |
| Rogare                                               | To appoint or propose |

### **2. Literal Meaning**

To appoint someone or something in place of another.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *surrogare* meant to substitute or appoint another person in someone's place. The idea developed into the modern concept of replacement or representation.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

A person, institution or thing that acts as a substitute for another.

**Synonyms:** Substitute, replacement, proxy, alternate.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation              | Meaning                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Surrogate decision-maker | A person authorised to make decisions on behalf of another. |
| Surrogate institution    | An institution performing the function of another.          |
| Surrogate representation | Representation through a substitute or proxy.               |

**UPSC application:**

- Civil society organisations sometimes act as surrogate channels of representation for marginalised communities.
- In situations where individuals cannot make decisions themselves, legal frameworks may recognise surrogate decision-makers.

**Memory hook:** SURROGATE = Substitute → Someone appointed in another person's place.

**Related forms:**

- Surrogacy (noun): The practice or arrangement of acting as a substitute, particularly in reproduction.
- Surrogate (adjective): Serving as a substitute.

**Important note:** The word is historically connected to Latin *subrogare*, a compound of *sub* and *rogare*.

## **G. SUBROGATION — Substitution of one party's legal rights by another**

**Part of speech:** Noun\
**Pronunciation:** sub-roh-GAY-shun

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

| Component | Meaning               |
| --------- | --------------------- |
| Sub-      | Under, in place of    |
| Rogare    | To appoint or propose |
| -ation    | Process or action     |

### **2. Literal Meaning**

The process of putting one person in the place of another.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

From the Latin concept of *subrogare*, meaning to substitute or appoint in another's place, the term developed a specialised legal meaning involving the substitution of one party into another's legal position.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

A legal principle under which one person or entity acquires the rights or remedies of another, usually after satisfying that person's claim or obligation.

It is especially important in insurance law.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation             | Meaning                                                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Doctrine of subrogation | Legal principle of substitution of rights.                                                |
| Right of subrogation    | Right to exercise another party's legal remedies.                                         |
| Insurance subrogation   | Insurer's right to recover from a responsible third party after compensating the insured. |

**UPSC application:**

- The doctrine of subrogation prevents unjust enrichment and enables an insurer to recover compensation from a third party responsible for a loss.
- Subrogation illustrates how legal rights and remedies can be transferred through established legal principles.

**Memory hook:** SUB = In place of + ROG = Appoint → One party takes another's legal position.

**Part of speech:** Noun\
**Pronunciation:** roh-GAY-shun

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

### **2. Literal Meaning**

The act of asking, requesting or proposing something.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

In Roman usage, a *rogatio* could refer to a formal proposal submitted for approval, particularly a legislative proposal. The word survives in specialised religious, historical and legal contexts.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

A formal request, petition or proposal; also a specialised historical or religious term.

### **5. Common Usage**

| Collocation          | Meaning                                                                          |

**UPSC application:**

- The historical Roman concept of rogation illustrates the evolution of participatory lawmaking.
- The study of rogation provides insight into the relationship between religion, agriculture and community life in European history.

**Memory hook:** ROG = Request → Rogation = Formal request.

### **2. Literal Meaning**

Relating to a request or formal inquiry.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin root *rogare* gave rise to terminology associated with formal requests. In legal usage, *rogatory* became associated with judicial requests made to another court or authority for assistance.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

Relating to a formal judicial request, especially one made by a court in one jurisdiction to a court in another jurisdiction.

**Memory hook:** ROG = Request → Rogatory = Relating to a formal request.

## **6. Important Constitutional Connections**

Three words deserve special attention for Indian Polity.

**1. Prerogative — Article 85**

The President's power to summon and prorogue Parliament is constitutionally provided. It is exercised within India's parliamentary and constitutional framework, not as an unrestricted personal discretion.

**2. Prorogue — Article 85**

Article 85(2)(a) empowers the President to prorogue either House of Parliament.

**3. Abrogate — Article 368**

The constitutional amendment power does not authorise Parliament to destroy the Constitution's basic structure. The basic structure doctrine, established in *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)*, limits the amending power.

Break the word into three meaningful components:

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

**Guyot** is an eponymous geographical term. Unlike words formed from Latin or Greek roots, it does not have two independently meaningful linguistic components.

- **Guyot** — derived from the surname of Arnold Henry Guyot (1807–1884), a Swiss-American geographer and geologist.
- The geological term was introduced in honour of Guyot by the American geologist Harry Hammond Hess.

**Important distinction:** Guyot is not a compound word. Its origin is commemorative rather than descriptive.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

A submarine mountain named after Guyot.

The name does not literally mean flat-topped mountain, although that is the geological feature it subsequently came to designate.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word underwent the following transition:

1. **Personal name:** Guyot referred to Arnold Henry Guyot.
2. **Scientific naming:** The surname was adopted in geological terminology.
3. **Geomorphological classification:** It came to denote a distinctive underwater volcanic mountain with a flat summit.
4. **Modern scientific usage:** It became an established term in marine geology and ocean-floor geomorphology.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Guyot (noun):** An isolated submarine volcanic mountain with a characteristically flat summit, generally located at considerable depth below sea level.

A guyot is essentially a flat-topped seamount.

**Etymological roots:**

- Greek: *abyssos* — bottomless, unfathomable.
- *A-* — without (in the historical Greek formation).
- *Byssos* — depth (in the historical formation of the word).

The Greek *abyssos* literally conveyed the idea of something without a bottom.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Abyssal = Pertaining to a bottomless depth.**

Imagine looking into an ocean trench whose bottom is beyond ordinary human perception.

The adjective captures this extraordinary depth.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word underwent the following conceptual progression:

**Stage 1: Physical depth**

An immeasurably deep gulf or chasm.

**Stage 2: Oceanographic meaning**

The deepest regions of the ocean.

**Stage 3: Figurative association**

Profound, extreme or seemingly immeasurable conditions.

An important scientific development was its association with the **abyssal zone**, the deep-ocean region generally located at approximately 3,000–6,000 metres.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Abyssal** has two principal applications:

1. **Scientific meaning:** Relating to the deep ocean, particularly the abyssal zone.
2. **Figurative meaning:** Profoundly deep, extreme or immeasurable.

*Important distinction:* In everyday English, *abyssal* is not simply a sophisticated synonym for *deep*. It often carries an implication of extraordinary depth or extremity.

# **PART I — THERMOHALINE**

## **1. Meaning of the Components**

The word **Thermohaline** is formed by combining two Greek-derived elements:

| Component | Origin                           | Meaning           |
| --------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Thermo-   | Greek *thermos*                  | Heat, temperature |
| Haline    | Greek *hals* (genitive: *halos*) | Salt, saline      |

**Root formula:**

\\[ \text{THERMO (Heat)}+\text{HALINE (Salt)} \\]

## **2. Literal Meaning**

Thermohaline literally means **related to heat and salt** or, more precisely, related to temperature and salinity.

In oceanography, these two variables influence the density of seawater.

- Temperature generally decreases → seawater density increases.
- Salinity increases → seawater density increases.

Differences in seawater density contribute to large-scale ocean circulation.

## **3. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed through the combination of two scientific concepts:

1. **Thermal properties:** Scientists recognised that temperature influences seawater density.
2. **Saline properties:** They also recognised that dissolved salts influence seawater density.
3. **Combined understanding:** Ocean circulation was understood to be influenced by the interaction of temperature and salinity.

Thus, thermohaline emerged as a scientific descriptor for processes involving both temperature and salinity.

## **4. Modern Meaning**

**Thermohaline** is an adjective referring to oceanic processes governed by variations in seawater temperature and salinity, particularly their combined influence on seawater density.

Its most important application is **thermohaline circulation**, a component of global ocean circulation involving the movement of water masses due to density differences.

# **PART IV — THE HALINE FAMILY**

## **ROOT 2: HAL- / HALO- — Salt**

**Core idea:** Anything associated with salt or saline conditions.

The Greek word *hals* means salt or sea. Its genitive form is *halos*, which explains the appearance of the combining form *halo-* in scientific vocabulary.

An important distinction:

- **Haline** = Relating to salt or salinity.
- **Halo-** = A combining form used in scientific terminology, often relating to salt.
- **Salinity** = Salt concentration, derived from Latin *sal*, meaning salt.

The English word *salt* and the Latin-derived *saline* belong to a different etymological family from Greek *hal-*.

# **PART VIII — RETENTION TECHNIQUE**

## **1. The root-decoding method**

Whenever you encounter an unfamiliar scientific word, break it into components.

For example:

**Thermohaline**

- Thermo → Heat.
- Haline → Salt.
- Combined meaning → Heat and salt-related.
- Scientific application → Ocean density and circulation.

**Halophyte**

- Halo → Salt.
- Phyte → Plant.
- Combined meaning → Salt plant.
- Scientific application → Plants adapted to saline environments.

**Geothermal**

- Geo → Earth.
- Thermal → Heat.
- Combined meaning → Earth's heat.
- Scientific application → Geothermal energy.

## **3. Common etymological traps**

These distinctions are important because similar-looking words do not necessarily share the same origin.

| Words                               | Distinction                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Halo (salt) vs Halo (ring of light) | Different etymological histories; do not assume the optical halo belongs to the salt family |
| Haline vs Saline                    | Both mean salt-related, but have different roots                                            |
| Thermocline vs Thermohaline         | The former refers to temperature variation; the latter combines temperature and salinity    |
| Halophyte vs Halophile              | Halophyte is a salt-tolerant plant; halophile is a salt-loving organism                     |
| Thermosphere vs Stratosphere        | Different atmospheric layers, named using different concepts                                |

## **Final takeaway: What should you remember?**

Three roots unlock a substantial vocabulary network:

1. **THERMO = HEAT** → Thermodynamics, thermocline, thermosphere, geothermal, thermoregulation.
2. **HAL / HALO = SALT** → Haline, halite, halogen, halophyte, halophile, halocline.

**The ultimate learning principle:** Do not memorise *thermohaline*, *thermocline* and *halocline* as three unrelated geographical terms. Understand their roots, and their meanings become almost self-explanatory.
