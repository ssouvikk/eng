1. DIASTROPHIC
Pronunciation: /ˌdaɪ.əˈstrɒf.ɪk/
Part of Speech: Adjective
Root: Greek diastrophē (distortion, twisting)
Core Idea: A process or force that bends, twists, deforms or structurally alters something.
A. Meaning of the Components
Component	Origin	Meaning
Dia-	Greek dia	Apart, across, through, or in different directions
'-stroph-	Greek strephein	To turn, twist or bend
'-ic	Greek/Latin-derived suffix	Pertaining to; relating to

Important root: STROPH = TURN / TWIST / BEND.
B. Literal Meaning
Diastrophic = Pertaining to turning or twisting something out of its original form.

Think of the Earth's crust being slowly bent, folded, tilted or fractured by immense internal forces.
C. Semantic Evolution
The evolution of the word can be understood through three stages:
1. Original Greek sense: Diastrophē referred to distortion, displacement or a turning away from the normal position.
2. Scientific development: The concept acquired a geological application to describe deformation of the Earth's crust.
3. Modern geological usage: It refers to processes associated with large-scale deformation of the Earth's crust due to tectonic forces.
D. Modern Meaning
Diastrophic (Adjective): Relating to the deformation of the Earth's crust through tectonic movements, including folding, faulting, warping and related structural changes.

Its associated noun is Diastrophism.

Conceptual distinction:
- Diastrophic movements: Deformation of the Earth's crust.
- Orogenic movements: Mountain-building movements, generally involving horizontal compression and deformation.
- Epeirogenic movements: Broad, predominantly vertical uplift or subsidence of continental regions.
Orogenic and epeirogenic movements are commonly discussed as major categories of diastrophic movements in traditional geomorphology.
E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant Collocations)
1. Diastrophic movements – Tectonic movements responsible for deforming the Earth's crust.
2. Diastrophic forces – Internal forces that cause folding, faulting and crustal deformation.
3. Diastrophic deformation – Structural alteration of the Earth's crust due to tectonic activity.

C. Semantic Evolution

3. Apostrophe in English punctuation.

Here, Justice is directly addressed as though it were a person.

## 1. GEOMORPHOLOGY

Pronunciation: jee-oh-mor-FOL-uh-jee\
Part of Speech: Noun\
Disciplinary Context: Physical Geography / Earth Sciences

### A. Meaning of the Components

The word Geomorphology is constructed from three Greek elements:

| Component | Origin       | Meaning                                  |
| --------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Geo       | Greek gē     | Earth                                    |
| Morph     | Greek morphē | Form, shape or structure                 |
| -logy     | Greek logia  | Study, discourse or systematic knowledge |

Important root: MORPH = FORM / SHAPE

### B. Literal Meaning

Geomorphology = Study of the forms or shapes of the Earth.

In simple terms, it is the scientific study of Earth's landforms, their origin, evolution, distribution and the processes responsible for their development.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The word developed through the combination of Greek scientific vocabulary:

1. Geo: Established the subject matter as Earth.
2. Morph: Introduced the idea of physical shape and configuration.
3. -logy: Converted the concept into a systematic field of scientific study.

The discipline subsequently expanded beyond merely describing landforms to explaining their genesis, evolution and transformation through time.

Thus, modern geomorphology is not simply the study of mountains, valleys and plateaus; it investigates the dynamic interaction between geological structures, tectonic forces, climate, water, wind, glaciers, gravity and human activities.

### D. Modern Meaning

Geomorphology is the scientific study of Earth's landforms and the processes, materials and historical developments responsible for their formation and modification.

It examines:

- Endogenic processes: Folding, faulting, volcanism and tectonic uplift.
- Exogenic processes: Weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition.
- Landform evolution: Mountains, plateaus, plains, valleys, deltas and coastal features.
- Human-induced geomorphic changes: Mining, deforestation, urbanisation and river regulation.

### E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Structural geomorphology – Study of the relationship between geological structures and landforms.
2. Fluvial geomorphology – Study of landforms created by rivers and running water.
3. Applied geomorphology – Application of geomorphological knowledge to environmental management, infrastructure planning and disaster mitigation.

UPSC application:

"An understanding of Himalayan geomorphology is essential for assessing landslide susceptibility, river dynamics and the vulnerability of mountain infrastructure."

Meaning of the Components

Semantic Evolution: The Greek word metamorphōsis referred to transformation in form. It acquired biological significance through the transformation of organisms during development.

Literal Meaning: Relating to a change in form.

Semantic Evolution: The term acquired specialised geological meaning through the study of rocks transformed by heat, pressure and chemically active fluids.

Modern Meaning: Relating to transformation or change in physical structure.

Common Usage:

Semantic Evolution: Developed into a geological term describing the transformation of pre-existing rocks through changes in physical and chemical conditions.

### A. Meaning of the Components

The word orogenic consists of two Greek-derived elements:

| Component | Origin                          | Meaning                                    |
| --------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Oro-      | Greek oros                      | Mountain                                   |
| -genic    | Greek genēs, related to genesis | Born of, produced by, or causing formation |

Important root distinction:

- Oros = mountain.
- Genesis = origin, birth or creation.
- -genic = producing, generating or relating to formation.

### B. Literal Meaning

Orogenic = Mountain-forming.

Something associated with the formation, development or structural deformation of mountains.

### C. Semantic Evolution

1. Greek foundation: Oros referred to a mountain, while genesis conveyed birth or origin.
2. Scientific development: Geology adopted orogeny to describe mountain-building processes.
3. Modern geological usage: Orogenic came to describe processes, movements, structures and events associated with mountain formation, particularly tectonic deformation, folding, faulting and crustal thickening.

The scientific concept is broader than simply pushing land upwards: orogeny can involve compression, folding, thrust faulting, metamorphism, magmatism and uplift.

### D. Modern Meaning

Orogenic (adjective): Relating to or responsible for the formation of mountain ranges through tectonic processes, particularly the deformation and thickening of the Earth's crust.

Conceptual example: The collision of the Indian Plate with the Eurasian Plate initiated the orogenic processes responsible for the formation of the Himalayas.

### E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Orogenic movements – Tectonic movements responsible for mountain-building.
2. Orogenic belt – An elongated geological zone characterised by mountain-building deformation.
3. Orogenic processes – Geological processes such as folding, faulting, metamorphism and crustal thickening involved in mountain formation.

| Parameter         | Orogenic                                                             | Epeirogenic                                                  |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Nature            | Mountain-building deformation                                        | Broad regional crustal movement                              |

### The Three-Question Memory Technique

Whenever you encounter a word beginning with ORO-, ask:

1. Does it end in -geny or -genesis? Think of mountain formation.
2. Does it end in -genic or -genetic? Think of something related to mountain formation.
3. Does it end in -graphy or -graphic? Think of mountain description, configuration or topographical influence.

This approach allows you to decode unfamiliar scientific vocabulary instead of memorising every word independently.

### A. Meaning of the Components

- Scarp: A steep slope or cliff, particularly one marking an abrupt change in elevation.
- It is not a modern combination of two independently meaningful English components. It is a borrowed word with a historical European etymology.

Etymological origin:

Italian scarpa = a slope or declivity.

The word entered English through French military and architectural vocabulary, particularly in connection with defensive earthworks.

### B. Literal Meaning

A steeply inclined surface or slope.

Originally, the term had a particularly strong association with the sloping sides of defensive fortifications.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The word developed through three broad stages:

1. Military engineering: The inner slope of a defensive ditch or the exterior slope of a fortification.
2. Fortification terminology: A steep, deliberately constructed defensive slope.
3. Physical geography: A steep natural slope, particularly one separating two relatively different elevations.

The transition is from a constructed defensive slope to a naturally occurring geological landform.

### D. Modern Meaning

Scarp (noun):

A steep slope or cliff, especially one produced by faulting, erosion or differential geological processes.

Scarp (verb):

To form or create a steep slope or escarpment.

### E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Fault scarp – A steep slope created by displacement along a geological fault.
2. Scarp slope – The relatively steep face of a geological landform.
3. Erosional scarp – A steep slope produced by erosional processes.

UPSC application:

- GS Paper I: Geomorphology, tectonic movements, landforms and slope development.
- Geography Optional: Structural landforms, faulting, erosion and escarpment formation.
- Disaster Management: Scarps associated with earthquakes and landslides.

Example sentence:

"The formation of a fault scarp provides geomorphological evidence of tectonic displacement along an active fault."

## 1. GEYSER

Pronunciation: /ˈɡaɪzər/ (GY-zer)\
Part of speech: Noun\
Origin: Icelandic

### A. Meaning of the Components

- Geysa (Icelandic): To gush, rush forth or burst out.
- -er: A suffix indicating an agent, instrument or thing that performs an action.

The word comes from the Icelandic verb geysa, meaning to gush or erupt. The famous Icelandic spring Geysir, whose name derives from this verb, gave English the word geyser.

### B. Literal Meaning

Something that gushes or bursts forth.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The semantic journey can be understood as:

Geysa (to gush) → Geysir (the Icelandic erupting hot spring) → Geyser (an intermittent hot-water spring that ejects water and steam under pressure).

The word originally became associated with a particular natural phenomenon in Iceland and subsequently entered English as a general geological term.

### D. Modern Meaning

Geyser (noun): A natural hot spring that periodically ejects hot water and steam into the air due to the accumulation and release of pressure in underground geothermal systems.

Example: The geothermal activity of Iceland is responsible for its numerous geysers and hot springs.

Conceptual distinction:

- Hot spring: Water naturally heated underground that reaches the surface.
- Geyser: A special type of hot spring that periodically erupts.
- Volcano: An opening through which magma, gases and volcanic material may reach the surface.

### E. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Geothermal activity — Heat-related processes occurring within the Earth.
2. Geyser eruption — The periodic expulsion of hot water and steam.
3. Geothermal energy — Energy derived from the Earth's internal heat.

UPSC application: Geysers are important examples of geothermal processes and demonstrate the relationship between Earth's internal heat, groundwater circulation and surface geological phenomena.

> Important etymological correction: Geyser should not be divided into geysa + -er. It is a borrowing of Icelandic Geysir, the proper name of a particular hot spring. The name itself means approximately the gusher, from geysa (to gush). The apparent English suffix -er is not a separately added suffix in its formation.&#x20;

# 2. THE ETYMOLOGICAL ROOT: GHEU-

Master root for retention

Proto-Indo-European (PIE) root: gheu-\
Core meaning: To pour, pour out or cause a liquid to flow.

This ancient root developed into several word families through different Indo-European languages.

The Latin pathway is particularly productive in English and gives us several high-value words used in governance, sociology, economics, science, geography and international relations.

- Pouring outward → Diffusion
- Pouring into → Infusion
