# ADDENDUM

WORD POWER • ETYMOLOGY • UPSC VOCABULARY

Pronunciation: /əˈdendəm/\
Part of Speech: Noun\
Plural: Addenda (Latin plural); addendums (also accepted English plural)

## 1. Meaning of the Components

The word addendum comes from Latin:

| Component | Meaning                                                           |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ad-       | To, towards, in addition                                          |
| dare      | To give                                                           |
| addere    | To add, put or give something extra                               |
| -endum    | A Latin gerundive ending, expressing something that is to be done |

Root idea: Something that is to be added.

## 2. Literal Meaning

Addendum literally means something that must be added or an additional item to be supplied.

It derives from the Latin expression addendum, the neuter singular gerundive of addere (to add).

Think of it as an additional piece of information attached to an already completed document.

## 3. Semantic Evolution

The semantic development can be understood through four stages:

1. Latin origin: Something that ought to be added.
2. Documentary usage: An additional passage or item attached to an existing written work.
3. Legal and administrative usage: Supplementary information added to an existing agreement, report, contract or official document.
4. Modern usage: An additional section that supplements, corrects or updates an already-issued document without necessarily replacing the original.

Conceptual progression:

ADDERE

To add something

ADDENDUM

Something to be added

SUPPLEMENTARY DOCUMENT

Additional information attached to an existing document

## 4. Modern Meaning

Addendum (noun): An additional section, note or document appended to an existing document to provide supplementary information, clarification, correction or an update.

An addendum generally supplements the original document rather than replacing it.

Examples:

- A government committee may issue an addendum to its original report to incorporate newly available data.
- A contract may contain an addendum specifying additional obligations of the parties.
- An official notification may be accompanied by an addendum clarifying certain provisions.

Important distinction:

| Term        | Meaning                                                    |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Addendum    | An additional section attached to an existing document     |
| Appendix    | Supplementary material placed at the end of a document     |
| Amendment   | A formal change or modification to an existing document    |
| Corrigendum | An official correction of an error in a published document |
| Erratum     | A specific error identified in a published work            |

## 5. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Addendum to a report – Supplementary information attached to an already-published report.
2. Contractual addendum – An additional document supplementing an existing contract.
3. Addendum to an official notification – Additional information or clarification accompanying an official notification.

UPSC-style sentence:

"The committee issued an addendum to its original report to incorporate updated demographic data and address certain methodological limitations."

# PART II — ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY

## The ADD / ADDERE Family

High-value vocabulary cluster

The central Latin root is:

ADDERE = AD- (to) + DARE (to give)

Its fundamental idea is to put something extra, attach, contribute or increase.

This root gives us several useful English words associated with governance, public administration, economics, law, constitutional affairs and social policy.

Note: The following words are members of the same Latin root family, but not all have identical grammatical or semantic development. The distinction matters for accurate etymology.

## 1. ADD

Pronunciation: /æd/\
Part of Speech: Verb

### Meaning of the Components

- Ad- = To, towards.
- Dare = To give.

### Literal Meaning

To give or put something towards something else.

### Semantic Evolution

Originally associated with putting something together or joining one thing to another; it developed into the general idea of increasing quantity, combining elements or contributing information.

### Modern Meaning

To join, combine, include or increase something by putting another thing with it.

### Common Usage

1. Add value – Increase the usefulness or worth of something.
2. Add to economic growth – Contribute towards economic expansion.
3. Add a provision – Introduce an additional legal or administrative provision.

UPSC application:

"Investment in human capital adds value to economic growth by improving labour productivity."

## 2. ADDITION

Pronunciation: /əˈdɪʃən/\
Part of Speech: Noun

### Meaning of the Components

- Addere = To add.
- -tion = An action, process or result.

### Literal Meaning

The act or process of adding something.

### Semantic Evolution

The mathematical meaning of combining quantities gradually expanded into the general sense of introducing an extra element into an existing system, structure or body of knowledge.

### Modern Meaning

The act of adding something; an extra element or an increase in an existing quantity.

### Common Usage

1. In addition to – Along with something else.
2. Addition of new provisions – Introduction of further legal provisions.
3. Value addition – Increasing the economic value of a product through processing or other improvements.

UPSC application:

"In addition to economic redistribution, inclusive development requires the expansion of quality education and healthcare."

## 3. ADDITIONAL

Pronunciation: /əˈdɪʃənəl/\
Part of Speech: Adjective

### Meaning of the Components

- Addition = The act or result of adding.
- -al = Relating to or characterised by.

### Literal Meaning

Relating to something that has been added.

### Semantic Evolution

The noun addition developed into an adjective describing anything that exists beyond the original amount, requirement or arrangement.

### Modern Meaning

Extra, supplementary or more than what already exists or is required.

### Common Usage

1. Additional financial assistance – Financial support beyond the existing allocation.
2. Additional safeguards – Extra protective measures.
3. Additional responsibilities – Further duties imposed on an individual or institution.

UPSC application:

"Additional safeguards are necessary to prevent the misuse of discretionary powers by public authorities."

## 4. ADDITIVE

Pronunciation: /ˈædɪtɪv/\
Part of Speech: Noun / Adjective

### Meaning of the Components

- Addere = To add.
- -ive = Having the nature or function of.

### Literal Meaning

Something that has the function or property of adding.

### Semantic Evolution

The word developed from the general idea of adding something to another substance, particularly in scientific, industrial and food-related contexts.

### Modern Meaning

- As a noun: A substance added to another material to improve or modify its properties.
- As an adjective: Relating to the process of addition or cumulative increase.

### Common Usage

1. Food additives – Substances added to food for preservation, flavour or other functions.
2. Additive manufacturing – A manufacturing process in which material is added layer by layer, as in 3D printing.
3. Additive effect – A combined effect resulting from the addition of individual influences.

UPSC application:

"Additive manufacturing has the potential to strengthen India's advanced manufacturing ecosystem and reduce dependence on certain imported components."

## 5. ADDEND

Pronunciation: /ˈædend/\
Part of Speech: Noun

### Meaning of the Components

- Addere = To add.
- -end = From the Latin gerundive formation, indicating something to be added.

### Literal Meaning

A quantity that is to be added to another quantity.

### Semantic Evolution

The term acquired a specialised mathematical meaning to describe individual numbers being combined through addition.

### Modern Meaning

A number or quantity that is added to another number or quantity.

### Common Usage

1. Sum of two addends – The result of adding two quantities.
2. Multiple addends – Several quantities combined through addition.
3. Missing addend – An unknown quantity in an addition problem.

UPSC relevance: Primarily mathematical and analytical rather than a high-frequency GS vocabulary word.

## 6. ADDENDA

Pronunciation: /əˈdendə/\
Part of Speech: Noun (plural)

### Meaning of the Components

- Addendum = Something to be added.
- -a = Latin neuter plural ending.

### Literal Meaning

Things that are to be added.

### Semantic Evolution

The plural form of addendum became established in English for referring to multiple supplementary items or sections attached to an existing document.

### Modern Meaning

Additional materials, sections or documents appended to an existing publication or record.

### Common Usage

1. Addenda to the report – Multiple supplementary sections to a report.
2. Technical addenda – Additional technical information.
3. Contractual addenda – Multiple supplementary contractual documents.

UPSC application:

"The ministry released several addenda to the original guidelines to clarify implementation procedures."

## 7. REDOUBT — A RELATED WORD? NO.

A useful etymological caution: words that look or sound similar are not necessarily members of the same root family.

For example, redouble has a different formation, originating from Latin duplus (double), through French. It should not be memorised as a derivative of addere.

Similarly, adduce is not a derivative of addere despite its apparent resemblance.

This distinction helps avoid false etymological connections.

# CENTRIPETAL

Pronunciation: sen-TRIP-uh-tl | Adjective

Core idea: Moving towards a centre; drawing things inward.

## 1. Meaning of the Components

Break the word into its historical components:

| Component | Origin        | Meaning                          |
| --------- | ------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Centr(i)- | Latin centrum | Centre                           |
| -petal    | Latin petere  | To seek, aim at, or move towards |

The suffix -al converts the formation into an adjective.

Memory association:

- CENTR = Centre
- PET = Seek or move towards
- CENTRIPETAL = Seeking or moving towards the centre.

## 2. Literal Meaning

Centripetal = Centre-seeking.

In physics, centripetal force is the force directed towards the centre of a circular path, which keeps an object moving along that path.

For example:

- Gravitational force provides centripetal force for a satellite in orbit.
- Tension in a string provides centripetal force when a stone is whirled in a circle.

Remember: Centripetal force describes the inward direction of the net force; it is not necessarily a separate physical force.

## 3. Semantic Evolution

The word developed from the physical idea of seeking or moving towards a centre into a broader metaphorical idea.

PHYSICAL MEANING

Movement towards the centre of a circle.

FUNCTIONAL MEANING

A force or influence that pulls different elements together.

POLITICAL AND SOCIAL MEANING

Forces that promote centralisation, national integration and unity.

Thus, the word acquired an important metaphorical application in political science, sociology, geography and governance.

## 4. Modern Meaning

Centripetal (adjective): Characterising a force, tendency, influence or process that draws different elements towards a common centre, thereby promoting concentration, unity or integration.

Its meaning depends on context:

| Discipline        | Meaning                                                                  |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Physics           | Directed towards the centre of circular motion                           |
| Political Science | Forces promoting national unity and centralisation                       |
| Sociology         | Forces binding individuals or groups together                            |
| Geography         | Processes concentrating population or economic activity towards a centre |
| Governance        | Institutional mechanisms strengthening central coordination              |

UPSC conceptual application:

India experiences both centripetal and centrifugal forces.

- Centripetal forces: Constitutional unity, common citizenship, national institutions and economic integration.
- Centrifugal forces: Regionalism, linguistic tensions, separatism and subnational identities.

A healthy federal democracy balances both forces rather than eliminating regional diversity.

## 5. Common Usage: High-Frequency Collocations

| Collocation                  | Meaning                                                |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Centripetal forces           | Forces promoting unity or integration                  |
| Centripetal tendency         | A tendency towards concentration or centralisation     |
| Centripetal political forces | Political influences that strengthen national cohesion |

UPSC-ready sentence:

"Constitutional morality, democratic institutions and a shared national identity act as centripetal forces, strengthening India's unity while accommodating its pluralistic character."

# WORD POWER MADE EASY: CENTRIFUGAL

ETYMOLOGY • ROOT-BASED VOCABULARY • UPSC APPLICATION

The most effective way to remember centrifugal is not to memorise its dictionary definition but to understand the two ideas hidden inside it:

- CENTR- = Centre
- FUG- / FUGERE = To flee, escape or run away

Once you understand the root FUGERE, you can unlock an entire family of English words associated with escape, flight, avoidance, displacement and dispersal.

# 1. CENTRIFUGAL

**Pronunciation:** sen-TRIF-yoo-guhl\
**Part of Speech:** Adjective\
**Etymology:** Latin *centrum* (centre) + *fugere* (to flee)

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **CENTR-** → Centre, central point.
- **FUG- / FUGERE** → To flee, escape or move away.

### B. Literal Meaning

Moving or tending to flee away from the centre.

Imagine a stone tied to a string and whirled around in a circle. The tendency of the stone to move outward, away from the centre of rotation, is associated with centrifugal force.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The original idea of fleeing away from a centre developed into two important meanings:

1. **Physical meaning:** Moving or tending outward from a central point.
2. **Figurative meaning:** Moving away from a central authority, institution, ideology or organising principle.

This figurative extension makes the word particularly important in political science, sociology and governance.

### D. Modern Meaning

**Centrifugal** refers to something that moves, acts or tends to move away from a centre.

It can describe:

- Physical forces or outward movement.
- Political forces weakening national unity.
- Social, cultural or economic tendencies producing fragmentation.
- Institutional forces encouraging decentralisation or disintegration.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Centrifugal forces:** Forces that weaken national integration or pull a political system apart.
2. **Centrifugal tendencies:** Trends towards regionalism, separatism or fragmentation.
3. **Centrifugal political forces:** Regional, ethnic or ideological pressures challenging central authority.

**UPSC Application:**

"India's federal structure must accommodate regional aspirations while preventing centrifugal tendencies from undermining national integration."

**Important conceptual distinction:**

Centrifugal does not automatically mean negative. Decentralisation and regional autonomy can strengthen democracy, even though they involve movement away from centralised authority.

# 2. ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY: FUGERE

**Root: FUGERE (Latin) = To flee, escape or run away**

The following words share this historical root. Their meanings have evolved into different conceptual domains, but the underlying idea of flight or escape remains identifiable.

## WORD 1: FUGITIVE

**Pronunciation:** FYOO-juh-tiv\
**Part of Speech:** Noun / Adjective\
**Etymology:** Latin *fugitivus*, from *fugere* (to flee).

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **FUG-** → To flee or escape.
- **-ITIVE** → An adjectival or noun-forming element associated with a tendency or characteristic.

### B. Literal Meaning

A person who flees or escapes.

### C. Semantic Evolution

Initially referred to someone escaping from captivity, slavery or legal custody.

Its meaning subsequently expanded to include anyone escaping justice, avoiding arrest or remaining beyond the reach of law.

It also acquired an adjectival meaning: temporary, fleeting or difficult to retain.

### D. Modern Meaning

**As a noun:** A person who has escaped from custody or is fleeing from justice.

**As an adjective:** Something fleeting, temporary or short-lived.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Fugitive economic offender:** An economic offender who leaves the country to avoid criminal prosecution or faces the statutory conditions specified under the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018.
2. **Fugitive from justice:** A person evading legal proceedings or arrest.
3. **Fugitive emissions:** Unintentional releases of gases or pollutants into the atmosphere.

**UPSC Application:**

"The Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018 seeks to deter economic offenders from evading Indian law by remaining outside the country."

## WORD 2: REFUGE

**Pronunciation:** REF-yooj\
**Part of Speech:** Noun / Verb\
**Etymology:** Latin *refugium*, from *re-* (back) + *fugere* (to flee).

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **RE-** → Back, again or away.
- **FUG-** → To flee or escape.

### B. Literal Meaning

A place to flee back to or a place of escape.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The original sense of fleeing for safety developed into the idea of a protected place where someone could escape danger, persecution or hardship.

It eventually acquired broader meanings involving protection, security and relief from difficult circumstances.

### D. Modern Meaning

A safe place, protection or shelter from danger, persecution or difficulty.

As a verb, it can mean to take shelter or seek protection.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Seek refuge:** Escape to a place of safety.
2. **Political refuge:** Protection sought by individuals facing political persecution.
3. **Refuge for displaced populations:** Safe shelter for people forced to leave their homes.

**UPSC Application:**

"India's historical tradition of providing refuge to persecuted communities reflects its civilisational ethos of tolerance and pluralism."

## WORD 3: REFUGEE

**Pronunciation:** ref-yoo-JEE\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Etymology:** French *réfugié*, from *refuge*.

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **RE-** → Back or away.
- **FUG-** → To flee.
- **-EE** → A person who is the recipient or subject of an action.

### B. Literal Meaning

A person who has fled to seek safety.

### C. Semantic Evolution

Originally used for people fleeing religious persecution, particularly French Protestants (Huguenots) who escaped France.

Its meaning expanded to encompass people fleeing war, persecution, violence and other serious threats.

### D. Modern Meaning

A refugee is a person who has fled their country because of persecution, conflict, violence or other circumstances seriously disturbing public order and who meets the applicable criteria for international refugee protection.

Under the 1951 Refugee Convention, the legal definition specifically requires a well-founded fear of persecution on specified grounds and being outside one's country of nationality or habitual residence.

**Important Indian Context:** India is not a party to the 1951 Refugee Convention or its 1967 Protocol. Refugee protection in India operates through domestic laws, constitutional protections and administrative arrangements rather than a dedicated national refugee statute.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Refugee crisis:** A large-scale displacement of people seeking international protection.
2. **Refugee rehabilitation:** Measures for resettlement and restoration of displaced populations.
3. **Refugee protection:** Legal and humanitarian safeguards for people fleeing persecution.

**UPSC Application:**

"India's refugee policy must balance humanitarian obligations, national security considerations and the principle of non-refoulement."

**Pronunciation:** SUB-ter-fyooj\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Etymology:** Latin *subter* (beneath, secretly) + *fugere* (to flee).

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **SUBTER-** → Beneath, secretly or by stealth.
- **FUG-** → To flee or escape.

### B. Literal Meaning

A secret means of escape or evasion.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The idea of escaping secretly evolved into the practice of avoiding an obligation, responsibility or difficult situation through deception or indirect methods.

### D. Modern Meaning

A deceptive strategy, trick or dishonest means used to avoid an obligation, conceal the truth or escape responsibility.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Political subterfuge:** Deceptive political manoeuvring.
2. **Diplomatic subterfuge:** Concealed or misleading diplomatic tactics.
3. **Avoid responsibility through subterfuge:** Using deception to escape accountability.

**UPSC Application:**

"Transparency and institutional accountability are essential to prevent the use of administrative subterfuge to circumvent public scrutiny."

## WORD 5: FUGACIOUS

**Pronunciation:** fyoo-GAY-shuhs\
**Part of Speech:** Adjective\
**Etymology:** Latin *fugax*, from *fugere* (to flee).

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **FUG-** → To flee or escape.
- **-ACIOUS** → An adjectival suffix indicating a quality or tendency.

### B. Literal Meaning

Something that tends to flee or disappear.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The idea of something escaping or passing away developed into the sense of something extremely temporary, transient or short-lived.

### D. Modern Meaning

Fleeting, transitory or quickly disappearing.

It is a relatively uncommon literary and scientific word.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

## WORD 6: FUGACITY

**Pronunciation:** fyoo-GAS-uh-tee\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Etymology:** From *fugacious* + *-ity* (state or quality).

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **FUG-** → To flee or escape.
- **-ITY** → State, condition or quality.

### B. Literal Meaning

The quality or tendency of escaping.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The general idea of escape developed into a technical scientific concept describing the tendency of a real gas to escape or its effective thermodynamic pressure.

### D. Modern Meaning

In thermodynamics, **fugacity** is an effective pressure that accounts for the non-ideal behaviour of real gases.

It is used in chemical thermodynamics to express chemical potential and phase equilibrium.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Fugacity coefficient:** Ratio of fugacity to pressure.
2. **Gas fugacity:** Effective thermodynamic pressure of a gas.
3. **Fugacity of real gases:** A concept used in non-ideal gas behaviour.

**UPSC Relevance:**

Primarily useful for science and technology awareness rather than direct GS answer writing.

## WORD 7: FUGUE

**Pronunciation:** FYOOG\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Etymology:** Italian *fuga*, from Latin *fuga* (flight), derived from *fugere*.

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **FUG-** → To flee or escape.
- **-UE** → Part of the borrowed word form; not an independent English suffix.

### B. Literal Meaning

Flight or an act of fleeing.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The idea of flight developed into two specialised meanings:

1. **Music:** A musical composition in which a theme is introduced and successively imitated by different voices or instruments.
2. **Psychology:** A state involving impaired autobiographical memory and, in some cases, unexpected travel or wandering.

### D. Modern Meaning

A fugue is primarily a contrapuntal musical composition. In psychology, the term also appears in the expression dissociative fugue.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Musical fugue:** A structured musical composition.
2. **Dissociative fugue:** A rare dissociative condition involving autobiographical memory disruption.
3. **Fugue-like state:** A state resembling dissociative wandering or disorientation.

**UPSC Relevance:**

Limited direct GS relevance; useful for enriching vocabulary and understanding interdisciplinary terminology.

## WORD 8: CENTRIFUGE

**Pronunciation:** SEN-truh-fyooj\
**Part of Speech:** Noun / Verb\
**Etymology:** *Centr-* (centre) + *-fuge* (from Latin *fugere*, to flee).

### A. Meaning of the Components

- **CENTR-** → Centre.
- **FUGE** → Fleeing or moving away.

### B. Literal Meaning

Something that moves or drives matter away from the centre.

### C. Semantic Evolution

The concept of outward movement from a rotating centre developed into the name of a mechanical device that uses rapid rotation to separate substances according to differences in density.

### D. Modern Meaning

A centrifuge is a machine that spins materials rapidly to separate their components.

As a verb, to centrifuge means to subject a substance to centrifugation.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Centrifuge technology:** Technology used for separating substances.
2. **Uranium enrichment centrifuges:** Gas centrifuges used to enrich uranium isotopes.
3. **Laboratory centrifugation:** Separation of biological or chemical materials through rapid rotation.

**UPSC Application:**

"Gas centrifuge technology is an important component of the nuclear fuel cycle and has implications for nuclear non-proliferation."

The process of moving or separating material through centrifugal action.

The mechanical principle of outward movement became associated with a scientific separation technique using rapid rotation.

### D. Modern Meaning

A process in which a mixture is rotated at high speed to separate its components based on differences in density, size or other physical properties.

### E. Common Usage (UPSC-Relevant)

1. **Differential centrifugation:** Separation of cellular components based on sedimentation characteristics.
2. **Ultracentrifugation:** High-speed centrifugation used in advanced biological and chemical research.
3. **Isotope separation by centrifugation:** A technique used in uranium enrichment.

**UPSC Application:**

"Centrifugation has important applications in biotechnology, medical diagnostics and nuclear technology."

## 6. A crucial UPSC vocabulary distinction

One important point to remember while using these words in Mains:

Centrifugal ≠ Separatist.

A centrifugal tendency is any tendency pulling authority, identity or power away from the centre. It may be democratic and constructive (decentralisation, regional autonomy), or destabilising (secessionism, violent fragmentation).

Similarly:

- Refugee ≠ Migrant: Every refugee is a displaced person, but not every migrant is a refugee.
- Fugitive ≠ Convict: A fugitive may be evading justice without having been convicted.
- Subterfuge ≠ Strategy: A strategy need not be deceptive; subterfuge inherently involves deception.

My recommendation for vocabulary retention: Give priority to centrifugal, fugitive, refuge, refugee and subterfuge. These five have the greatest practical value for UPSC Essay, GS-II, GS-III and GS-IV.

### 1. PACE

## PART III — IMPORTANT ETYMOLOGICAL DISTINCTION

### The two different meanings of PACE

English contains two words spelled pace that have different historical origins.

| Word  | Origin                      | Meaning                                 |
| ----- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Pace¹ | Latin passus                | Step, stride, speed or rate of progress |
| Pace² | Latin pāce, ablative of pāx | With due respect to; by the leave of    |

Example of the second meaning:

"His argument is, pace the critics, entirely constitutional."

Meaning: With due respect to the critics, his argument is entirely constitutional.

This formal expression is used to introduce a polite or sometimes ironic disagreement.

Common vocabulary trap

Do not associate pace meaning "with due respect to" with pace meaning speed or movement. They are etymologically unrelated.&#x20;
