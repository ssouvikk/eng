## **1. TRANQUILITY**

**Word:** Tranquility (also spelled *Tranquillity* in British English)

**Pronunciation:** tran-KWIL-i-tee\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Root Word:** Latin *tranquillus*

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The word *tranquility* comes from the Latin adjective **tranquillus**, meaning:

- Calm
- Quiet
- Still
- Undisturbed
- Peaceful

The Latin word *tranquillus* has an uncertain deeper etymology. Therefore, it is better not to forcefully divide it into smaller Latin roots.

Its English formation is:

| Component | Function            | Meaning                     |
| --------- | ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Tranquil  | Base adjective      | Calm, peaceful, undisturbed |
| -ity      | Noun-forming suffix | State or quality of being   |

**Memory principle:** The suffix *-ity* frequently converts an adjective into a noun denoting a state, condition or quality.

Examples:

- Serene → Serenity
- Stable → Stability
- Equal → Equality
- Tranquil → Tranquility

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Tranquility = The state or quality of being calm, peaceful and undisturbed.**

Think of a lake whose surface remains completely still, without disturbances or ripples.

That stillness represents tranquility.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The semantic development can be understood through three stages:

1. **Physical stillness:** Originally associated with calmness and the absence of disturbance, particularly in the natural environment.
2. **Emotional calmness:** Extended to describe a person's peaceful state of mind, free from anxiety, agitation or emotional disturbance.
3. **Social and political peacefulness:** Further extended to describe an environment or society free from conflict, violence and disorder.

Thus, the word moved from the idea of physical stillness to psychological serenity and eventually to broader social harmony.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Tranquility:** A state of peace, calmness and freedom from disturbance, agitation, anxiety or conflict.

It can refer to three dimensions:

| Dimension          | Meaning                          | Example                                                  |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Physical           | Absence of noise or disturbance  | The tranquility of rural surroundings                    |
| Psychological      | Peace of mind                    | Mental tranquility                                       |
| Social / Political | Absence of violence and disorder | Restoration of tranquility in a conflict-affected region |

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

1\. **Peace and tranquility:** A condition of social harmony and absence of disturbance.

*Example:* The rule of law is essential for maintaining peace and tranquility in a democratic society.

2\. **Mental tranquility:** A state of psychological calmness and emotional stability.

*Example:* Economic security and social protection contribute to the mental tranquility of vulnerable populations.

3\. **Public tranquility:** A condition of public order and freedom from disturbances.

*Example:* The administration must maintain public tranquility while safeguarding the constitutional right to peaceful assembly.

**UPSC Connection:**

- GS Paper II: Law and order, governance, fundamental rights.
- GS Paper III: Internal security, conflict management.
- GS Paper IV: Emotional intelligence, mental well-being, inner peace.
- Essay: Peace, happiness, human development and quality of life.

# **2. ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY OF TRANQUILITY**

**The word-family structure**

The words below share the same English lexical root; they are not all independent Latin-root derivatives.

## **WORD 1. TRANQUIL**

**Part of Speech:** Adjective

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm, quiet and undisturbed.
- No additional suffix.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

A condition of complete calmness or absence of disturbance.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Initially associated with physical stillness, the word gradually acquired psychological and social meanings.

It can now describe:

- A peaceful natural environment.
- A person's calm temperament.
- A society experiencing peace and stability.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

Calm, peaceful and free from disturbance, agitation or turmoil.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Tranquil environment:** A peaceful and undisturbed surrounding.

*Example:* Access to a tranquil environment contributes to psychological well-being.

2\. **Tranquil mind:** A mind free from anxiety and agitation.

*Example:* Meditation helps individuals maintain a tranquil mind amid increasing social and economic pressures.

3\. **Tranquil surroundings:** An environment characterised by peace and quiet.

*Example:* Urban planning should preserve green spaces to provide tranquil surroundings for citizens.

**UPSC Keywords:** Environmental well-being, mental health, quality of life, urban planning.

## **WORD 2. TRANQUILLITY / TRANQUILITY**

**Part of Speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm and peaceful.
- -ity: State or quality.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The state or quality of being tranquil.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The adjective *tranquil* developed into the abstract noun *tranquility*, allowing the concept of calmness to be expressed as a condition or state.

It expanded from physical quietness to psychological and societal peace.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

A state of peacefulness, serenity and freedom from disturbance.

**Spelling note:** Both *tranquility* and *tranquillity* are correct. The former is standard American spelling; the latter is common in British English.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Social tranquility:** Peace and harmony within society.

*Example:* Inclusive development is essential for ensuring social tranquility.

2\. **Political tranquility:** Political stability and absence of serious disturbances.

*Example:* Institutional accountability strengthens political tranquility in a constitutional democracy.

3\. **Peace and tranquility:** A condition of general peace and order.

*Example:* Effective policing is necessary to maintain peace and tranquility.

**UPSC Keywords:** Social harmony, political stability, public order, inclusive development.

## **WORD 3. TRANQUILLY**

**Part of Speech:** Adverb

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm and peaceful.
- -ly: Adverb-forming suffix meaning *in a particular manner*.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

In a calm, peaceful or undisturbed manner.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The adjective *tranquil* describes a state of calmness.

Adding *-ly* converts it into an adverb, describing how an action takes place.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

In a peaceful, composed or undisturbed manner.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Live tranquilly:** To live in a peaceful and undisturbed manner.

*Example:* Communities should have access to safe and secure environments in which they can live tranquilly.

2\. **Flow tranquilly:** To move smoothly and peacefully.

*Example:* Rivers flowing tranquilly through forest ecosystems reflect the importance of ecological conservation.

3\. **Sleep tranquilly:** To sleep peacefully without disturbance.

*Example:* Social security and access to healthcare can help vulnerable populations sleep more tranquilly.

**UPSC Keywords:** Human well-being, social security, environmental harmony.

## **WORD 4. TRANQUILIZE**

**Part of Speech:** Verb

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm or peaceful.
- -ize: Verb-forming suffix meaning *to make, cause or render*.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To make someone or something tranquil.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from the general idea of making someone calm or peaceful.

Over time, it acquired a specialised medical meaning: reducing agitation through the administration of medication.

It can also be used metaphorically to describe calming public anxiety or social unrest.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

1. To calm or soothe someone.
2. To reduce agitation or excitement, especially through medication.
3. To make a disturbed situation less agitated.

**Important distinction:** In contemporary medical usage, *tranquilize* is associated particularly with the use of sedative or tranquillizing drugs. It should not be confused with simply reassuring someone psychologically.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Tranquilize an agitated patient:** Medically reduce severe agitation.

*Example:* Medical professionals may use appropriate medication to tranquilize a severely agitated patient when clinically necessary.

2\. **Tranquilize public anxiety:** Reduce widespread public fear or apprehension.

*Example:* Transparent communication by the government can help tranquilize public anxiety during a crisis.

3\. **Tranquilize an animal:** Reduce an animal's activity or agitation using medication.

*Example:* Veterinary professionals may tranquilize wild animals during certain conservation and relocation operations.

**UPSC Keywords:** Public health, crisis communication, wildlife conservation, medical ethics.

## **WORD 5. TRANQUILIZATION**

**Part of Speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm.
- -ize: To make or cause.
- -ation: The process, action or result of an activity.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The process or act of making someone or something tranquil.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from the verb *tranquilize*.

Its meaning became particularly associated with medical and veterinary procedures involving the use of medication to reduce agitation, anxiety or excessive activity.

It also has a broader, less technical sense of bringing calmness to a disturbed situation.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

The process of calming an individual or animal, particularly through the use of tranquillizing medication.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Chemical tranquilization:** Use of chemical agents to calm an individual or animal.

*Example:* Chemical tranquilization may be employed in wildlife management under appropriate veterinary supervision.

2\. **Animal tranquilization:** Medically reducing an animal's activity or agitation.

*Example:* Animal tranquilization may be necessary during the rescue and relocation of certain wild species.

3\. **Emergency tranquilization:** Medically managing severe agitation in emergency settings.

*Example:* Emergency tranquilization must follow established clinical and ethical safeguards.

**UPSC Keywords:** Wildlife management, veterinary ethics, emergency medicine, conservation.

## **WORD 6. TRANQUILIZER / TRANQUILLISER**

**Part of Speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm.
- -ize: To make calm.
- -er: A person or thing that performs an action.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Something that makes a person or animal tranquil.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word originated from the verb *tranquilize*.

It became a medical term for drugs that reduce anxiety, agitation or excessive nervous activity.

In modern pharmacology, it is a relatively broad and somewhat dated term. More precise classifications include sedatives, anxiolytics and antipsychotics, depending on the drug and its action.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

A drug or medication used to reduce anxiety, agitation or excessive excitement.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Minor tranquilizer:** A traditional term for an anxiolytic medication used to reduce anxiety.

*Example:* The use of minor tranquilizers must be medically supervised to minimise dependence and adverse effects.

2\. **Major tranquilizer:** A traditional term for an antipsychotic medication.

*Example:* Major tranquilizers are used in the treatment of certain serious psychiatric conditions.

3\. **Tranquilizer dependence:** Dependence associated with prolonged or inappropriate use of certain tranquillizing medicines.

*Example:* Public health interventions should address the risks of inappropriate tranquilizer use and dependence.

**UPSC Keywords:** Mental health, drug dependence, public health, healthcare regulation.

Terminology note: Minor and major tranquilizer are older classifications. Modern medical writing generally prefers specific pharmacological categories.

## **WORD 7. TRANQUILIZING**

**Part of Speech:** Adjective / Present participle

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm.
- -ize: To make calm.
- -ing: Indicates an ongoing action or describes something having a particular effect.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Making someone or something calm; producing a calming effect.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed from *tranquilize* and acquired both literal and figurative meanings.

In medical contexts, it can describe the action of reducing agitation. In everyday and literary contexts, it describes something soothing or calming.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

Having a soothing or calming effect; or the act of making someone tranquil.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Tranquilizing effect:** An effect that reduces agitation or anxiety.

*Example:* Access to green spaces can have a tranquilizing effect on urban populations.

2\. **Tranquilizing influence:** An influence that helps restore calmness.

*Example:* Responsible leadership can exert a tranquilizing influence during periods of social unrest.

3\. **Tranquilizing medication:** Medication used to reduce agitation or anxiety.

*Example:* The administration of tranquilizing medication requires careful clinical assessment.

**UPSC Keywords:** Leadership, emotional intelligence, urban well-being, mental health.

## **WORD 8. TRANQUILNESS**

**Part of Speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Tranquil: Calm and peaceful.
- -ness: Noun-forming suffix indicating a state or quality.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The quality or condition of being tranquil.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The word was formed by adding the productive English suffix *-ness* to the adjective *tranquil*.

It conveys essentially the same basic meaning as *tranquility*.

However, *tranquilness* is considerably less common and less idiomatic than *tranquility*.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

The condition of being calm, peaceful and undisturbed.

### **E. Common Usage**

1\. **Tranquilness of nature:** The peaceful character of the natural environment.

2\. **Atmosphere of tranquilness:** An environment characterised by calmness.

3\. **Sense of tranquilness:** A feeling of peace and freedom from disturbance.

**UPSC Writing Advice:** Prefer *tranquility* over *tranquilness* in formal essays and Mains answers.

## **1. Meaning of the Components**

**Delegate** = *de-* + *legare*

| Component  | Origin       | Meaning                                           |
| ---------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **de-**    | Latin prefix | Away, down, from                                  |
| **legare** | Latin verb   | To send as an envoy, appoint, entrust, commission |

**Root idea:** To send someone away with authority or entrust a responsibility to another person.

**Important distinction:** The root *legare* is associated with sending an envoy or entrusting a commission. Do not confuse it with Latin *legere* (to read or choose), despite the similarity in spelling.

## **2. Literal Meaning**

**Delegate = To send someone as an authorised representative or entrust a task to another person.**

Imagine a king who cannot personally attend a diplomatic meeting. He sends a trusted representative with the authority to negotiate on his behalf.

That representative is a *delegate*.

The underlying idea is the transfer of authority, responsibility or representation from one person to another.

## **3. Semantic Evolution**

The word developed through the following conceptual stages:

**Latin: legare**

To send as an envoy; entrust a commission

**Delegare**

To assign or entrust something to another person

**Middle English / Old French influence**

The idea of authorising or appointing a representative

**Modern English: Delegate**

To assign responsibility or appoint an authorised representative

## **4. Modern Meaning**

**Delegate (verb):** To assign a task, responsibility or authority to another person, usually while retaining overall accountability.

**Delegate (noun):** A person authorised to represent an individual, organisation or group at a meeting, conference or assembly.

**Two important dimensions:**

1. **Administrative delegation:** A superior assigns work or decision-making authority to a subordinate.
2. **Representative delegation:** An individual represents a larger group in a formal gathering.

**UPSC Governance Connection:**

Delegation is an essential principle of administrative efficiency. It distributes responsibilities, reduces excessive centralisation, improves decision-making and promotes accountability.

However, delegation of authority does not necessarily mean complete transfer of accountability.

| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Delegate authority**        | Transfer decision-making powers to another person or institution                       |
| **Delegate responsibilities** | Assign duties to subordinates                                                          |
| **Delegated legislation**     | Rules and regulations made by the executive under authority granted by the legislature |

# **6. The Etymological Word Family**

The central root is Latin **LEGARE** — to send as an envoy, appoint or entrust.

The most useful way to remember this family is through three interconnected ideas:

- **Sending or representing:** Delegate, Delegation, Delegate (noun).
- **Sending as an authorised representative:** Legate, Legation, Legacy.
- **Entrusting or assigning authority:** Delegable, Delegative.

A caution about etymology: not every word that looks like *leg* belongs to this family. For example, *legal*, *legitimate*, *legislation* and *legislature* derive from Latin *lex/legis* (law), not *legare*. They are related conceptually to governance, but not members of the same etymological family.

It is important for understanding:

- Separation of powers.
- Parliamentary control over the executive.
- Administrative law.
- Judicial review.
- Legislative accountability.

**Example:** Parliament passes an Act, while the concerned ministry frames detailed rules under the authority granted by that Act.

A person sent by someone in authority to perform an official mission.

**3. Semantic Evolution**

In ancient Rome, a *legatus* was an appointed representative or official entrusted with a mission. The term later became associated particularly with papal representatives.

**4. Modern Meaning**

A diplomatic or ecclesiastical representative, especially a papal representative.

**5. Common Usage**

1. Papal legate.
2. Diplomatic legate.
3. Roman legate.

**UPSC connection:** Useful in ancient Roman administrative history and the study of diplomatic representation.

**Memory link:** A legate is an individual sent on a mission; a delegate is an authorised representative.

Historical / Diplomatic

Noun

- *legatus* = envoy or representative
- *-ation* = action, process or resulting institution

**2. Literal Meaning**

The office, mission or activity of an authorised representative.

**3. Semantic Evolution**

From the role or mission of an envoy, the term developed into a word describing a diplomatic mission or its official premises.

## **H. LEGACY**



High-value UPSC vocabulary

Noun



**1. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *legatus* = something bequeathed or entrusted.
- *-acy* = state, condition or quality.

**2. Literal Meaning**

Something handed down or bequeathed to another person.

**3. Semantic Evolution**

Originally associated with a bequest or property left to someone through a will. Its meaning subsequently expanded to include anything transmitted from the past to the present.

**4. Modern Meaning**

Something inherited or transmitted from a previous generation, institution, government or historical period.

**5. Common Usage**

1. Colonial legacy.

In a sneaky or stealthy manner.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The adjective *sneaky* developed an adverbial form to describe the manner of performing an action secretly or deceptively.

- **-ness:** Noun-forming suffix denoting a state, quality or condition.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The quality or state of being sneaky.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The word transformed a behavioural characteristic into an abstract quality that can be discussed, evaluated or criticised.

### **2.5 SNEAKER**

**Part of speech:** Noun

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Sneak:** To move stealthily.
- **-er:** Agent-forming suffix, generally indicating a person or thing that performs an action.

**B. Literal Meaning**

One who sneaks or moves stealthily.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Historically, *sneaker* referred to a person who sneaked. Later, in American English, it acquired the meaning of a rubber-soled shoe.

Rubber-soled shoes allow relatively quiet movement, making it possible to walk without producing much noise.

Thus, the name evolved from the stealthy movement associated with the footwear.

1. A person who sneaks.
2. A lightweight shoe, typically with a flexible rubber sole.

**E. Common Usage**

1. Sneaker culture.
2. Rubber-soled sneakers.
3. Sneaker manufacturing industry.

**UPSC relevance:** The footwear meaning is useful for understanding semantic extension and lexical change, but has limited direct GS vocabulary relevance.

# **JUS SANGUINIS — The Principle of Citizenship by Blood**



UPSC GS-II

Polity & Governance

Etymology & Vocabulary



## **1. Meaning of the Components**

**Jus sanguinis** is a Latin legal expression composed of two words:

| Component        | Origin / Meaning                               | Explanation                                                                      |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Jus (or Ius)** | Latin: law, right, justice                     | A legally recognised right, entitlement or principle of law.                     |
| **Sanguinis**    | Latin: *sanguis* (blood), in the genitive case | Literally means "of blood". It indicates descent, lineage or blood relationship. |

**Grammatical insight:** *Sanguinis* is the genitive singular form of *sanguis*, meaning blood. The genitive case expresses possession or association — "of blood".

## **2. Literal Meaning**

\\[ \text{Jus}+\text{Sanguinis} \\]

\\[ \text{Law/Right}+\text{Of Blood} \\]

**Literal meaning: The Law of Blood.**

In legal terminology, it refers to the principle that citizenship is determined by a person's descent or parentage rather than merely by the place of birth.

## **3. Semantic Evolution**

The expression evolved through the relationship between blood, ancestry, family lineage and political membership.

1. **Blood — Biological connection**

   Blood came to represent biological kinship and family relationships.
2. **Bloodline — Ancestral descent**

   The concept expanded from immediate biological relationships to inherited family lineage and ancestry.
3. **Descent — Legal affiliation**

   States began using parentage and descent as criteria for determining membership in the political community.
4. **Citizenship — Legal nationality**

   Blood relationship became a legal basis for transmitting citizenship from parent to child, even when the child was born outside the parent's country.

**Evolution in one line:**

Blood → Kinship → Ancestry → Descent → Citizenship by Descent.

## **4. Modern Meaning**

**Jus sanguinis (Right of Blood):** A principle of citizenship under which an individual's nationality is determined by the nationality or citizenship of one or both parents, irrespective of the country in which the individual is born, subject to the relevant national law.

**Example:**

An Indian citizen living in Canada gives birth to a child in Canada. Depending on the applicable citizenship laws of both countries, the child may acquire Indian citizenship through descent.

### **Indian Constitutional and Legal Relevance**

India follows neither an unrestricted *jus soli* nor an unrestricted *jus sanguinis* principle.

- **Article 5:** Citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution, with domicile and specified conditions relating to birth or parentage.
- **Article 6:** Citizenship rights of certain persons who migrated from Pakistan to India.
- **Article 7:** Citizenship provisions concerning certain persons who migrated to Pakistan.
- **Article 8:** Citizenship rights of certain persons of Indian origin residing outside India.
- **Article 9:** Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship and loss of Indian citizenship under the constitutional framework.
- **Article 11:** Parliament's power to regulate citizenship by law.
- **Citizenship Act, 1955, Section 4:** Citizenship by descent.

**Important UPSC distinction:** India's citizenship-by-birth provisions under Section 3 have progressively incorporated parental citizenship and immigration-status conditions. Citizenship by descent under Section 4 is the more direct statutory expression of *jus sanguinis*.

1. **Principle of jus sanguinis** — Citizenship based on descent.
2. **Citizenship by descent** — Nationality inherited through one's parents.
3. **Jus sanguinis-based citizenship regime** — A nationality framework primarily based on parentage.

# **A. SANGUINE — Optimistic and Confident**

High-value vocabulary

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

- **Sanguis:** Blood.
- **-ine:** An adjectival suffix meaning "of or relating to".

### **2. Literal Meaning**

"Of blood" or "blood-related".

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

In ancient medical theory, particularly the theory of the four bodily humours, blood was associated with a particular temperament.

A person believed to possess an abundance of blood was considered cheerful, energetic and optimistic.

This association gradually developed into the modern psychological meaning of *sanguine*.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

**Sanguine (adjective):** Optimistic, cheerful or confident, especially in a difficult or uncertain situation.

It can also mean blood-red in certain literary or descriptive contexts.

### **5. Common Usage**

1. **Sanguine outlook** — An optimistic perspective.
2. **Sanguine expectations** — Positive expectations about future outcomes.
3. **Remain sanguine about prospects** — Maintain optimism regarding future possibilities.

**UPSC application:**

"Despite persistent geopolitical tensions and economic uncertainties, India remains sanguine about its long-term growth prospects."

**Retention hook:** Blood → Cheerful temperament → Optimism.

# **B. SANGUINITY — Optimistic Temperament**

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

- **Sanguin-:** Blood; related to *sanguis*.
- **-ity:** A suffix denoting a state, quality or condition.

### **2. Literal Meaning**

The quality or condition of being sanguine.

### **3. Semantic Evolution**

The historical association between blood and cheerful temperament gave rise to *sanguine*. The suffix *-ity* transformed the adjective into an abstract noun describing that quality.

### **4. Modern Meaning**

**Sanguinity (noun):** Optimism, confidence or a hopeful disposition.

It is a relatively uncommon word; *optimism* and *sanguine outlook* are more natural in contemporary formal writing.

### **5. Common Usage**

1. **Unwavering sanguinity** — Persistent optimism.
2. **Sanguinity about economic recovery** — Confidence in economic revival.
3. **Excessive sanguinity** — Unrealistic optimism.

**UPSC application:**

"Excessive sanguinity regarding economic recovery may obscure structural weaknesses in employment generation."

**Retention hook:** Sanguine = Optimistic; Sanguinity = Optimism.

### **1. Meaning of the Components**

### **2. Literal Meaning**

The quality of being blood-related or characterised by blood.

**Retention hook:** Blood → Bloodshed → Violence.

- **Sanguis (Latin)** = Blood.
- **Jus sanguinis** = Right of blood → Citizenship by descent.
- **Sanguine** = Optimistic.
- **Sanguinary** = Bloody or violent.
- **Consanguinity** = Blood relationship.
- **Haima (Greek)** = Blood → Scientific vocabulary such as haematology and haemoglobin.

**One root, multiple intellectual domains:** A single Latin root connects constitutional law, citizenship, political identity, kinship, biological anthropology, medicine and conflict studies.

That is the real advantage of learning vocabulary etymologically: you remember not merely individual words, but the conceptual relationships that generate them.

## **1. JUS SOLI**

**Pronunciation:** /dʒʌs ˈsoʊlaɪ/ (commonly, *jus soh-lie*)\
**Language of origin:** Latin\
**Word category:** Legal expression (noun phrase)

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The expression consists of two Latin components:

| Component | Origin                           | Meaning                                  |
| --------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Jus**   | Latin *jus* (also written *ius*) | Law, right, legal entitlement            |
| **Soli**  | Latin *solum*                    | Of the soil, of the land (genitive form) |

**Important grammatical point:** *Soli* is the genitive singular form of *solum*. It expresses possession or association — "of the soil".

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Jus soli = Right of the soil / Right by virtue of the land.**

Break it down:

- Jus = Right
- Soli = Of the soil

Therefore, citizenship acquired by virtue of the territory in which a person is born.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The evolution of the expression can be understood in three stages:

1. **Original Latin sense:** *Jus* referred to law, right or an entitlement, while *solum* referred to ground or land.
2. **Legal development:** The expression came to represent a territorial principle of nationality — the relationship between birth within a territory and entitlement to citizenship.
3. **Modern constitutional usage:** It denotes the principle under which citizenship is acquired on the basis of the place of birth, subject to the conditions and exceptions prescribed by a country's law.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Jus soli (Right of soil):** A principle of citizenship under which a person acquires nationality or citizenship by virtue of being born within the territorial jurisdiction of a state.

It is contrasted with:

**Jus sanguinis (Right of blood):** A principle under which citizenship is acquired through the nationality or citizenship of one's parents.

**Illustrative comparison**

**Jus soli**

# **PLACE**

Where were you born?

**Jus sanguinis**

# **PARENTAGE**

What is your parents' nationality?

**UPSC distinction:** Jus soli is territorial in orientation; jus sanguinis is descent-based.

**Indian constitutional context**

India initially adopted a relatively broad birth-based citizenship principle under Article 5 and Section 3 of the Citizenship Act, 1955. However, subsequent amendments progressively introduced restrictions.

Under the present Section 3 of the Citizenship Act:

- Born in India between 26 January 1950 and 1 July 1987: citizenship generally follows birth in India, irrespective of parents' citizenship, subject to statutory exceptions.
- Born between 1 July 1987 and 3 December 2004: at least one parent must be an Indian citizen.
- Born on or after 3 December 2004: both parents must be Indian citizens, or one must be an Indian citizen and the other must not be an illegal migrant, subject to statutory exceptions.

Thus, India has moved towards a qualified or restricted form of *jus soli*, incorporating elements of *jus sanguinis*.

### **E. Common Usage: High-Frequency Collocations**

1. **Unrestricted jus soli** — Birth-based citizenship granted broadly, regardless of parents' nationality.
2. **Qualified jus soli** — Birth-based citizenship subject to statutory conditions.
3. **Jus soli principle** — The territorial principle governing acquisition of citizenship.

**UPSC-ready sentence:**

"India's citizenship regime has gradually transitioned from a relatively liberal application of jus soli towards a qualified model that incorporates elements of jus sanguinis."

### **WORD 1. JURISDICTION**

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʊərɪsˈdɪkʃən/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Juris** = Of law (genitive form of Latin *jus*).
- **Dictio** = Saying, declaration or pronouncement; from *dicere*, to speak or declare.

**B. Literal Meaning**

*Juris + dictio* = Declaration or pronouncement of law.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

1. Originally referred to the administration or pronouncement of justice.
2. Gradually acquired the meaning of legal authority exercised by courts and other competent authorities.
3. Developed into the modern concept of the geographical, subject-matter and institutional limits of legal authority.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Jurisdiction:** The legal authority of a court, government or administrative body to exercise power over a particular territory, person, subject matter or dispute.

Think of it as answering three questions:

- Where can an authority exercise power?
- Over whom can it exercise power?
- Over what subject can it exercise power?

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Territorial jurisdiction** — Legal authority confined to a particular geographical area.
2. **Subject-matter jurisdiction** — Legal competence to hear a particular category of cases.
3. **Original jurisdiction** — Authority to hear a case at its first instance.

**UPSC connection:** Article 131 (original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court), Article 226 (High Court writ jurisdiction), Centre–State disputes and federalism.

### **WORD 2. JURISPRUDENCE**

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʊərɪsˈpruːdəns/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Juris** = Of law.
- **Prudentia** = Knowledge, wisdom, foresight or practical understanding.

**B. Literal Meaning**

*Juris + prudentia* = Knowledge or wisdom of law.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

1. Originally referred to systematic knowledge of law.
2. Developed into the study of legal principles, doctrines and institutions.
3. Gradually acquired a philosophical dimension, examining the nature, origin, validity and purpose of law.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Jurisprudence:** The science, philosophy or systematic study of law, including its nature, principles, sources, interpretation and relationship with morality and society.

It is not merely the study of legal provisions; it examines the fundamental questions underlying the legal system.

For example:

- What makes a law legitimate?
- Is every legally valid law morally justified?
- What is the relationship between law and justice?
- Should judges interpret law literally or purposively?

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Constitutional jurisprudence** — Judicial principles and doctrines concerning constitutional interpretation.
2. **Natural law jurisprudence** — Legal philosophy connecting law with morality, reason and natural rights.
3. **Transformative constitutional jurisprudence** — Constitutional interpretation that facilitates social transformation.

**UPSC connection:** Basic Structure Doctrine, constitutional morality, privacy jurisprudence, transformative constitutionalism and judicial activism.

### **WORD 3. JURIST**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʊərɪst/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Jur- / Jus** = Law or legal right.
- **-ist** = A person associated with a particular discipline, profession or field of knowledge.

**B. Literal Meaning**

A person associated with the study or practice of law.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

1. Originally referred to a person practising law.
2. Expanded to include legal scholars and writers.
3. Acquired the modern meaning of an expert in legal principles and jurisprudence.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Jurist:** A legal scholar, expert or authority who possesses specialised knowledge of law and legal philosophy.

A jurist need not necessarily be a practising advocate or judge.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Eminent jurist** — A highly respected legal scholar.
2. **Constitutional jurist** — An expert in constitutional law and theory.
3. **Legal jurist** — A scholar or authority specialising in legal principles.

**UPSC connection:** Constitutional interpretation, legal philosophy, judicial independence and the role of legal scholarship in constitutional development.

### **WORD 4. JURIDICAL**

**Pronunciation:** /dʒʊəˈrɪdɪkəl/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Juri-** = Law or legal right.
- **Dicere** = To speak, declare or pronounce.
- **-al** = Pertaining to.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Pertaining to the declaration or administration of law.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Derived from Latin *iuridicus*, meaning relating to right or justice. It evolved into a formal adjective describing matters connected with law, legal authority and judicial institutions.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Juridical:** Relating to law, legal rights, legal institutions or the administration of justice.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Juridical personality** — Recognition of an entity as a legal person.
2. **Juridical authority** — Authority recognised or established by law.
3. **Juridical framework** — The legal structure governing an institution or activity.

**UPSC connection:** Article 300 (Government as a suable entity), corporate personality, local self-government institutions and legal status of statutory bodies.

### **WORD 5. JURISPRUDENTIAL**

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʊərɪspruːˈdɛnʃəl/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Juris** = Of law.
- **Prudentia** = Knowledge or wisdom.
- **-al** = Pertaining to.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Pertaining to the knowledge or philosophy of law.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed as the adjective of jurisprudence. While jurisprudence refers to the systematic study or philosophy of law, jurisprudential describes ideas, arguments and approaches associated with that discipline.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Jurisprudential:** Relating to the theoretical, philosophical or conceptual foundations of law.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Jurisprudential debate** — A debate concerning the philosophy or foundations of law.
2. **Jurisprudential foundations** — The underlying legal and philosophical principles.
3. **Jurisprudential approach** — An approach based on legal theory rather than merely statutory interpretation.

**UPSC connection:** Debate between positivist and natural-law approaches, constitutional morality, rights-based constitutionalism.

### **WORD 6. JURISCONSULT**

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʊərɪskənˈsʌlt/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Juris** = Of law.
- **Consultus** = Consulted, advised or one whose advice is sought; related to *consulere*, to consult.

**B. Literal Meaning**

A person consulted for legal advice.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The Latin *iurisconsultus* referred to a person whose specialised knowledge of law was sought for opinions and advice. The term subsequently entered English as a formal designation for a legal expert or adviser.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Jurisconsult:** A legal adviser or expert who provides authoritative opinions on questions of law.

It is a relatively uncommon, formal word; *legal adviser* or *legal consultant* is more common in contemporary usage.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Renowned jurisconsult** — A distinguished legal expert.
2. **Opinion of a jurisconsult** — An expert legal opinion.
3. **Roman jurisconsults** — Legal experts who contributed to the development of Roman law.

**UPSC connection:** Evolution of legal institutions, Roman law, codification and comparative legal systems.

### **WORD 7. JUST**

**Pronunciation:** /dʒʌst/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin **iustus** = Lawful, righteous, conforming to what is right.
- **Jus** = Law or right.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Conforming to law, right or justice.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

1. Originally conveyed the idea of acting in accordance with law and established standards.
2. Expanded beyond mere legal conformity to include moral righteousness and fairness.
3. Developed into an adjective describing fairness, impartiality and moral correctness.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Just:** Morally right, fair, reasonable or consistent with principles of justice.

Important distinction:

- **Legal:** Conforming to existing law.
- **Just:** Conforming to principles of fairness and justice.

An action may be legally valid but morally unjust.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Just and equitable society** — A society founded on fairness and equality.
2. **Just cause** — A morally or legally defensible reason.
3. **Just and reasonable restrictions** — Restrictions satisfying constitutional standards of reasonableness and justice.

**UPSC connection:** Preamble, social justice, equality before law, substantive justice and constitutional morality.

### **WORD 8. JUSTICE**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʌstɪs/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Justus** = Righteous, lawful or fair.
- **-itia** = A Latin suffix used to form abstract nouns expressing a quality or state.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The quality or state of being just.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

1. Initially represented conformity with law and righteousness.
2. Expanded to include fairness, impartiality and the proper treatment of individuals.
3. Developed into a broad legal, political and ethical concept encompassing distributive, procedural, social and corrective dimensions.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Justice:** The principle of fairness, impartiality and rightful treatment, ensuring that individuals receive what is due to them under law and moral standards.

Four important dimensions:

- **Social justice:** Fairness in social relations and opportunities.
- **Economic justice:** Fair distribution of economic resources and opportunities.
- **Political justice:** Equal political rights and participation.
- **Procedural justice:** Fairness in the process through which decisions are made.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Social justice and empowerment** — Removing structural inequalities and ensuring dignity.
2. **Access to justice** — Ensuring that individuals can effectively seek legal remedies.
3. **Distributive justice** — Fair allocation of resources, benefits and burdens.

**UPSC connection:** Preamble, Articles 14–18, DPSPs, affirmative action, inclusive development and SDGs.

### **WORD 9. JUSTIFY**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈdʒʌstɪfaɪ/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Justus** = Right, lawful or fair.
- **-fy** = To make, cause or render; from Latin *-ficare*.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To make something right or demonstrate that it is right.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Originally connected with making something conform to what is right. It gradually acquired the meaning of demonstrating or defending the reasonableness, legitimacy or correctness of an action.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Justify:** To provide valid reasons or evidence demonstrating that an action, decision, belief or policy is reasonable, legitimate or morally defensible.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Justify government intervention** — Provide reasons supporting state action.
2. **Justify affirmative action** — Establish the constitutional and social rationale for preferential measures.
3. **Justify the use of force** — Demonstrate that coercive action is legally and morally defensible.

**UPSC connection:** Essay argumentation, Ethics case studies, proportionality, reasonable restrictions and welfare policies.

### **WORD 10. JUSTIFICATION**

**Pronunciation:** /ˌdʒʌstɪfɪˈkeɪʃən/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Justus** = Right or fair.
- **-ficare** = To make or render.
- **-ation** = The process, act or result.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The act or process of making something appear or become right.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Moved from the idea of making something conform to righteousness towards providing reasons or grounds that establish the legitimacy of a decision or action.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Justification:** A reason, argument or evidence demonstrating the legitimacy, necessity or appropriateness of an action, decision or policy.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Constitutional justification** — A legal rationale supporting a governmental action or restriction.
2. **Moral justification** — An ethical basis for an action.
3. **Policy justification** — Reasons supporting the adoption of a particular public policy.

**UPSC connection:** Reasonable restrictions under Article 19, proportionality test, affirmative action and ethical decision-making.

### **WORD 11. JUSTICIABLE**

**Pronunciation:** /dʒʌˈstɪʃəbəl/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Justice / Just-** = Law, fairness or right.
- **-able** = Capable of being subjected to or dealt with through a particular process.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Capable of being dealt with by a court of law.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed from the legal concept of matters capable of judicial determination. It distinguishes enforceable legal claims from questions that courts cannot appropriately adjudicate.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Justiciable:** Capable of being examined, determined or enforced by a court of law.

A justiciable right is one for which judicial enforcement or a legal remedy is available.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Justiciable rights** — Rights enforceable through judicial proceedings.
2. **Non-justiciable provisions** — Provisions not directly enforceable through courts.
3. **Justiciable dispute** — A dispute capable of judicial determination.

**UPSC connection:** Fundamental Rights versus Directive Principles of State Policy.

**Constitutional illustration**

| Fundamental Rights                          | DPSPs                                              |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Generally justiciable                       | Non-justiciable                                    |
| Article 32 and Article 226 provide remedies | Article 37 declares them non-enforceable by courts |
| Legally enforceable                         | Fundamental in governance                          |

**Nuance:** Non-justiciability does not mean constitutional irrelevance. DPSPs influence legislation, interpretation and governance.

### **WORD 12. JUSTICIABILITY**

**Pronunciation:** /dʒʌˌstɪʃiəˈbɪləti/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Justiciable** = Capable of judicial determination.
- **-ity** = State, condition or quality.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The quality or condition of being capable of judicial determination.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed as an abstract legal term describing whether a matter is suitable for judicial adjudication. It also came to encompass questions about the limits of judicial intervention.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Justiciability:** The quality or condition of a legal issue being appropriate for determination by a court.

A court may examine whether a dispute involves legal rights, whether a remedy is available and whether the issue falls within its judicial competence.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Question of justiciability** — Whether a matter can be decided by a court.
2. **Limits of justiciability** — Boundaries of judicial intervention.
3. **Justiciability of socio-economic rights** — Whether courts can adjudicate and enforce such rights.

**UPSC connection:** Judicial review, separation of powers, DPSPs, environmental rights and judicial activism.

### **WORD 13. INJURY**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪndʒəri/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **In-** = Not, or a negative prefix in this formation.
- **Juria / Iuria** = A legal right or law; from Latin *ius*.

**B. Literal Meaning**

A condition contrary to one's legal right; a wrong or violation.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Latin *iniuria* referred to an unlawful act, injustice or violation of another person's rights. Its meaning gradually expanded to include physical, emotional and material harm.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Injury:** Harm, damage or violation suffered by a person, body, property or legally protected interest.

Important distinction:

- Physical injury = Bodily harm.
- Legal injury = Violation of a legally recognised right or interest.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Injury to fundamental rights** — Violation of constitutionally guaranteed rights.
2. **Personal injury** — Physical or psychological harm suffered by an individual.
3. **Legal injury** — Harm involving infringement of a legal right.

**UPSC connection:** Article 32, Article 226, tort law, compensation, human rights and constitutional remedies.

### **WORD 14. INJURE**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈɪndʒər/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **In-** = Negative element.
- **Jus / Ius** = Right or law.

**B. Literal Meaning**

To do wrong to someone or violate someone's rights.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Derived through Latin *iniuria* and related forms. Its meaning expanded from committing a legal wrong to causing physical, psychological or reputational harm.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Injure:** To cause harm or damage to a person, property, reputation or legally protected interest.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Injure fundamental rights** — Violate constitutionally protected rights.
2. **Injure public interest** — Cause harm to collective welfare.
3. **Injure the reputation of an individual** — Damage someone's public standing.

**UPSC connection:** Fundamental rights, defamation, public interest and state accountability.

### **WORD 15. INJURIOUS**

**Pronunciation:** /ɪnˈdʒʊəriəs/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Injury / Injur-** = Harm or violation.
- **-ous** = Full of, characterised by or causing.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Causing injury or harm.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed from the idea of conduct that violates rights or causes harm. It now covers actions, substances, policies or circumstances that produce damaging effects.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Injurious:** Harmful, damaging or detrimental to a person, society, public welfare or a legally protected interest.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Injurious to public health** — Harmful to collective health.
2. **Injurious to national interest** — Detrimental to the country's interests.
3. **Injurious conduct** — Behaviour causing harm or damage.

**UPSC connection:** Public health governance, environmental protection, consumer protection and the doctrine of public nuisance.

### **WORD 16. INJUSTICE**

**Pronunciation:** /ɪnˈdʒʌstɪs/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **In-** = Not; negative prefix.
- **Justice** = Fairness, righteousness or rightful treatment.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Absence or denial of justice.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed from the idea of conduct contrary to justice. Its meaning broadened to encompass institutional discrimination, structural inequalities, exploitation and violations of human dignity.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Injustice:** Unfair, discriminatory or oppressive treatment that violates principles of fairness, equality, rights or dignity.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Social injustice** — Unfair treatment arising from social structures or institutions.
2. **Structural injustice** — Systemic arrangements that perpetuate inequality.
3. **Gender injustice** — Discrimination or unequal treatment based on gender.

**UPSC connection:** Social justice, gender equality, caste discrimination, vulnerability and inclusive development.

### **WORD 22. SOIL**

The ground or earth.

1. **Soil fertility** — The capacity of soil to support plant growth.
2. **Soil conservation** — Protection of soil from erosion and degradation.
3. **Soil health** — The continued capacity of soil to function as a living ecosystem.

**UPSC connection:** GS-III, agriculture, sustainable development, land degradation, food security and climate change.

### **WORD 23. TOPSOIL**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈtɒpsɔɪl/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Top** = Uppermost or surface.
- **Soil** = Ground or earth.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The uppermost layer of soil.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

The term emerged from agricultural and soil-science vocabulary to distinguish the biologically active surface layer from deeper soil horizons.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Topsoil:** The uppermost soil layer, generally rich in organic matter, nutrients and biological activity.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Loss of topsoil** — Removal of the fertile upper layer through erosion.
2. **Topsoil conservation** — Protection of the productive surface layer.
3. **Topsoil degradation** — Deterioration of the upper soil layer.

**UPSC connection:** Soil erosion, deforestation, unsustainable agriculture and desertification.

### **WORD 24. SUBSOIL**

**Pronunciation:** /ˈsʌbsɔɪl/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Sub-** = Under or beneath.
- **Soil** = Ground or earth.

**B. Literal Meaning**

Soil beneath the surface layer.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed as a technical agricultural term to describe deeper soil horizons lying below the topsoil.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Subsoil:** The layer of soil beneath the topsoil, generally containing less organic matter but often accumulating clay and minerals.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Subsoil moisture** — Water stored in deeper soil layers.
2. **Subsoil characteristics** — Physical and chemical properties of deeper soil horizons.
3. **Subsoil compaction** — Compression of deeper soil layers, restricting root growth and water movement.

**UPSC connection:** Irrigation, groundwater recharge, agricultural productivity and soil management.

### **WORD 25. SOIL EROSION**

**Pronunciation:** /sɔɪl ɪˈrəʊʒən/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Soil** = Ground or earth.
- **Erosion** = Gradual wearing away or removal.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The wearing away or removal of soil.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Initially associated with the physical removal of earth through natural processes. Its modern usage includes accelerated erosion caused by human activities.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Soil erosion:** The detachment and transportation of soil particles by water, wind or other agents.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Water-induced soil erosion** — Removal of soil through rainfall and surface runoff.
2. **Wind erosion** — Removal of soil particles by wind.
3. **Soil erosion control** — Measures to prevent or reduce soil loss.

**UPSC connection:** Land degradation, watershed management, afforestation and sustainable agriculture.

### **WORD 26. SOIL DEGRADATION**

**Pronunciation:** /sɔɪl ˌdɛɡrəˈdeɪʃən/

**A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Soil** = Ground or earth.
- **De-** = Reduction or reversal.
- **Gradation** = A process of change or decline.

**B. Literal Meaning**

The deterioration of soil quality.

**C. Semantic Evolution**

Developed from agricultural and environmental terminology describing the gradual decline of soil productivity and ecological functions.

**D. Modern Meaning**

**Soil degradation:** The deterioration of soil's physical, chemical and biological properties, reducing its productive capacity and ecological functions.

**E. Common Usage**

1. **Land and soil degradation** — Deterioration of terrestrial ecosystems and soil quality.
2. **Soil degradation neutrality** — A condition in which the quantity and quality of land resources remain stable or improve.
3. **Anthropogenic soil degradation** — Soil deterioration resulting from human activities.

**UPSC connection:** UNCCD, SDG 15 (Life on Land), desertification, climate resilience and sustainable land management.

## **5. Important Etymological Distinctions: Avoid False Friends**

Not every word that looks or sounds similar belongs to the same etymological family.

| Word           | Actual origin / meaning                              | Relationship            |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Jus            | Latin: law or right                                  | Original root           |
| Jurisdiction   | Jus + dictio                                         | Direct family           |
| Jurisprudence  | Juris + prudentia                                    | Direct family           |
| Justice        | Latin *iustitia*                                     | Related to jus          |
| Judicial       | Latin *iudicialis*                                   | Extended cognate family |
| Injury         | Latin *iniuria*                                      | Related to jus          |
| Jury           | Old French *jurée*, from a word relating to swearing | Not from jus            |
| Juror          | From jury                                            | Not from jus            |
| Jurisdictional | From jurisdiction                                    | Derivative              |
| Soil           | Latin *solum*, through French                        | Direct descendant       |
| Sole (foot)    | Latin *solea*                                        | Not from solum          |
| Solitary       | Latin *solitarius*, from *solus* (alone)             | Not from solum          |
| Solar          | Latin *sol* (sun)                                    | Not from solum          |

**Retention rule:** Similar spelling does not guarantee common ancestry. Etymology concerns historical origin, not superficial resemblance.

## **7. How to Retain the Entire Family: The Association Method**

Instead of memorising 26 isolated words, remember four conceptual clusters.

**Cluster 1: LAW — JUS**

# **JUS = RIGHT / LAW**

Jurisdiction → Where does the law apply?

Jurisprudence → What is the philosophy of law?

Jurist → Who studies or specialises in law?

Juridical → What relates to law?

Jurisprudential → What relates to legal philosophy?

**Cluster 2: JUSTICE — JUST**

# **JUST = RIGHT / FAIR**

Just → What is fair?

Justice → What is fairness?

Justify → How do you defend an action?

Justification → What is the rationale?

Justiciable → Can a court enforce it?

Justiciability → Is it suitable for judicial determination?

**Cluster 3: JUDGMENT — JUDIC**

# **JUDIC = JUDGMENT**

Judicial → Related to courts.

Judiciary → Institution of courts.

Judicious → Wise judgment.

Adjudicate → Decide a dispute.

Adjudication → Process of deciding.

**Cluster 4: LAND — SOLUM**

# **SOLUM = GROUND**

Soil → Earth.

Topsoil → Upper soil layer.

Subsoil → Deeper soil layer.

Soil erosion → Removal of soil.

Soil degradation → Deterioration of soil.

## **8. Application in UPSC Mains Answer Writing**

Observe how these words can elevate the precision of an answer.

**Ordinary expression**

"India has gradually changed its citizenship rules to make it more difficult to obtain citizenship through birth."

**UPSC-oriented expression**

"India's citizenship framework has witnessed a gradual transition from a relatively liberal application of *jus soli* towards a qualified model incorporating elements of *jus sanguinis*. This evolution reflects the interplay between territoriality, descent-based nationality, sovereign authority and constitutional conceptions of citizenship."

**Another example — GS-II (Judiciary):**

"An independent judiciary, supported by robust judicial review and a coherent body of constitutional jurisprudence, is indispensable for protecting justiciable rights and upholding constitutional supremacy."

**Another example — GS-IV (Ethics):**

"Administrative discretion must be exercised judiciously and supported by a defensible moral justification, rather than arbitrary considerations."
