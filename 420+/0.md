## **1. CONCUR**

**Pronunciation:** /kənˈkɜːr/ (kuhn-KUR)\
**Part of speech:** Verb\
**Word family:** Concur, concurrence, concurrent, concurrently, concurrency

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The word *concur* comes from Latin:

- **Con-** = together, with, jointly.
- **Currere** = to run.

The Latin verb *concurrere* means **to run together, come together, or meet**.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To run together; to come together at a common point.

Imagine two rivers flowing from different directions and meeting at a confluence. Similarly, two people may arrive at the same conclusion through different lines of reasoning.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The meaning developed through the following stages:

**CON- + CURRERE**

Together + To run

**To run together**

Physical convergence

**To meet at a common point**

Coming together of ideas or opinions

**To agree or share the same opinion**

Modern meaning

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Concur (verb):** To agree with someone or something; to have the same opinion, judgment, or conclusion.

It can also mean that two or more events occur simultaneously.

Two important senses:

1. **Agreement:** To share another person's opinion or judgment.
2. **Simultaneous occurrence:** To happen at the same time or coincide.

**Examples:**

- The Supreme Court judges did not concur with the reasoning of the dissenting opinion.
- I concur with the committee's recommendation regarding electoral reforms.
- Several factors concurred to produce the economic crisis.

### **E. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation            | Meaning                        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Concur with an opinion | Agree with an opinion          |
| Concur in a decision   | Agree in reaching a decision   |
| Factors concur         | Multiple factors come together |

**UPSC answer-writing example:**

"While scholars concur on the importance of inclusive development, they differ on the institutional mechanisms required to achieve it."

**Synonyms:** Agree, assent, accord, coincide, endorse.

**Antonyms:** Disagree, dissent, differ, oppose.

**Important distinction:**

- **Concur with:** Agree with a person, opinion or argument.
- **Concur in:** Agree in an action, decision or conclusion.
- **Concur with an event:** Can mean coincide, depending on context.

# **2. THE ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY**

## **Understanding the root: CURRERE = TO RUN**

The Latin root *currere* (to run) has generated an extensive vocabulary in English.

Its derivatives are particularly useful in UPSC because they appear in discussions involving:

- Governance and administration
- Constitutional law and judicial interpretation
- Public policy and institutional mechanisms
- Economy and development
- Social change and historical processes
- Ethics and philosophical reasoning

The following words share the same historical Latin root or are formed from its derivatives.

## **WORD 1. CONCURRENCE**

**Part of speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Con-** = together.
- **Currere** = to run.
- **-ence** = state, condition or process.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The state of running together or coming together.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Physical convergence → convergence of views or circumstances → agreement or simultaneous occurrence.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Concurrence:** Agreement between two or more people; or the simultaneous occurrence of different events or circumstances.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Broad concurrence of opinion.
2. Concurrence of circumstances.
3. Judicial concurrence.

**UPSC example:**

"The success of cooperative federalism requires the concurrence of political will, institutional capacity and administrative accountability."

**Retention clue:** Concur is the action; concurrence is the state of agreement.

## **WORD 2. CONCURRENT**

**Part of speech:** Adjective

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Con-** = together.
- **Currere** = to run.
- **-ent** = having the quality or characteristic of.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Running together.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Things moving together → events occurring together → simultaneous or overlapping activities.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Concurrent:** Occurring, existing or operating at the same time.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Concurrent jurisdiction.
2. Concurrent powers.
3. Concurrent development.

**UPSC relevance: Constitutional Law**

The Seventh Schedule of the Indian Constitution contains the Concurrent List, covering subjects on which both Parliament and State Legislatures can legislate, subject to constitutional provisions.

**UPSC example:**

"Education is a subject of concurrent jurisdiction, requiring coordination between the Union and State governments."

**Retention clue:** Concurrent = running parallel in time.

## **WORD 3. CONCURRENTLY**

**Part of speech:** Adverb

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Con-** = together.
- **Currere** = to run.
- **-ly** = in a particular manner.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

In a manner that runs together.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Parallel movement → simultaneous operation → happening at the same time.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Concurrently:** Simultaneously; at the same time.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Concurrently implemented policies.
2. Concurrently functioning institutions.
3. Concurrently occurring crises.

**UPSC example:**

"India must address poverty, unemployment and environmental degradation concurrently rather than treating them as isolated challenges."

**Retention clue:** Concurrent is an adjective; concurrently is its adverbial form.

## **WORD 4. OCCUR**

**Part of speech:** Verb

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Ob-** (assimilated to *oc-*) = toward, against or before.
- **Currere** = to run.

Latin *occurrere* meant to run towards, meet or present itself.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To run towards or meet.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

To encounter → to present itself → to happen or take place.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Occur:** To happen, take place or exist at a particular time or place.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Occur frequently.
2. Occur naturally.
3. Occur in response to a stimulus.

**UPSC example:**

"Regional disparities occur when the benefits of economic growth are unevenly distributed across geographical areas."

**Retention clue:** An event that comes into existence occurs.

## **WORD 5. OCCURRENCE**

**Part of speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Occur** = to happen.
- **-ence** = state or process.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The act or state of happening.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Encounter or appearance → happening of an event → an incident or phenomenon.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Occurrence:** An event, incident or phenomenon; the act of happening.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Frequent occurrence of natural disasters.
2. Rare occurrence of a phenomenon.
3. Incidence and occurrence of social problems.

**UPSC example:**

"The increasing occurrence of extreme weather events highlights the urgency of climate adaptation."

**Important distinction: Occurrence vs Incidence**

- **Occurrence:** The fact that something happens.
- **Incidence:** Often refers to the rate or frequency of something happening, especially in statistics, epidemiology and social sciences.

## **WORD 6. RECUR**

**Part of speech:** Verb

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Re-** = again, back.
- **Currere** = to run.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

To run again.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Returning → happening again → repeating periodically or repeatedly.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Recur:** To happen repeatedly, especially at regular intervals or after a period of absence.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Recurring economic crises.
2. Recurrent patterns of inequality.
3. Problems that recur periodically.

**UPSC example:**

"Recurring agrarian distress indicates that structural weaknesses in the agricultural economy remain unresolved."

**Retention clue:** Recur = run again = happen again.

## **WORD 7. RECURRENCE**

**Part of speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Re-** = again.
- **Currere** = to run.
- **-ence** = state or process.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The process of running again.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Return → repetition → repeated appearance of an event or condition.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Recurrence:** The repeated occurrence of an event, phenomenon or condition.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Recurrence of poverty.
2. Recurrence of communal violence.
3. Recurrence of economic instability.

**UPSC example:**

"Addressing the recurrence of communal violence requires not merely law enforcement but also social trust-building and institutional accountability."

**Retention clue:** Recur is the verb; recurrence is the noun.

## **WORD 8. CURRENT**

**Part of speech:** Adjective / Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *currere* = to run.
- Latin *currens* = running (present participle of *currere*).

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Running or flowing.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Flowing water → continuous movement → something presently flowing or existing → present or contemporary.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

As an adjective:

**Current:** Belonging to the present time; prevailing or existing now.

As a noun:

A continuous flow of water, air or electricity; also a movement or tendency of opinion.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Current affairs.
2. Current socio-economic trends.
3. Political currents.

**UPSC example:**

"Current demographic trends suggest that India must invest in human capital to realise its demographic dividend."

**Retention clue:** A current flows continuously; hence current means what is presently in motion or prevailing.

## **WORD 9. CURRENCY**

**Part of speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *currere* = to run.
- Latin *currens* = running.
- The historical development passed through Medieval Latin and French forms associated with circulation.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

The condition of flowing or circulating.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Circulation → general acceptance or prevalence → money circulating within an economy.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Currency:**

1. The system of money used in a country.
2. The state of being generally accepted, prevalent or in circulation.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Foreign currency reserves.
2. Currency depreciation.
3. Currency in circulation.

**UPSC example:**

"Persistent currency depreciation can increase the cost of imports and contribute to imported inflation."

**Retention clue:** Currency is money that circulates through an economy.

## **WORD 10. DISCOURSE**

**Part of speech:** Noun / Verb

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Dis-** = apart, in different directions (in this historical formation, the prefix contributes to the idea of movement).
- **Currere** = to run.
- Latin *discursus* = running about, movement in different directions.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Running about or moving in different directions.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Movement in different directions → exchange of thoughts and arguments → organised communication or discussion.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Discourse:** A formal or extended discussion, communication or expression of ideas on a particular subject.

In social sciences, discourse can also mean a system of language, ideas and practices through which a subject is understood and represented.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Public discourse.
2. Development discourse.
3. Constitutional discourse.

**UPSC example:**

"Public discourse on development must move beyond GDP growth to incorporate equity, sustainability and human well-being."

**Anthropology connection:**

The concept of discourse is important in understanding how power, culture and social institutions shape knowledge and social realities.

**Retention clue:** Discourse = movement of ideas through discussion.

## **WORD 11. COURSE**

**Part of speech:** Noun / Verb

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *currere* = to run.
- Latin *cursus* = a running, course or journey.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

A path along which something runs.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Running path → route or direction → sequence of events, progression or a programme of study.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Course:**

1. A route or direction.
2. A sequence or progression of events.
3. A programme of study.
4. A particular line of action.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Course of action.
2. Course of development.
3. Course correction.

**UPSC example:**

"The government must adopt a calibrated course of action to balance economic growth with environmental sustainability."

**Retention clue:** A river follows a course; similarly, events follow a course.

## **WORD 12. CURSORY**

**Part of speech:** Adjective

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *currere* = to run.
- Latin *cursus* = running.
- The word developed through Latin *cursorius*, associated with running.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

Done while running or moving quickly.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Performed rapidly → done without sufficient attention → superficial or hurried.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Cursory:** Hasty and superficial; performed without paying sufficient attention to detail.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Cursory examination.
2. Cursory review of evidence.
3. Cursory assessment of policy outcomes.

**UPSC example:**

"A cursory assessment of poverty may overlook multidimensional deprivation, including health, education and living standards."

**Retention clue:** Cursory = running through something without examining it deeply.

## **WORD 13. EXCURSION**

**Part of speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- **Ex-** = out.
- **Currere** = to run.
- Latin *excursio* = a running out or going out.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

A running out or moving outward.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Movement outward → going away from the usual path → a short journey or departure from the normal pattern.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Excursion:**

1. A short journey or trip.
2. A deviation from a normal range, course or pattern (especially in technical contexts).

### **E. Common Usage**

1. Educational excursion.
2. Temporary excursion from the norm.
3. Excursion beyond permissible limits.

**UPSC example:**

"Any temporary excursion from fiscal discipline must be justified by exceptional economic circumstances and accompanied by a credible consolidation roadmap."

**Retention clue:** Ex = out; excursion = going out.

## **WORD 14. CURRICULUM**

**Part of speech:** Noun

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

- Latin *currere* = to run.
- Latin *curriculum* = a course, race or running track.
- **-um** = Latin neuter noun ending.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

A course to be run or followed.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

Racecourse → prescribed course → structured course of education and learning.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

**Curriculum:** The complete set of subjects, learning experiences and academic content prescribed for an educational programme.

### **E. Common Usage**

1. School curriculum.
2. Competency-based curriculum.
3. Curriculum reform.

**UPSC example:**

"Curriculum reform should promote critical thinking, constitutional values, scientific temper and experiential learning."

**Retention clue:** A curriculum is the learning path that a student is expected to follow.

# **6. THE MASTER MEMORY FORMULA**

Remember the root through one simple mental image:

# **CURRERE = TO RUN**

- **CON + CURRERE** → Run together → Concur (agree).
- **OB + CURRERE** → Run towards → Occur (happen).
- **RE + CURRERE** → Run again → Recur (repeat).
- **CURRERE → CURRENS** → Running → Current (present/flowing).
- **EX + CURRERE** → Run out → Excursion (a trip).
- **CURRERE → CURSUS** → Running/course → Course, Curriculum.
- **DIS + CURSUS** → Movement in different directions → Discourse (discussion).
- **CURSUS → CURSORIUS** → Running quickly → Cursory (superficial).

**A final UPSC application paragraph using the vocabulary:**

"Several structural factors concur in explaining India's developmental challenges. While economic growth and social inclusion must proceed concurrently, recurring inequalities often occur due to institutional weaknesses and uneven resource distribution. A cursory assessment of these challenges may overlook their historical and structural dimensions. Therefore, public discourse must focus on a comprehensive course of action that integrates inclusive governance, institutional accountability and sustainable development."

This paragraph demonstrates how root-based vocabulary can improve precision, analytical depth and expression in GS answers and Essay writing.

**Editorial note:** The Latin root connections above are historical, not merely visual similarities. In particular, *concur, occur, recur, current, currency, course, curriculum* and *discourse* belong to related etymological pathways. Learning the root helps infer meaning, but does not mean every modern meaning can be mechanically derived from its Latin components.
