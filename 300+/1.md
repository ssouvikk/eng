## 1. CRONY

# Crony



Negative connotation

UPSC GS-II / GS-III / Essay



Pronunciation: /ˈkroʊ.ni/ (KROH-nee)

Part of speech: Noun

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Crony: Derived from Greek chronios, meaning lasting a long time or long-standing.
- Chronos (χρόνος): Greek for time.

The underlying idea is a relationship that has lasted for a long time.

### 2. Literal Meaning

A long-standing companion or old friend.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The word underwent an interesting transformation:

1. Original sense: A long-standing companion or intimate friend.
2. Social sense: A close associate with whom someone shares personal loyalty and familiarity.
3. Political-economic sense: A trusted associate who receives privileges, influence or economic benefits because of personal connections rather than merit or fair competition.

The negative political meaning emerged from the idea that personal loyalty and proximity to power could override institutional rules and public interest.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Crony (noun): A close friend or associate, particularly one who benefits from their relationship with a powerful person.

Cronyism (noun): The practice of giving jobs, favours, contracts or other advantages to friends and associates, especially without considering merit.

Key distinction: A crony is the associate; cronyism is the practice of favouritism.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1\. Crony capitalism: An economic system in which businesses prosper through privileged relationships with political authorities rather than through genuine competition, efficiency and innovation.

2\. Political cronies: Friends and associates of political leaders who enjoy disproportionate access to power, resources or decision-making.

3\. Cronyism in public appointments: The appointment of friends or loyalists to public positions instead of selecting candidates through transparent, merit-based procedures.

UPSC application:

> Crony capitalism undermines competitive neutrality, distorts resource allocation, weakens institutional accountability and erodes public trust in market institutions.

## 3. CHRONIC

# Chronic

Adjective

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ic: Relating to or characterised by.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Related to time or lasting over a prolonged period.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

Originally, the word described something pertaining to time or continuing over a long period.

It gradually acquired the medical sense of a disease or condition that persists over an extended duration, as opposed to an acute condition.

Its modern usage has expanded to describe persistent and deeply entrenched problems in society, politics and governance.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronic: Continuing for a long time, recurring frequently or deeply rooted and difficult to eliminate.

It is particularly useful for describing persistent structural problems.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Chronic unemployment: Persistent unemployment that remains a structural feature of an economy.
2. Chronic poverty: Long-term poverty characterised by persistent deprivation and limited opportunities.
3. Chronic governance deficits: Deep-rooted institutional weaknesses that repeatedly undermine governance outcomes.

UPSC application:

> Chronic unemployment reflects structural deficiencies in skill formation, labour-market absorption and employment generation.

Memory association: Chronic = Continuing through time.

## 4. CHRONICLE

# Chronicle

Noun / Verb

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -icle: Part of the historical word formation; not an independent time-related suffix.

### 2. Literal Meaning

A record of events arranged according to the time in which they occurred.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The word travelled through Greek khronika (historical records), Latin chronica and Old French before entering English.

Initially, it referred to historical accounts recording events in chronological order.

Later, it developed a broader meaning: any systematic account or record of developments over time.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronicle (noun): A factual record or account of events in the order in which they occurred.

Chronicle (verb): To record or document events as they unfold.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. A chronicle of colonial exploitation: A historical account documenting the different dimensions of colonial exploitation.
2. Chronicle of social transformation: A record of the evolution of social institutions and relationships.
3. Chronicle political developments: To systematically document political events and institutional changes.

UPSC application:

> India's constitutional journey is a chronicle of the gradual expansion of democratic participation and social justice.

Memory association: Chronicle = A recorded journey through time.

## 5. CHRONOLOGY

# Chronology

Noun

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -logy (logos): Study, systematic account or branch of knowledge.

### 2. Literal Meaning

The systematic study or arrangement of time and events.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

Initially associated with the study of dates and the ordering of historical events, chronology evolved into the systematic arrangement of events according to their temporal sequence.

It is now used in history, law, archaeology, administration and investigative analysis.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronology: The arrangement of events in the order in which they occurred.

It can also refer to the systematic study of historical dates and time periods.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Chronology of the freedom struggle: Sequential arrangement of major events in India's independence movement.
2. Establish the chronology of events: Determine the exact sequence in which events occurred.
3. Chronological order: Arrangement from earliest to latest or vice versa.

UPSC application:

> A clear chronology of constitutional developments helps explain the evolution of India's parliamentary democracy.

Memory association: Chronology = Time + Systematic arrangement.

## 6. CHRONOLOGICAL

# Chronological

Adjective

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -logy: Order or systematic account in this word family.
- -ical: Relating to or characterised by.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Relating to the arrangement of events according to time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

Derived from chronology, the adjective came to describe anything arranged or presented according to temporal sequence.

It is frequently used in historical writing, legal proceedings and administrative records.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronological: Arranged according to the sequence in which events occurred.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Chronological sequence of events: Events arranged from earliest to latest.
2. Chronological account of history: A historical description organised according to time.
3. Chronological reconstruction: Reconstructing the sequence of events using available evidence.

UPSC application:

> Chronological reconstruction of the events leading to the Revolt of 1857 helps identify its immediate and structural causes.

Memory association: Chronological = Arranged by time.

## 7. CHRONOMETER

# Chronometer

Noun

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -meter (metron): Measure or measuring instrument.

### 2. Literal Meaning

An instrument for measuring time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The term developed with advances in precision timekeeping. It became associated particularly with highly accurate time-measuring devices, including marine chronometers used for determining longitude during navigation.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronometer: A highly accurate timekeeping instrument.

The related adjective chronometric refers to the measurement or precise recording of time.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Marine chronometer: A precision timekeeping instrument historically used in maritime navigation.
2. Precision chronometer: A highly accurate time-measuring device.
3. Chronometric measurement: Measurement based on precise time intervals.

UPSC application:

> The development of accurate marine chronometers transformed navigation by enabling sailors to calculate longitude more reliably.

Memory association: Chronometer = Time + Measuring instrument.

## 8. SYNCHRONOUS

# Synchronous

Adjective

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Syn- (syn): Together, jointly or at the same time.
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ous: Possessing the quality of.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Occurring together in time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The term evolved to describe events, processes or activities occurring simultaneously.

With technological development, it acquired particular importance in communication, computing and institutional functioning.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Synchronous: Occurring or operating at the same time.

In technology, it often refers to real-time interaction or processes coordinated by a common timing mechanism.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Synchronous communication: Communication in which participants interact simultaneously, such as a live meeting.
2. Synchronous elections: Elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies conducted simultaneously or in a coordinated electoral cycle.
3. Synchronous development: Different developmental processes progressing in coordination with one another.

UPSC application:

> The proposal for simultaneous elections seeks to synchronise electoral cycles while raising questions concerning federalism, democratic accountability and constitutional design.

Memory association: Syn + Chronos = Together + Time.

## 9. SYNCHRONIZATION

# Synchronization

Noun

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Syn-: Together.
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ize: To make or cause to become.
- -ation: The process or result of an action.

### 2. Literal Meaning

The process of making events or activities occur together in time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

Originally associated with coordinating events temporally, the term expanded into engineering, information technology, organisational management and public administration.

It now refers both to temporal coordination and to the alignment of multiple processes, systems or activities.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Synchronization: The process of coordinating different activities, systems or events so that they operate in a coordinated temporal relationship.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Electoral synchronization: Coordinating the timing of different elections.
2. Centre-State policy synchronization: Aligning Union and State policy implementation.
3. Synchronization of development programmes: Coordinating multiple government schemes to improve convergence and outcomes.

UPSC application:

> Effective synchronization between fiscal policy, monetary policy and structural reforms is important for achieving macroeconomic stability and sustainable growth.

Memory association: Synchronization = Making different clocks or activities work together.

## 10. ASYNCHRONOUS

# Asynchronous

Adjective

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- A-: Not, without or absence of.
- Syn-: Together.
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ous: Possessing the quality of.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Not occurring together in time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The word developed as the opposite of synchronous.

It initially described events that did not occur simultaneously. Its modern usage is especially prominent in digital communication, education and computing.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Asynchronous: Occurring or operating at different times rather than simultaneously.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Asynchronous communication: Communication in which participants respond at different times, such as email.
2. Asynchronous learning: A learning arrangement in which students access educational materials at different times.
3. Asynchronous processes: Processes that operate independently without requiring simultaneous execution.

UPSC application:

> Asynchronous digital learning can expand educational access for geographically isolated populations, although it requires reliable connectivity and learner autonomy.

Memory association: A + Synchronous = Not happening together.

## 11. ANACHRONISM

# Anachronism

Noun

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Ana-: Backwards, against or out of place (in this formation).
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ism: A condition, practice or phenomenon.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Something placed in the wrong time period.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The term originally described an error in chronological placement, particularly when an event, person, object or custom was assigned to an incorrect historical period.

Its meaning subsequently expanded to include institutions, practices and ideas that appear outdated or incompatible with contemporary circumstances.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Anachronism: Something that belongs to a different historical period, particularly when it is mistakenly placed in the present or in another period.

It can also describe an outdated institution or practice that has failed to adapt to changing circumstances.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Historical anachronism: An error involving the incorrect placement of an event, practice or object in history.
2. Institutional anachronism: An institution or arrangement that has become outdated in contemporary circumstances.
3. Constitutional anachronism: A constitutional provision or institutional arrangement considered outdated in the context of contemporary political and social realities.

UPSC application:

> Administrative structures inherited from colonial rule may become institutional anachronisms when they fail to respond to the requirements of a participatory, citizen-centric democracy.

Memory association: Ana + Chronos = Wrong placement in time.

## 12. ANACHRONISTIC

# Anachronistic

Adjective

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Ana-: Out of place or backwards.
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ism: Condition or phenomenon.
- -ic: Relating to.
- -istic: Characterised by.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Characterised by being out of place in time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

Derived from anachronism, the adjective came to describe things that are historically misplaced or inconsistent with the period in which they exist.

It is also frequently used in political and institutional criticism to describe outdated practices.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Anachronistic: Belonging to an earlier period and therefore appearing outdated, inappropriate or inconsistent with present-day circumstances.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Anachronistic colonial mindset: An outdated administrative outlook inconsistent with democratic and constitutional values.
2. Anachronistic social practices: Traditional practices that conflict with contemporary constitutional principles and social justice.
3. Anachronistic institutional structures: Outdated institutional arrangements that are inadequate for present-day requirements.

UPSC application:

> The persistence of a hierarchical and authoritarian administrative culture is anachronistic in a constitutional democracy founded on liberty, equality and dignity.

Memory association: Anachronism is the noun; anachronistic is the adjective.

## 13. DIACHRONIC

# Diachronic

Adjective

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Dia-: Through, across or throughout.
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ic: Relating to.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Extending through time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The term emerged prominently in linguistics to distinguish the historical study of language from the study of language at a particular point in time.

It subsequently became useful in social sciences, anthropology and historical analysis to describe changes in institutions, cultures and social structures over time.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Diachronic: Analysing a phenomenon through its historical development and transformation over time.

In anthropology, a diachronic approach examines how social institutions, cultural practices and kinship systems change across generations.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Diachronic analysis: Analysis of changes in a phenomenon over time.
2. Diachronic study of social institutions: Examination of how institutions evolve historically.
3. Diachronic perspective in anthropology: Understanding cultural and social changes across different historical periods.

UPSC application:

> A diachronic analysis of the caste system reveals its historical transformations in response to colonial interventions, urbanisation, modernisation and constitutional reforms.

Anthropology connection: Particularly useful when studying cultural change, kinship, caste, tribal transformations and social evolution.

Memory association: Dia + Chronos = Through time.

## 14. SYNCHRONIC

# Synchronic

Adjective

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Syn-: Together or at the same time.
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ic: Relating to.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Relating to phenomena existing at the same time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The term became prominent in linguistics through the distinction between synchronic and diachronic analysis.

A synchronic approach studies a system at a particular point in time without necessarily investigating its historical evolution.

This distinction was subsequently adopted in anthropology and other social sciences.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Synchronic: Examining a phenomenon at a particular point in time rather than tracing its historical development.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Synchronic analysis: Examination of a phenomenon at a particular point in time.
2. Synchronic study of kinship: Examination of the structure and functioning of kinship relationships within a society at a given time.
3. Synchronic perspective in anthropology: Understanding the interrelationships between cultural institutions within a society at a particular period.

UPSC application:

> A synchronic study of a tribal community examines the interrelationships among its kinship system, economy, religion and political organisation at a particular point in time.

Anthropology connection: Particularly useful for understanding structural-functional approaches, social structure and cultural integration.

Memory association: Syn + Chronos = Together at one point in time.

## 15. SYNCHRONY

# Synchrony

Noun

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Syn-: Together.
- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -y: Forms a noun expressing a state or condition.

### 2. Literal Meaning

The state of occurring together in time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The term developed to describe the simultaneous occurrence or coordinated functioning of different events and processes.

In linguistics, it refers to the study of a language as a system at a particular point in time.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Synchrony: The state or condition of events, activities or systems occurring simultaneously or functioning in temporal coordination.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Social synchrony: Coordinated patterns of behaviour or interaction among individuals within a society.
2. Institutional synchrony: Coordinated functioning of different institutions.
3. Synchrony in social systems: The simultaneous functioning and interdependence of different social components.

UPSC application:

> Effective governance requires synchrony between policy formulation, institutional capacity and ground-level implementation.

Memory association: Synchrony = The state; synchronization = The process.

## 16. CHRONICALLY

Adverb

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ic: Relating to.
- -ally: In a particular manner or condition.

### 2. Literal Meaning

In a manner that persists over time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

Developed from the adjective chronic to describe conditions that persist over an extended period or repeatedly occur.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronically: Persistently, continuously or repeatedly over a long period.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Chronically underfunded institutions: Institutions that consistently receive inadequate financial resources.
2. Chronically vulnerable populations: Groups experiencing persistent exposure to social, economic or environmental risks.
3. Chronically weak implementation: Long-standing deficiencies in the execution of policies and programmes.

# Chronicity

Noun

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- -ic: Relating to.
- -ity: State, quality or condition.

### 2. Literal Meaning

The state or quality of persisting over time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

Originally associated with the duration of medical conditions, chronicity acquired wider application in describing persistent social, economic and institutional problems.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronicity: The condition or characteristic of being prolonged, persistent or long-lasting.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

## 18. CHRONOBIOLOGY

# Chronobiology

Noun • Scientific terminology

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Chron / Chronos: Time.
- Bio (bios): Life.
- -logy (logos): Study.

### 2. Literal Meaning

The study of biological processes in relation to time.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The term developed as a scientific discipline examining biological rhythms and their relationship with time, environmental cycles and internal biological clocks.

### 4. Modern Meaning

Chronobiology: The scientific study of biological rhythms and periodic processes in living organisms.

It includes the study of circadian rhythms, seasonal biological patterns and sleep-wake cycles.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Circadian rhythms: Biological processes that follow an approximately 24-hour cycle.
2. Biological clock: The internal mechanism regulating periodic biological activities.
3. Chronobiological adaptation: Adjustment of biological rhythms to changing environmental or temporal conditions.

UPSC application:

> Chronobiology helps explain the relationship between environmental conditions, human health, sleep patterns and physiological adaptation.

Memory association: Chronos + Biology = The study of life's relationship with time.

## 19. CRONYISM

# Cronyism

High-value UPSC vocabulary

### 1. Meaning of the Components

- Crony: A close or long-standing friend.
- -ism: A practice, system, doctrine or characteristic behaviour.

### 2. Literal Meaning

The practice or system of favouring one's friends.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The word cronyism appeared around 1840, initially referring to friendship.

By the mid-twentieth century, particularly in American English, it acquired the specific political meaning of appointing friends and associates to influential positions irrespective of their competence.

Its contemporary usage extends to preferential treatment in public appointments, government contracts, regulatory decisions and allocation of economic resources.&#x20;

### 4. Modern Meaning

Cronyism: The practice of favouring friends, relatives or personal associates, particularly in appointments and the distribution of public resources, instead of following objective and merit-based criteria.

### 5. Common Usage — UPSC-Relevant Collocations

1. Political cronyism: Preferential treatment given to political associates by those exercising political power.
2. Administrative cronyism: Favouritism in public appointments, transfers, promotions and administrative decisions.
3. Institutionalised cronyism: A situation in which favouritism becomes embedded in institutional practices and decision-making structures.

UPSC application:

> Institutionalised cronyism undermines meritocracy, weakens administrative neutrality, distorts public resource allocation and erodes citizens' trust in democratic institutions.

## 22. Particularly important: Diachronic vs Synchronic

## Two approaches to studying society

DIACHRONIC APPROACH

Study society across time

Historical development → Transformation → Contemporary condition

Past

Present

Change

Example: How has the caste system transformed from the colonial period to contemporary India?

SYNCHRONIC APPROACH

Study society at a particular point in time

Society at a given time

Kinship

Economy

Religion

Politics

Social structure

Culture

Example: How do kinship, economy, religion and political organisation interact within a tribal community today?

Anthropological significance:

- Diachronic analysis helps understand cultural evolution, historical transformations and processes of social change.
- Synchronic analysis helps understand social structure, functional relationships and cultural integration.

Answer-writing advantage: Use both approaches for a more comprehensive analysis of social institutions. A synchronic account explains how a system functions; a diachronic account explains how it has evolved.

## 1. STRIP — The Root Word

Word: Strip\
Pronunciation: /strɪp/\
Part of Speech: Verb (primarily); Noun\
Core Idea: To remove something, especially a covering, possession, privilege, protection or essential component.

### A. Meaning of the Components

The word strip is an Old English/Germanic-origin word. Unlike many English words derived from Latin or Greek, it does not have two independently meaningful components in its modern form.

Its historical development is associated with the idea of stripping, plundering or removing something.

Memory association: Imagine removing the outer covering of an object, layer by layer, until nothing is left.

### B. Literal Meaning

To remove something from someone or something, particularly something that covers, protects or belongs to it.

Examples:

- To strip a tree of its bark.
- To strip a person of their clothes.
- To strip an institution of its powers.

### C. Semantic Evolution

## 2. THE ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY OF STUMBLE

### First, understand the root connection

The word stumble has a relatively small direct derivational family. It would be misleading to manufacture a large family by grouping unrelated words that merely express mistakes or difficulties.

Its probable historical connection is with Old Norse stumla (to stumble or grope), and a Germanic root associated with checking or impeding movement. It is also historically associated with the Germanic family behind stammer.

This gives us two useful learning clusters:

1. Direct family: stumble, stumbled, stumbling, stumbler, stumblingly, stumbling block.
2. Historical cognate / extended connection: stammer and its derivatives.

The second cluster is useful for vocabulary building, but should not be mistaken for a set of words all directly derived from stumble.&#x20;

## 1. INVARIABLY



UPSC Vocabulary

Adverb

Formal English



Pronunciation: /ɪnˈveəriəbli/\
Part of Speech: Adverb\
Root: Latin varius = diverse, changing, different\
Word Formation: In- + variable + -ly

### 1. Meaning of the Components

| Component | Meaning                            |
| --------- | ---------------------------------- |
| in-       | Not (negative prefix in this word) |
| vari      | Change, difference, diversity      |
| -able     | Capable of being                   |
| -ly       | In a particular manner             |

Important: The prefix in- is negative here. It does not mean in/inside.

### 2. Literal Meaning

Invariable = Not capable of changing; remaining constant.

Invariably = In an unchanging or constant manner.

### 3. Semantic Evolution

The Latin adjective varius meant diverse, different or changeable.

It developed into Latin variabilis (changeable), which entered English through French as variable.

Adding the negative prefix in- produced invariable, meaning unchanging or constant.

Finally, adding -ly produced invariably, meaning something happens consistently, without exception or almost every time.

Evolution:

VARIUS (Latin)

Diverse / changing

VARIABLE

Capable of changing

INVARIABLE

Not changing / constant

INVARIABLY

Consistently / without exception

### 4. Modern Meaning

Invariably: Always, consistently or on every occasion; without exception.

It is frequently used to describe a recurring pattern, predictable consequence or consistent institutional behaviour.

Nuance: Invariably is stronger than frequently or usually. It suggests an almost universal regularity, although it can sometimes be used rhetorically rather than literally.

Examples:

1. Economic inequality invariably undermines social cohesion when left unaddressed.
2. Weak institutional accountability invariably creates opportunities for corruption.
3. Development policies invariably produce better outcomes when supported by effective implementation mechanisms.

### 5. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations

| Collocation                | Meaning                     |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Invariably leads to        | Consistently results in     |
| Invariably associated with | Consistently connected with |
| Invariably results in      | Almost always produces      |

UPSC Answer-Writing Application:

- "The absence of institutional accountability invariably leads to governance deficits."
- "Inclusive development is invariably associated with social stability."
- "Unplanned urbanisation invariably results in pressure on civic infrastructure."

## 6. The Most Important Takeaway for UPSC

Remember the distinction between three conceptual dimensions:

1. Variation → Differences between entities or across time and space.
2. Variability → The extent or degree of those differences.
3. Diversity → The existence or presence of multiple forms.

And remember:

INVARIABLE = Unchanging

INVARIABLY = Consistently

VARIABLE = Changeable

VARIABILITY = Degree of change

DIVERSIFICATION = Process of increasing variety

These distinctions are especially useful in Anthropology (human variation and cultural diversity), GS-I (Indian society), GS-III (climate variability and economic diversification), and GS-II (regional variations in governance outcomes).
