# **SWELLING — Etymological Analysis and Word Family**

VOCABULARY BUILDING • ROOT-BASED LEARNING • UPSC PREPARATION

The objective is to understand not merely what *swelling* means, but how its original physical meaning evolved into expressions used in governance, economics, sociology, political science, geography and essay writing.

The central idea connecting this word family is:

**INCREASE → EXPANSION → RISE → SURGE → EXCESSIVE GROWTH**

# 1. SWELLING

## Meaning of the Components

**Root word:** SWELL\
**Part of speech:** Noun (also the present participle of the verb *swell*)\
**Etymology:** Old English *swellan* — to grow, become enlarged or increase in size.\
**Proto-Germanic:** *swellaną* — to swell or expand.\
**Proto-Indo-European root:** Often reconstructed as *swel-* — to swell or become enlarged.

The word does not have two independent components. It is formed from the verb **swell + -ing**, where *-ing* indicates an ongoing action or its resulting condition.

- **Swell:** To increase in size, volume, intensity or number.
- **-ing:** Indicates an ongoing process, activity or resulting state.

## Literal Meaning

The physical enlargement of something beyond its normal size or volume.

For example:

- Swelling of a body part due to inflammation.
- Swelling of a river after heavy rainfall.
- Swelling of a material due to heat or moisture.

## Semantic Evolution

The original idea of physical enlargement gradually acquired several figurative meanings:

1. **Physical enlargement:** An increase in the size or volume of something.
2. **Increase in quantity:** A rise in population, expenditure, debt or demand.
3. **Increase in intensity:** An intensification of emotions, tensions or public anger.
4. **Expansion of collective sentiment:** A growing movement, public opinion or popular resistance.

The underlying idea remains the same: something becomes progressively larger, stronger or more extensive.

## Modern Meaning

**Swelling (noun):**

1. An abnormal enlargement of a body part, generally caused by injury, inflammation or fluid accumulation.
2. A gradual increase in the size, volume, quantity or intensity of something.
3. A growing accumulation of public sentiment, economic pressure or social tension.

**In UPSC terminology:** Swelling can describe an increase in economic indicators, social tensions, public expenditure, population pressure or environmental phenomena.

## Common Usage

1. **Swelling of public expenditure** — A sustained increase in government spending.
2. **Swelling social unrest** — A growing intensity of social dissatisfaction and protest.
3. **Swelling population pressure** — Increasing pressure on resources and public infrastructure due to population growth.

# 2. ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY OF SWELL

The family is best understood through five semantic branches:

- Physical enlargement
- Increase in quantity
- Intensification of emotions
- Rising public opinion and collective movements
- Expansion in natural phenomena

## A. SWELL

**Meaning of the Components**

- Root: Old English *swellan* — to grow or become enlarged.
- It is the original verb from which *swelling* and *swollen* are derived.

**Literal Meaning**

To increase in size, volume or physical dimensions.

**Semantic Evolution**

The physical idea of becoming larger gradually expanded to include increases in quantity, intensity, importance and emotional expression.

It also developed the meaning of a wave rising and becoming larger.

**Modern Meaning**

To increase or expand in size, number, volume, intensity or strength.

As a noun, it can refer to a gradual rise, a large wave or a growing movement.

**Common Usage**

1. **Swell in numbers** — To increase in number.
2. **Swell with pride** — To experience an intense feeling of pride.
3. **A swell of public opinion** — A growing wave of public sentiment.

## B. SWOLLEN

**Meaning of the Components**

- Root: *Swell* — to enlarge or expand.
- *-en*: Historical past participle formation.

**Literal Meaning**

Something that has become enlarged or expanded.

**Semantic Evolution**

Originally associated with physical enlargement, the word gradually acquired figurative meanings relating to excessive accumulation, inflated importance and exaggerated emotions.

**Modern Meaning**

1. Physically enlarged, particularly due to injury or inflammation.
2. Excessively increased in size, volume or quantity.
3. Figuratively, excessively inflated with pride, arrogance or self-importance.

**Common Usage**

1. **Swollen river** — A river whose water level has risen significantly.
2. **Swollen public debt** — Public debt that has increased substantially.
3. **Swollen-headed individual** — A person excessively proud or arrogant.

## C. SWELLING

**Meaning of the Components**

- *Swell* — to enlarge or increase.
- *-ing* — An ongoing action or resulting condition.

**Literal Meaning**

The process or condition of becoming enlarged.

**Semantic Evolution**

Its meaning expanded from physical enlargement to the progressive increase of abstract quantities and social phenomena.

**Modern Meaning**

An increase in physical size, volume, number or intensity.

**Common Usage**

1. **Swelling expenditure** — Increasing expenditure.
2. **Swelling discontent** — Intensifying public dissatisfaction.
3. **Swelling demand** — Increasing demand for goods or services.

## D. GROUNDSWELL

**Meaning of the Components**

- **Ground:** The land, or metaphorically, the underlying population or social base.
- **Swell:** A rising wave or gradual increase.

**Literal Meaning**

Originally, a broad, deep ocean swell generated by distant weather systems.

**Semantic Evolution**

The image of a large wave gradually rising and moving forward became a metaphor for a powerful movement developing beneath the surface.

It came to represent an emerging collective sentiment that may not initially be visible but eventually becomes powerful enough to influence events.

**Modern Meaning**

A widespread and growing movement of public opinion, sentiment or political support.

It is especially useful in analysing democratic movements, political transformations and social change.

**Common Usage**

1. **Groundswell of public opinion** — A rapidly growing collective public sentiment.
2. **Groundswell of popular support** — Increasing support among ordinary citizens.
3. **Groundswell of protest** — A growing wave of public resistance.

**UPSC Application:** The anti-corruption movement, environmental movements and demands for democratic accountability can be analysed through the concept of a groundswell of public opinion.

## E. SWELL UP

**Meaning of the Components**

- **Swell:** To increase or enlarge.
- **Up:** Indicates upward movement or intensification.

**Literal Meaning**

To increase in size or rise upwards.

**Semantic Evolution**

The expression moved from physical enlargement to emotional and social intensification.

**Modern Meaning**

To become larger, more intense or emotionally charged.

**Common Usage**

1. **Emotions swell up** — Emotions become increasingly intense.
2. **Anger swelled up** — Anger gradually intensified.
3. **A river swelled up** — The river increased in volume.

## F. SWELLING TIDE

**Meaning of the Components**

- **Swelling:** Increasing in size, volume or intensity.
- **Tide:** The periodic rise and fall of sea levels.

**Literal Meaning**

A rising or increasingly powerful tide.

**Semantic Evolution**

The image of an expanding ocean tide became a metaphor for a powerful and steadily increasing phenomenon.

**Modern Meaning**

A growing and potentially overwhelming movement, tendency or social force.

**Common Usage**

1. **Swelling tide of nationalism** — Increasing nationalist sentiment.
2. **Swelling tide of migration** — A substantial increase in migration.
3. **Swelling tide of populism** — Increasing influence of populist politics.

# 3. IMPORTANT DERIVATIONAL AND COMPOUND EXPRESSIONS

These expressions retain the original idea of expansion or rising, although some are less frequent than the core family members.

**Meaning of the Components**

- Swelling: Increasing.
- Pressure: A force or demand exerted on something.

**Literal Meaning**

Pressure that is progressively increasing.

**Semantic Evolution**

The physical idea of increasing force evolved into a description of growing economic, political and social constraints.

**Modern Meaning**

An increasingly intense burden or demand on an individual, institution, government or system.

**Common Usage**

1. **Swelling fiscal pressure** — Increasing pressure on government finances.
2. **Swelling urban pressure** — Increasing demands on urban infrastructure.
3. **Swelling resource pressure** — Increasing demand on limited natural resources.

## B. SWELL OF MIGRATION

**Meaning of the Components**

- Swell: A rising wave or increase.
- Migration: Movement of people from one place to another.

**Literal Meaning**

A rising wave of migration.

**Semantic Evolution**

The wave metaphor is used to describe a sudden or sustained increase in the number of migrants.

**Modern Meaning**

A substantial increase in migration flows.

**Common Usage**

1. **A swell of rural-to-urban migration.**
2. **A swell of cross-border migration.**
3. **A swell of climate-induced migration.**

## C. SWELL OF NATIONALISM

**Meaning of the Components**

- Swell: A growing wave or increase.
- Nationalism: Identification with and attachment to one's nation, sometimes accompanied by demands for national sovereignty.

**Literal Meaning**

A rising wave of nationalist sentiment.

**Semantic Evolution**

The physical image of an expanding wave became a metaphor for the increasing intensity and spread of national consciousness.

**Modern Meaning**

A significant rise in nationalist sentiment or political mobilisation.

**Common Usage**

1. **A swell of nationalistic sentiment.**
2. **A swell of anti-colonial nationalism.**
3. **A swell of subnational identity politics.**

## D. SWELL OF DISCONTENT

**Meaning of the Components**

- Swell: To grow or increase.
- Discontent: Dissatisfaction with an existing condition.

**Literal Meaning**

An increasing wave of dissatisfaction.

**Semantic Evolution**

The physical idea of a rising wave became associated with growing dissatisfaction among individuals or groups.

**Modern Meaning**

An intensifying feeling of dissatisfaction that may eventually result in protests, movements or political instability.

**Common Usage**

1. **A swell of public discontent.**
2. **A swell of popular resentment.**
3. **A swell of anti-establishment sentiment.**

## E. SWELLING WAVE

**Meaning of the Components**

- Swelling: Increasing in size.
- Wave: A moving disturbance or surge.

**Literal Meaning**

A wave that is increasing in size or height.

**Semantic Evolution**

The expression became a useful metaphor for the gradual expansion of a phenomenon, particularly in social and political contexts.

**Modern Meaning**

An increasingly powerful or extensive movement, trend or phenomenon.

**Common Usage**

1. **A swelling wave of protests.**
2. **A swelling wave of public mobilisation.**
3. **A swelling wave of environmental activism.**

## **6. Important etymological distinctions**

For accurate vocabulary building, keep these distinctions in mind:

| Word          | Relationship with SWELL                |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Swell         | Original root verb                     |
| Swelling      | Direct derivative                      |
| Swollen       | Past participle of swell               |
| Groundswell   | Compound containing swell              |
| Swell up      | Phrasal verb                           |
| Swelling tide | Metaphorical expression                |
| Swelter       | Not an established derivative of swell |
| Sweltering    | Not a member of this word family       |

**A note on etymology:** The historical relationship between *swell* and its Germanic cognates is well established. The deeper Proto-Indo-European reconstruction is less certain than the Old English derivation, so it should not be treated as an indisputable root decomposition.

**My recommendation for UPSC retention:** Prioritise *groundswell*, *swelling*, *swollen*, and *swell* rather than memorising every possible compound. These offer the greatest practical value in Essay, GS-I (Society), GS-II (Polity and Governance), GS-III (Economy and Environment), and newspaper editorials.



# **WORD POWER MADE EASY: CRIPPLED**

ROOT-BASED VOCABULARY • ETYMOLOGY • UPSC APPLICATION

# **CRIPPLED**



Adjective / Verb (past tense & past participle)

GS II • GS III • Essay • Ethics



**Central idea:** Something that has lost its normal capacity to function, move, develop or operate effectively.

The word *crippled* is particularly useful in UPSC answer writing because it can describe not only physical disability but also institutional weakness, economic paralysis, administrative incapacity and structural constraints.

## **1. Meaning of the Components**

**Etymological origin:** Middle English *cripel* / *crepel*, associated with Old English *crypel*, meaning a person who cannot walk normally or who moves by creeping.

Unlike words such as *benevolent* or *malnutrition*, **crippled cannot be reliably divided into independent Latin or Greek prefixes and roots**.

Its historical development is more important than artificial word segmentation.

- **Cripple:** A person with a physical impairment affecting mobility (historical usage).
- **-ed:** Suffix indicating a resulting state or condition.

Thus:

**Cripple + -ed → Crippled**

The suffix *-ed* indicates that something has been rendered incapable or impaired.

*Important sensitivity note:* When referring to people, *crippled* is now generally considered outdated and potentially offensive. Prefer *person with a disability* or a more specific description. Its metaphorical use for institutions and systems remains common, although alternatives such as *weakened*, *hampered* and *paralysed* may be more precise.

## **2. Literal Meaning**

**Crippled = rendered physically impaired or deprived of normal functioning capacity.**

Originally, the word was associated with impaired bodily movement, especially difficulty walking.

The underlying image is that of a person whose ability to move freely has been restricted.

## **3. Semantic Evolution**

The word underwent an important metaphorical expansion.

1. **Physical impairment**

   Originally associated with bodily impairment and difficulty walking.
2. **Loss of functional capacity**

   Expanded to describe anything that could no longer perform its normal function.
3. **Institutional and economic weakness**

   Began describing organisations, economies and governments unable to function effectively.
4. **Severe restriction or paralysis**

   Acquired a broader figurative meaning: severely weakened, obstructed or rendered ineffective.

**Evolutionary formula:**

Physical impairment → Functional impairment → Institutional incapacity → Systemic paralysis.

## **4. Modern Meaning**

**Crippled (adjective):**

1. Severely damaged or weakened.
2. Deprived of the capacity to function effectively.
3. Severely restricted in development, operation or performance.

**Cripple (verb):**

To severely damage, weaken or impair the functioning or effectiveness of something.

Examples:

- Corruption has crippled public service delivery.
- Excessive fiscal deficits can cripple economic growth.
- Institutional distrust can cripple democratic governance.
- Poor infrastructure cripples industrial competitiveness.

## **5. Common Usage: UPSC-Relevant Collocations**

| Collocation                  | Meaning                                                             |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Crippled economy             | An economy severely weakened by a crisis                            |
| Crippled governance          | Governance rendered ineffective by institutional weaknesses         |
| Crippled public institutions | Institutions unable to discharge their responsibilities effectively |

**UPSC-ready sentence:**

"Persistent institutional inefficiencies, regulatory uncertainty and inadequate infrastructure have crippled India's manufacturing competitiveness, thereby constraining employment generation and structural transformation."

# **PART II — THE ETYMOLOGICAL WORD FAMILY**

Before proceeding, an important distinction:

The words below are organised into two groups:

1. **Historical and morphological relatives:** Words directly connected to *cripple*.
2. **Semantic and conceptual relatives:** Words from other roots that express similar ideas, such as impairment, obstruction, weakness and paralysis.

This distinction matters because similar meanings do not necessarily indicate a common etymological root.

## **A. Direct Word Family of CRIPPLE**

The direct family is relatively small. English does not have a large productive vocabulary derived from this particular root.

### **1. Cripple**

Core word

**Meaning of the Components**

Historical English *cripel/crepel*, associated with impaired movement.

**Literal Meaning**

A person who has difficulty walking or moving normally.

**Semantic Evolution**

The noun originally described physical impairment. It subsequently acquired a figurative association with anything that severely restricts functioning.

**Modern Meaning**

- Noun: A historically used term for a person with a mobility impairment; now generally inappropriate for referring to people.
- Verb: To severely damage or weaken something.

**Common Usage**

1. Cripple economic growth.
2. Cripple institutional functioning.
3. Cripple public service delivery.

### **2. Crippling**

**Meaning of the Components**

Cripple + -ing.

**Literal Meaning**

The act or process of causing severe impairment.

**Semantic Evolution**

From causing physical impairment to imposing damage or restrictions so severe that normal functioning becomes difficult.

**Modern Meaning**

An adjective describing something extremely damaging, burdensome or debilitating; also the present participle of *cripple*.

**Common Usage**

1. Crippling debt burden.
2. Crippling inflation.
3. Crippling economic sanctions.

**UPSC application:**

"Crippling debt servicing obligations reduce fiscal space for developmental expenditure."

### **3. Cripplingly**

**Meaning of the Components**

Cripple + -ing + -ly.

**Literal Meaning**

In a manner that severely impairs functioning.

**Semantic Evolution**

The adjective *crippling* developed an adverbial form to describe the intensity of a burden or disadvantage.

**Modern Meaning**

To an extremely damaging or disabling degree.

**Common Usage**

1. Cripplingly high public debt.
2. Cripplingly expensive healthcare.
3. Cripplingly high borrowing costs.

# **B. SEMANTICALLY RELATED VOCABULARY**

These words are particularly valuable for UPSC because they allow you to express different dimensions of incapacity with greater precision.

## **ROOT CLUSTER 1: HAMPER — Obstruction and Impediment**

**Central idea:** To obstruct progress or prevent something from functioning smoothly.

### **4. Hamper**

High-frequency

**Meaning of the Components**

From Middle English *hamperen*, of uncertain origin. It does not have a reliably established prefix-root decomposition.

**Literal Meaning**

To obstruct or entangle.

**Semantic Evolution**

From physically entangling or obstructing movement to restricting progress, development or performance.

**Modern Meaning**

To hinder, obstruct or make an activity difficult.

**Common Usage**

1. Hamper economic development.
2. Hamper administrative efficiency.
3. Hamper the implementation of welfare schemes.

**UPSC distinction:** Hampering something does not necessarily destroy its functioning; it makes functioning more difficult.

### **5. Hindrance**

**Meaning of the Components**

Hinder + -ance.

**Literal Meaning**

The condition or result of holding something back.

**Semantic Evolution**

From physically preventing movement to any obstacle that obstructs progress.

**Modern Meaning**

An obstacle or circumstance that delays or prevents progress.

**Common Usage**

1. Major hindrance to development.
2. Institutional hindrances.
3. Hindrance to ease of doing business.

### **6. Impediment**

**Meaning of the Components**

Latin *impedire*:

- *In-* = in, into.
- *Pes/pedis* = foot.

The original image is of something that entangles the feet.

**Literal Meaning**

Something that catches or obstructs the feet.

**Semantic Evolution**

From physical entanglement to any obstacle that prevents smooth movement or progress.

**Modern Meaning**

A barrier, obstacle or difficulty that obstructs an activity.

**Common Usage**

1. Structural impediments to growth.
2. Institutional impediments to governance.
3. Legal impediments to reform.

**Memory hook:** An impediment metaphorically catches your feet and prevents forward movement.

### **7. Encumber**

**Meaning of the Components**

From Old French *encombrer*, meaning to obstruct or block.

No reliable independent prefix-root decomposition is necessary here.

**Literal Meaning**

To burden or obstruct.

**Semantic Evolution**

From physically blocking a passage to burdening someone or something with responsibilities, debts or restrictions.

**Modern Meaning**

To burden, restrict or obstruct movement and functioning.

**Common Usage**

1. Encumbered by excessive regulations.
2. Encumbered with public debt.
3. Encumbered by procedural requirements.

### **8. Obstruct**

**Meaning of the Components**

Latin:

- *Ob-* = against, in the way.
- *Struere* = to build or pile up.

**Literal Meaning**

To build something in the way.

**Semantic Evolution**

From physically blocking a passage to preventing the progress of an activity or process.

**Modern Meaning**

To block, impede or deliberately prevent something from proceeding.

**Common Usage**

1. Obstruct justice.
2. Obstruct parliamentary proceedings.
3. Obstruct administrative reforms.

## **ROOT CLUSTER 3: PARALYSE — Complete Functional Incapacity**

**Central idea:** To render something incapable of normal movement or functioning.

### **14. Paralyse / Paralyze**

High-frequency

**Meaning of the Components**

Greek:

- *Para-* = beside, abnormal.
- *Lyein* = to loosen or release.

Through Greek *paralyein* and later medical Latin, the term acquired its association with loss of muscular function.

**Literal Meaning**

To loosen or impair normal bodily functioning.

**Semantic Evolution**

From loss of bodily movement to the complete disruption of institutions, markets, governments and administrative systems.

**Modern Meaning**

To render someone or something incapable of normal movement or functioning.

**Common Usage**

1. Paralyse economic activity.
2. Paralyse administrative functioning.
3. Policy paralysis.

**UPSC application:**

"Policy paralysis, regulatory uncertainty and institutional indecision can undermine investor confidence and delay capital formation."

### **15. Paralysis**

**Meaning of the Components**

Greek *paralysis*, associated with *paralyein*.

- *Para-* = beside, abnormal.
- *Lyein* = to loosen.

**Literal Meaning**

A condition involving loss of normal muscular function.

**Semantic Evolution**

From a medical condition to a metaphor for complete inability to act or make decisions.

**Modern Meaning**

The inability to move, act or function effectively.

**Common Usage**

1. Policy paralysis.
2. Administrative paralysis.
3. Economic paralysis.

**Distinction:**

- Paralyse = the act of rendering ineffective.
- Paralysis = the resulting condition.

### **16. Paralytic**

**Meaning of the Components**

Paralysis + -tic.

The suffix *-tic* forms an adjective relating to a condition.

**Literal Meaning**

Related to paralysis.

**Semantic Evolution**

From medical description to a figurative description of severe incapacity.

**Modern Meaning**

Medically, relating to paralysis; figuratively, severely incapacitating.

**Common Usage**

1. Paralytic condition.
2. Paralytic effects of a crisis.
3. Paralytic impact on economic activity.

*Note:* The figurative adjective is comparatively uncommon. Prefer *paralysing* or *paralysed* in most UPSC answers.

## **ROOT CLUSTER 5: UNDERMINE — Erosion of Strength and Stability**

**Central idea:** To weaken something gradually, often from within.

### **22. Undermine**

**Meaning of the Components**

- *Under* = beneath.

## **1. THERMOCLINE**

**Pronunciation:** THUR-muh-kline\
**Part of Speech:** Noun\
**Domain:** Physical Geography | Oceanography | Climatology | Environmental Science

### **A. Meaning of the Components**

The word *thermocline* is formed by combining two Greek-derived elements:

| Component | Origin                    | Meaning                   |
| --------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Thermo-   | Greek *thermē* (θερμή)    | Heat, warmth, temperature |
| -cline    | Greek *klīnein* (κλίνειν) | To lean, incline or slope |

**Important distinction:** The suffix *-cline* conveys the idea of an inclination or gradient. It is related to the Greek verb meaning 'to lean'.

### **B. Literal Meaning**

**Thermocline = A temperature gradient or zone of temperature inclination.**

In simple terms, it refers to a region in a water body where temperature changes rapidly with increasing depth.

### **C. Semantic Evolution**

The evolution of the word can be understood through three stages:

1. **Thermo (heat):** The concept of heat or temperature.
2. **Cline (inclination):** A gradual change or gradient in a physical property.
3. **Scientific application:** Oceanographers adopted the term to describe the layer in which water temperature decreases rapidly with depth.

Thus, the word moved from the general idea of a temperature gradient to a specific oceanographic concept.

### **D. Modern Meaning**

A **thermocline** is a distinct layer in a lake or ocean where water temperature decreases rapidly with increasing depth, separating relatively warm surface water from colder deep water.

It generally occurs between:

- **Epipelagic zone:** Relatively warm, sunlit surface waters.
- **Deep oceanic waters:** Colder waters with comparatively stable temperatures.

Traditional racial classifications attempted to categorise human populations into discrete biological groups. Modern population genetics, however, demonstrates that human biological variation is largely continuous and overlapping.

# **5. High-Value Etymological Comparisons**

An important aspect of Norman Lewis-style vocabulary learning is understanding how apparently similar words differ in meaning.

### **A. HALO- versus SAL-**

Both can refer to salt, but they have different linguistic origins.

| Word  | Root               | Meaning |
| ----- | ------------------ | ------- |
| Halo- | Greek *hals/halos* | Salt    |
| Sal-  | Latin *sal*        | Salt    |

Words derived from the Latin root *sal* include:

- **Saline:** Containing salt.
- **Salinity:** Concentration of dissolved salts.
- **Salination:** The process of increasing salt concentration.
- **Desalination:** Removal of dissolved salts from water.
- **Desalinisation:** British spelling of desalination.
- **Salinisation:** Development or increase of soil salinity.

Note: These are semantic relatives of the HALO- family, not words derived from the same Greek root.

### **B. HALOPHILE versus HALOPHYTE versus HALOTOLERANT**

| Word          | Key idea                   | Memory association |
| ------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Halophile     | Salt-loving organism       | Loves salt         |
| Halophyte     | Salt-adapted plant         | Plant              |
| Halotolerant  | Can withstand salt         | Endures salt       |
| Halophilic    | Having affinity for salt   | Characteristic     |
| Halotolerance | Capacity to withstand salt | Ability            |

### **C. HALOGEN versus HALOPHILE**

This distinction is particularly important.

| Halogen                    | Halophile                         |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Salt-producing             | Salt-loving                       |
| Chemical element           | Organism                          |
| Group 17                   | Biological/ecological terminology |
| Chlorine, fluorine, iodine | Halophilic archaea and bacteria   |

Do not assume that all words beginning with *halo-* have the same scientific meaning. The second component determines the specific meaning.
